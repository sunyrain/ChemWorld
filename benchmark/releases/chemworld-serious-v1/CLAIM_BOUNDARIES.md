# Claim boundaries

ChemWorld v1 supports a bounded claim about the intersection of:

1. a chemistry-native, stateful, partially observable material/process runtime;
2. agent-selected experimental operations, characterization, termination, and final assay;
3. campaign-wide physical and provider resource accounting;
4. paired prior-information conditions under matched physical identity;
5. immutable traces, exact replay, and fixed-world fresh-trajectory replication.

The release does not claim to be the first interactive science environment, the first virtual chemistry laboratory, the first hidden-law benchmark, or the first process-level scientific-agent evaluation. It does not replace robotic laboratories and does not test hardware control, visual perception, instrument integration, sim-to-real transfer, or real-world safety.

Current scientific limitations remain material: formal agent results cover two of 15 registered tasks; G2 v0.5 uses one task, two selected worlds, two information arms, and one model configuration; provider sampling is not seed-controlled; instrument outputs and physical transitions are synthetic; and trajectory constructs are operational endpoints rather than validated cognitive ground truth.

The terminal G2 v0.5 result supports a descriptive within-selected-world claim:
best-score and raw-terminal contrasts were sign-discordant in two of eight
complete fresh-trajectory pairs, and each pair yielded a continuous profile of
discovery, retention, drawdown and relative retention. The frozen six-of-eight
mixed classification is a supporting sensitivity summary. The release does not
identify provider variation as a causal source or estimate a population
frequency across chemical worlds.
