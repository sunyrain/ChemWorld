**Autonomous science promises to translate experimental agency into advances in scientific understanding [@tom2024selfdriving; @boiko2023autonomous]. Whether and how self-directed experimentation realizes this promise requires examining the research process itself. Here we use ChemWorld, a set of programmable chemical worlds, to connect experimental choices, observations, contemporaneous judgments and subsequent predictions. Broad comparisons across chemical systems motivate repeated equilibrium and crystallization studies, with 90 current process sessions and a separate 30-session comparison of research opportunity. Initial information is associated with distinct experimental starting points; ensuing observations support prospective tests, local relationships and explicit revisions. Yet the consequences of research depend on how these relationships are subsequently used. In the matched research-opportunity comparison, access to research lowers average prediction error by 73% overall while increasing it by 69% at extreme dilution. Crystallization reveals a complementary divergence: forecasts improve on a source-observation mean for recovery in 39 of 42 sessions, but in none for purity. Continuous records locate transitions between supported local judgments, further tests and extrapolations that remain unresolved by the acquired evidence. These findings show how scientific progress can differentiate within a research history. Experimental achievement, predictive generalization and evidential support describe distinct outcomes of autonomous inquiry.**

An autonomous researcher does more than act on the world. By choosing experiments and measurements, it also shapes the experience from which its scientific judgments form: which phenomena become visible, which explanations encounter a test and which results guide the next investigation. The promise of autonomous science therefore concerns both the expansion of experimental capability and the transformation of experimental experience into scientific understanding.

Robot scientists and self-driving laboratories have demonstrated hypothesis testing, materials discovery and reaction optimization [@king2009automation; @burger2020mobile; @macleod2020selfdriving; @szymanski2023alab]. Language-model agents extend the connection between scientific instructions, tools and laboratory operations [@bran2024augmenting; @dai2024mobile]. These advances make it possible to investigate a further question: how does an agent's own research change the relationships it uses to explain and predict the world? A successful procedure establishes an experimental achievement, and a correct forecast establishes predictive accuracy. Neither alone reveals the evidence from which a judgment arose or the scope that evidence supports [@elmachtoub2022spo; @shmueli2010explain].

We study this process in ChemWorld. Programmable world laws and independently specified public information make it possible to examine how initial knowledge enters experimental choices, how observations support or change judgments, and how these judgments guide further inquiry. Our evidence follows the organization of research through the formation of working relationships to their use under new interventions. Successful local tests, warranted revisions, unsupported interpretations of execution and response-specific transfer need not share a single failure mechanism. Together they reveal how the scientific consequences of research depend on the transitions between choosing evidence, interpreting it and applying what it supports.

## Making autonomous scientific inquiry experimentally tractable

ChemWorld combines materials, operations and instruments into stateful virtual chemical systems. Agents choose quantities, processing sequences, measurements, repetitions and subsequent experiments within a fixed resource allowance. Their choices change samples and determine which observations become available. An evaluator independently controls world parameters and public information, records committed operations and obtains withheld reference outcomes (Fig. 1). The agent does not receive those outcomes during research or assessment.

This arrangement makes the agent's research process experimentally accessible. Knowledge tests and supplied-data tasks assess important scientific abilities [@mirza2025chembench; @majumder2025discoverybench], and interactive discovery environments already connect action to observation [@wang2022scienceworld; @jansen2024discoveryworld; @gandhi2025boxinggym; @chen2025physgym]. ChemWorld connects an agent's evidence selection to evolving public judgments and their later use in composable chemical processes. An attempted operation can be rejected before changing the sample; an observation can support a relationship locally without establishing it under every compatible intervention. Execution records and independently evaluated world responses allow these distinctions to be checked.

The broad study comprises 240 Sol campaigns across six chemical families and 60 additional equilibrium campaigns from four other model configurations. The current process analysis combines 45 equilibrium sessions with 45 crystallization sessions conducted under a corrected, consistent recovery definition. Each system covers five fixed worlds, three information conditions and three fresh model sessions per condition. These 90 sessions contain 80 complete research-and-assessment chains and 83 valid forecast stages; incomplete sessions remain in the denominator. Earlier crystallization sessions affected by a recovery-description mismatch are retained separately, rather than pooled with their replacement cohort.

Contemporaneous notes connect publicly stated judgments to the observations available at that time. After research, agents seal a scientific account, predict outcomes under twelve new interventions without laboratory access, and reflect without receiving reference truth. Structured process analysis identifies 92 observable judgment–experiment–revision windows across 65 of the current 90 sessions (Extended Data Fig. 1). The other 25 sessions lack a complete window under this recording definition; this does not establish an absence of scientific learning. A separate contemporaneous comparison assigns 30 fresh sessions to research or no research under a common assessment protocol (Fig. 4). These evidence layers answer complementary questions and retain separate denominators.

## The self-organization and adaptation of scientific inquiry

The organization of inquiry begins with a goal, available information and a constrained set of actions. These conditions shape the research history, but do not determine it in a simple way (Fig. 2). Across 60 historical electrochemical campaigns, discovery and optimization instructions do not produce a consistent division between broad exploration and narrow local search. With twelve batches, mean solvent coverage is 3.93 and 3.73, respectively, and intermediate measurements average 12.00 and 10.53. With twenty-four batches, both goals cover all four solvents and use all twenty-four intermediate measurements. These coarse descriptions do not explain the full difference between the resulting procedures or predictions.

Initial information has a clearer relationship to the starting point of the corrected crystallization studies. All fifteen aligned-information sessions begin with solvent S2 and catalyst C1; all fifteen sessions without an additional instance dossier begin with S0 and C0. Under the specified mismatched dossier, thirteen of fifteen begin with S2 and fourteen with C1; one session ends before a catalyst is successfully added. Mean solvent coverage over the research history, including committed resuspension additions, is 4.00 without the dossier, 2.93 with aligned information and 2.40 with mismatched information. These are concrete differences in the evidence agents choose to acquire. Broader coverage is not itself evidence of better understanding, and the comparisons do not isolate coverage as a cause of subsequent error.

Observations can then change the direction of research. In one equilibrium session from the research-opportunity comparison, the initial note predicts a weak-acid trend: increasing concentration should lower pH and dissociation fraction. The first six experiments instead yield dissociation fractions near 0.064–0.079. The next note withdraws support for the expected trend over that range and proposes that another accessible component might be the relevant input. Experiments eight to twelve actually use that component, yet still yield a near-plateau response. The final account retains a bounded empirical relationship while leaving the hidden mechanism unresolved.

This sequence links an initial expectation, contrary observations, a proposed alternative and a changed experiment. Other studies continue a scan already planned at the outset, narrow a parameter interval or repeat a borderline recipe. All are forms of organizing inquiry, but only some constitute newly proposed tests in response to feedback. In the corrected crystallization cohort, 52 of 58 selected immediate plans match the ensuing committed actions and six match partly. This measures a public plan–action connection within observable windows; it is neither a discovery rate nor evidence that writing the note caused the action. The next question is what those actions and observations establish.

## The formation and evolution of scientific understanding through experimentation

Scientific relationships acquire practical meaning when they support a further test, constrain an explanation or guide a new intervention (Fig. 3). In a corrected crystallization study, a near-threshold fines result leads to a prospective proposal to lower the reagent charge from 0.03 to 0.02 mol. The next batch implements that proposal and passes the fines threshold. The result supports a local recipe improvement. Because the absolute catalyst amount remains fixed, however, the catalyst-to-reagent ratio also changes: the experiment does not separately identify a concentration mechanism.

A second study makes an interpolation. After observing different recovery and fines outcomes at nearby solvent volumes, the researcher proposes that 0.065 L should preserve the recovery benefit while improving fines. The proposed experiment fails, and the final note retracts the expectation. Here, an unsuccessful operating intervention produces an observable revision of a scientific judgment. A further near-threshold study explicitly discusses measurement noise, repeats a 310 K procedure with two waiting stages, and still recommends a recipe that fails retesting. Recognition of uncertainty, purposeful repetition and operational failure can coexist.

Whether an intervention actually occurred is itself part of its evidential status. One researcher plans approximately 1 g of cumulative seed addition, but only 0.05 g is committed and nineteen subsequent additions are rejected. Its later note correctly states that the attempted sequence cannot establish the effect of the high seed dose. Another session encounters analogous rejected additions yet describes 1 g as the tested condition and uses the outcome against a seed-based explanation. The contrast concerns the transformation of an execution history into scientific evidence: the world did not perform the intervention that the latter account treats as a negative test.

A related study attributes low recovery after heating to 440 K to a thermal-history boundary and subsequently tests 430 K. The later test is real, but the purported earlier comparison includes cooling requests to 265 and 270 K that were rejected. The sequence contains adaptation and further experimentation without establishing the thermal contrast claimed in the explanation. Action, observation and interpretation must therefore be connected individually rather than inferred from a coherent final report.

The equilibrium study introduced above makes a different transition. Its component tests appropriately weaken one explanation of the local plateau. When forecasting an extremely dilute condition, however, the agent writes: “The point estimate still favors the experimentally supported fixed acidic background”. It predicts dissociation of 0.085 with an 80% interval of 0.035–0.350; the withheld reference mean is 0.716. Both the scientific account and forecast recognize that the distant regime was not tested. The issue is not simply a failure to mention limits. A locally supported relationship receives substantial weight in a prediction whose continuation the experiments have not resolved.

That continuation is not inevitable. Two sessions in another fixed equilibrium world both observe plateaux at relatively high loading, yet their dilute macro errors are 0.16287 and 0.01462. For the same dilute query, they predict dissociation of 0.130 and 0.626 against a reference mean of 0.65463. The more accurate session uses a weak-acid continuation consistent with its plateau observations and explicitly describes it as conjectural. Accurate extrapolation does not require direct sampling of every target condition; neither does accuracy establish that research experimentally identified the successful explanation. Continuous records distinguish supported local relations, unresolved possibilities and their subsequent predictive use.

## The nature and conditions of scientific progress in autonomous inquiry

The process records locate changes in judgments; the separate research-opportunity comparison tests what the opportunity to investigate contributes to subsequent prediction (Fig. 4). Five equilibrium worlds are each studied by three fresh sessions with twelve experimental batches and three without source experiments. Both groups receive the same scientific public context and the same sealed assessment. All thirty sessions terminate, all 180 planned source batches complete, and twenty-nine sessions provide valid forecasts. One research session loses its forecast and later stages to a network interruption; its source observations and scientific account remain retained.

Research substantially improves prediction overall. Averaging valid sessions within each world and then weighting the five worlds equally, normalized macro mean absolute error falls from 0.132086 without research to 0.035632 with research, a 73.0% reduction. The direction holds in all five worlds. Yet for the three prospectively designated extreme-dilution queries, error rises from 0.061467 to 0.103677, a 68.7% increase, with deterioration in four of five worlds. Over the remaining nine queries it falls from 0.155625 to 0.012950. The comparison concerns the total opportunity to conduct research, including experiment selection, feedback and available recording tools; it does not isolate any one component.

The same opportunity can therefore improve average prediction while worsening transfer in a defined region. The continuous equilibrium case connects a revision induced by observations to the later weighting of a local relationship in a distant forecast. Its no-research counterpart predicts dissociation of 0.569 for the query above, closer to the reference, but explicitly relies on an untested prior assumption. That better forecast is not itself an experimentally established discovery.

The result extends beyond point error without yielding a universal story of misplaced confidence. At extreme dilution, research produces lower empirical coverage than no research for all three nominal 80% intervals, while the no-research intervals are substantially wider. An interval score penalizing both width and misses favours research for pH, favours no research for dissociation fraction and is similar for precipitation signal. Across all queries, research improves interval scores for all three responses. A conservative sensitivity analysis allowing the missing session's macro error to take any value in its legal range does not reverse either the overall benefit or the dilute deterioration (Methods).

Crystallization exposes the uneven use of evidence already acquired (Extended Data Fig. 2). For each of the 42 valid forecasts, we compare the agent with transparent references using only that same session's source observations. Five-world-equal purity error is 0.03853 for the agent and 0.00510 for the overall source mean; the source mean is more accurate in every valid session. Recovery shows the opposite pattern: agent error is 0.10129 against 0.20443, with the agent outperforming the mean in 39 of 42 sessions. Conditioning the reference on the exact committed solvent set preserves both aggregate directions. Size and fines differ again, so the response channels are reported separately rather than collapsed into a common learning score.

These observations complement earlier operating–predictive comparisons. In electrochemistry, optimization produces a better recommendation in 26 of 30 matched conditions; among those 26, prediction is better in thirteen and worse in thirteen. In the repeated equilibrium cohort, aligned partial knowledge reduces error over nine queries from 0.00907 to 0.00512 while increasing dilute error from 0.09880 to 0.13205. Initial information, experimental achievement and subsequent prediction each contribute to the scientific outcome, but none substitutes for examining how research supports a relationship and how that relationship is used.

## Discussion

Autonomous inquiry organizes both interventions in the world and the evidence on which subsequent judgments depend. ChemWorld makes the connections between these activities observable: initial information enters choices; observations support local relations and revisions; those relations guide further tests and predictions. The principal finding is that progress can differentiate within this process. An experiment can establish a useful local relationship without resolving its continuation elsewhere, and one research history can support markedly different predictive outcomes across response variables.

Studying the formation of scientific understanding requires specifying which relationship is proposed, what information supports it at that point, what the world actually tests and how the relationship is later used. The records include warranted retractions and bounded improvements alongside interpretations unsupported by committed operations. They also include accurate forecasts grounded in possibilities that remain experimentally unresolved. These distinctions are consistent with the role of discriminating tests and inference in scientific inquiry [@platt1964inference; @lindley1956information; @chaloner1995design], but their implementation by autonomous agents cannot be assumed from successful tool use or a plausible scientific account.

The controls delimit what can be explained causally. The matched opportunity comparison estimates the effect of the full research package under a common assessment protocol. A separate fixed-recipe quench intervention identifies physical effects of an operation in the virtual worlds, without providing outcomes to an adapting agent (Extended Data Fig. 3). Neither identifies a notebook's independent effect or proves that a particular judgment maintains itself through selective experimentation. Explaining the observed formation and use of relationships does not require every trajectory to share such a mechanism or end in a successful corrective strategy.

The study is limited to selected programmable systems, a small fixed set of worlds and, for repeated process analyses, one model configuration. Fresh sessions are not controlled model-seed replications or independent samples of world laws. Notebook statements are public records rather than access to private cognition; gaps restrict temporal inference. Cases are retrospectively selected from systematically retained records, with single-analyst interpretation and no second-rater reliability estimate. Historical and current protocols retain separate provenance, including the corrected crystallization block and all failures. These limits constrain claims about prevalence and generality while leaving the observed transitions and bounded comparisons available for scrutiny.

The scientific promise of autonomy concerns how self-organized investigation changes the relationships that guide understanding of the world. By connecting choices, evidence, judgments and subsequent use, ChemWorld turns this transformation into a tractable object of study—one in which experimental progress, warranted understanding and reliable transfer can be examined together without being treated as the same achievement.
