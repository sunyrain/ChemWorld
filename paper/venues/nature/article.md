**Autonomous science requires explanations that guide experimentation and remain responsive to evidence. How agents develop such explanations through their own research remains poorly understood. Here we introduce ChemWorld, programmable chemical worlds combining autonomous experimentation with independent control of underlying laws and supplied information. Across chemical systems, model configurations and repeated investigations, we trace candidate explanations through predictions, executed tests, revisions and subsequent use. Agents propose alternatives to unexpected observations, establish bounded relationships and retract unsupported expectations. Yet proposing a discriminating test does not ensure that its implementation distinguishes the proposed alternatives. An explanation can remain unresolved in a scientific account while acquiring experimental authority in a later prediction. Accurate forecasts also arise through distinct evidential routes, from observing a new response to extrapolating beyond local measurements. Controlled comparisons connect these histories to the consequences of conducting research. ChemWorld makes the formation, testing and evidential standing of explanations accessible to empirical investigation, linking experimental autonomy to the development of scientific understanding.**

Einstein opened his 1905 paper on special relativity with an explanatory tension: the observed effects of the relative motion of a magnet and a conductor did not require the asymmetry imposed by their prevailing theoretical descriptions. He introduced principles from which consequences for simultaneity, time and motion could be derived, using imagined clocks and light signals to develop their implications [@einstein1905electrodynamics]. This episode illustrates a central demand of scientific discovery: observations must be connected to explanations whose consequences can be reasoned through and brought into contact with evidence. The intellectual work extends from recognizing a problem to constructing and testing a way of understanding it.

Abduction names a crucial part of this activity: proposing explanatory hypotheses worth investigating. In the Peircean account, abduction introduces a candidate explanation, deduction develops its testable consequences, and empirical inquiry evaluates and revises it [@douven2025peirce]. These forms of reasoning work together. A conjecture becomes scientifically productive when it suggests what to investigate and remains answerable to what is observed. The relevant capacity is therefore sustained explanatory inquiry: developing accounts of the world, deriving expectations from them and allowing evidence to change what those accounts warrant.

This capacity becomes an empirical question as AI scientists and self-driving laboratories take responsibility for increasingly extended investigations [@tom2024selfdriving; @boiko2023autonomous]. Robot scientists and autonomous laboratories have demonstrated hypothesis testing, materials discovery and reaction optimization [@king2009automation; @burger2020mobile; @macleod2020selfdriving; @szymanski2023alab], while language-model agents connect scientific instructions with tools and laboratory operations [@bran2024augmenting; @dai2024mobile]. As agents gain authority over experiment selection, the scientific question concerns how they develop the explanations that organize those choices. Can an autonomous researcher turn an unexpected observation into a candidate explanation, an informative test and a revised understanding?

The challenge is that the evidence available to an autonomous researcher is itself an outcome of its inquiry. An explanation shapes which experiments seem useful; those experiments determine which observations can support or challenge it. Effective procedures and accurate forecasts are important achievements, but neither alone establishes how an explanation formed or what its evidence supports [@elmachtoub2022spo; @shmueli2010explain]. A successful experiment may establish a local relationship while leaving its mechanism or wider applicability unresolved. A failed prediction may advance understanding by motivating a warranted revision. Experimental design and strong inference describe how tests can discriminate possibilities [@platt1964inference; @lindley1956information; @chaloner1995design]; autonomous inquiry makes their practical realization a subject for investigation.

Here we use ChemWorld to study how autonomous researchers form, test and develop explanations through interaction with programmable chemical worlds. Contemporaneous records follow expectations through observations, candidate explanations, executed tests and revisions. We reconstruct what each test could establish and how its result changes the later standing of an explanation. Cross-system comparisons locate these histories within broader research choices and outcomes. Five-model studies reveal different routes from evidence to extrapolation, and matched research-opportunity comparisons examine what conducting research contributes to later judgments. Together, these layers connect the breadth of experimental autonomy to the scientific content produced through it: how explanations become useful, how they acquire support and how they are carried into conditions that research has yet to resolve.

## Making abductive inquiry experimentally tractable

ChemWorld combines materials, operations and instruments into stateful virtual chemical systems governed by thermodynamic, kinetic and coupled process relationships. Reaction, thermal processing, phase equilibrium, separation and crystallization components can be connected into multistage procedures. Sample states persist, so an upstream reaction or thermal history can change a later separation or measurement. Agents choose quantities, processing sequences, measurements, repetitions and subsequent experiments within a fixed resource allowance. Their choices determine both the procedures they develop and the observations from which they reason. An evaluator independently controls world parameters and public information, records committed operations and obtains withheld reference outcomes (Fig. 1). The agent does not receive those outcomes during research or assessment.

This arrangement makes the agent's research process experimentally accessible. Knowledge tests and supplied-data tasks assess important scientific abilities [@mirza2025chembench; @majumder2025discoverybench], and interactive discovery environments already connect action to observation [@wang2022scienceworld; @jansen2024discoveryworld; @gandhi2025boxinggym; @chen2025physgym]. ChemWorld connects an agent's evidence selection to evolving public judgments and their later use in composable chemical processes. An attempted operation can be rejected before changing the sample; an observation can support a relationship locally without establishing it under every compatible intervention. Execution records and independently evaluated world responses allow these distinctions to be checked.

The broad study comprises 240 Sol campaigns across electrochemistry, partitioning, reaction processing, aqueous equilibrium, reaction-coupled crystallization and purification, plus 60 equilibrium campaigns from four other model configurations. The systems support different scientific questions: developing an effective electrical or thermal procedure, characterizing a concentration response, or recovering material while respecting quality constraints. Discovery and optimization objectives, partial prior information and experimental budgets provide complementary comparisons within the specified study blocks. Each block uses five fixed worlds and three information conditions: Opaque retains the operational contract without an additional instance dossier; Aligned supplies correct but incomplete information; MisIndexed supplies a specified mismatch. These manipulations change starting conditions for inquiry while preserving the corresponding experimental interface.

The current process analysis adds temporal resolution through 45 equilibrium sessions and 45 crystallization sessions conducted under a corrected, consistent recovery definition. Each system covers five fixed worlds, three information conditions and three fresh model sessions per condition. These 90 sessions contain 80 complete research-and-assessment chains and 83 valid forecast stages; incomplete sessions remain in the denominator. Earlier crystallization sessions affected by a recovery-description mismatch are retained separately. The broad study establishes cross-system contrasts, whereas the repeated studies examine how particular judgments arise and vary across research histories; their protocols and denominators are not pooled.

Contemporaneous notes connect publicly stated judgments to the observations available at that time. After research, agents seal a scientific account, predict outcomes under twelve new interventions without laboratory access, and reflect without receiving reference truth. A structural inventory identifies 92 action-linked note windows across 65 of these 90 sessions (Extended Data Fig. 3). For explanation-level interpretation, we instead follow the same proposition across versions, including terminal revisions and proposed but unexecuted tests. The focal equilibrium history belongs to the separate research-opportunity comparison, not this window cohort. A separate contemporaneous comparison assigns 30 fresh sessions to research or no research under a common assessment protocol. Across these layers, we distinguish a relationship's agreement with world outcomes, the evidence available to support it and the way the researcher subsequently uses it.

## From unexpected observations to explanatory hypotheses

The starting point for explanatory inquiry is a relation between an observation and the account that was expected to explain it. In the trajectory we call equilibrium study A, the researcher begins with the public weak-acid/precipitation framing but no instance-specific archival relationship. Before any source experiment, it predicts that increasing nominal reagent concentration will lower pH and fractional dissociation. Its plan includes a concentration series, comparisons at equal concentration and repeated measurements. This is an initial theoretical expectation, not an explanation newly discovered from the subsequent data.

The first six batches span nominal reagent concentrations of 0.05–0.6 mol/L. Their final assays instead give normalized pH of 0.250–0.256 and dissociation fractions of 0.064–0.079, with small, non-monotone variation (Fig. 2a–c). The next note marks the expected concentration effect as unsupported in this range, explicitly distinguishing lack of resolution from proof that no effect exists. The discrepancy therefore changes the standing of a prior expectation before it changes the experimental plan.

The researcher then proposes a new account: “The selectable component 0, rather than anonymous limiting reagent, may be the chemically relevant total acid loading”. This candidate appears after the six assays and before the component-labelled operations. The note supplies conditional expectations: a systematic pH or dissociation curve, or a precipitation threshold, would support the candidate; flat responses would favour a fixed-background or weakly coupled interpretation. The candidate therefore connects a phenomenon requiring explanation to a proposed intervention and an anticipated evidential contrast. This is the observable abductive move in the record.

The next batch extends the original reagent series to 0.8 mol/L, and batches eight to twelve use the component command at final nominal concentrations of 0.1–0.8 mol/L. All twelve final assays remain near the same plateau. The terminal note withdraws support for the expected trend and retains a bounded empirical approximation. It explicitly leaves fixed-background, saturation or clamping, and loading-dependent responses below assay resolution observationally equivalent within the sampled range. It also proposes broader dilution or orthogonal solubility measurement as possible challenges to that approximation; these proposals are not followed by further source experiments.

This history separates three propositions that would otherwise be conflated: the initial weak-acid expectation, the alternative account of which input determines acid loading, and the empirical near-constancy of the measured response. The first loses support locally; the second motivates action; the third becomes a useful bounded description. The researcher does not claim to have uniquely identified the mechanism in its terminal account. Following these propositions across the complete history, rather than treating each adjacent note pair as a separate discovery, preserves both explanatory initiative and unresolved alternatives.

## When experiments constrain an explanation

A test's scientific content depends on what its execution changes. Inspection of the retained execution code and the focal world's material mapping reveals an important limit in study A: the reagent command and component 0 both add the same single feed species and update the same initial-loading ledger. Thus the component-labelled series does not implement a chemically distinct acid-source intervention. The candidate explanation and the intended contrast are visible in the notes, but the action names do not create the distinction the agent envisages (Fig. 3c).

The measurements still have evidential value. Across the tested preparations they fail to reveal the anticipated loading trend and extend the observed near-constant response. What they cannot establish is that changing chemical identity selects a fixed-background explanation over a loading-dependent alternative. This is a specific limit of this test, not a claim that every unresolved mechanism is unsupported. The evidence constrains local observables more directly than it ranks the proposed hidden causes. The terminal note partly preserves this distinction by retaining several equivalent explanations.

Crystallization supplies complementary histories in which a prospective prediction is tested and its result changes a working judgment. In the lower-charge study, an early solvent-and-catalyst prior is narrowed through successive screens. A high-seed thermal cycle fails to control fines, and reducing reagent charge to 0.03 mol improves the result but leaves fines just above the 0.50 limit. Before the final batch, the researcher predicts that a further reduction to 0.02 mol will cross the limit while retaining recovery above its feasible fallback. The committed experiment lowers fines from 0.529 to 0.387 and yields recovery of 0.520 (Fig. 3a). The terminal note marks this expectation as supported and recommends that completed batch.

This is a useful local intervention, with a defined evidential boundary. Absolute catalyst amount remains 0.005 mol as reagent charge falls, so catalyst-to-reagent ratio changes as well. The final note itself leaves separate catalyst/loading effects and repeatability unresolved. The observation supports a better tested recipe; it does not isolate a universal concentration mechanism. The analytical value is the complete progression from an outcome near a constraint, through a prospective expectation, to an executed test and a bounded recommendation.

The interpolation study follows the opposite outcome without losing that progression. After a change from 0.08 to 0.06 L raises recovery but leaves fines at 0.512, the researcher predicts that 0.065 L will preserve the recovery gain and restore fines below 0.50. The next batch instead produces fines of 0.601 (Fig. 3b). Its terminal note explicitly retracts the simple interpolation, states that monotonicity is unresolved and returns to the earlier feasible batch. The revision would be excluded by an analysis requiring another experiment after the revised note, yet it changes the final recommendation. A failed operating test can therefore yield an informative revision.

These crystallization expectations are not automatically new explanatory hypotheses. They show how deduction, empirical checking and revision contribute to inquiry once a working relationship has been proposed. Together with study A, they distinguish an intended explanation test that lacks the required physical contrast, a locally supported intervention and a warranted retraction. A retrospective panel follows seven related judgments across the focal equilibrium session and four previously illustrated crystallization sessions, retaining full versions and execution anchors (Methods). It includes two additional seed cases that respectively recognize an unexecuted test and mistake rejected requests for negative evidence. The panel is a content analysis of selected histories; the 92-window inventory remains a separate description of recording coverage.

## Different paths from local evidence to extrapolation

Research paths also differ under the same objective and scientific conditions. In the five-model equilibrium comparison, researchers can choose loading, dilution and addition order before predicting pH, dissociation and precipitation. The hidden laws produce a near-constant dissociation fraction over the higher-concentration region, with a changed response at extreme dilution. Eleven of Astra's fifteen campaigns assay positively loaded samples at nominal concentrations no greater than 1 mM, compared with none of fifteen for each of Sol, GPT-5.5, Luna and Terra (Fig. 4a). This difference describes which evidence these particular campaigns acquire, rather than a fixed exploratory trait of a model.

An Astra campaign illustrates the scientific use of newly acquired evidence (Fig. 4d). Its first three assays show dissociation of 6.3–7.4%; batch five tests 18.5 micromolar and observes 53.8%. The later scientific account distinguishes the high-concentration plateau from the dilute response, and the sealed forecast at 13.3 micromolar is 58.80% with an 80% interval of 54.20–63.40%, against a reference mean of 57.97%. Sol/Opaque takes a different route: despite remaining outside the dilute regime during research, its five-world dilute macro MAE is 0.01833 (Fig. 4b,c). Accurate prediction can therefore accompany either a newly observed change or extrapolation beyond local observations. Direct sampling of every target condition is not a prerequisite for successful inference.

The crucial question is what these different routes establish. Extrapolation can be scientifically legitimate without direct measurement of every target condition, but its support depends on the available theory, observations and unresolved alternatives. Following a judgment from its local evidential basis into a new use exposes that distinction.

The same equilibrium study A now connects a warranted local revision to its later use (Fig. 2d,e). When forecasting an extremely dilute condition, the agent writes: “The point estimate still favors the experimentally supported fixed acidic background”. It predicts dissociation of 0.085 with an 80% interval of 0.035–0.350; the withheld reference mean is 0.716. Both its scientific account and forecast recognize that this regime was not tested. The transition concerns the standing of an explanation, not simply a missing caveat. The experiments support the plateau locally, while the terminal account leaves its causes unresolved; the later prediction nevertheless gives the fixed-background account experimental authority. The available evidence does not determine how much weight each continuation should receive, and the failed forecast alone does not diagnose an irrational inference. What the public record establishes is a change in how the same unresolved account is justified and used. The matched no-research forecast for the same query is 0.569, with interval 0.040–0.980, and explicitly relies on an untested prior assumption. Its greater numerical accuracy does not establish a better experimental discovery.

That continuation is not inevitable. Two sessions in another fixed equilibrium world both observe plateaux at relatively high loading, yet their dilute macro errors are 0.16287 and 0.01462. For the same dilute query, they predict dissociation of 0.130 and 0.626 against a reference mean of 0.65463. The more accurate session uses a weak-acid continuation consistent with its plateau observations and explicitly describes it as conjectural. Together with the historical Sol/Opaque and Astra cases, these records distinguish several routes to a judgment: an observed change, a plausible extrapolation and a local relationship carried into an unresolved regime. Numerical correctness and experimental establishment remain separate questions.

Partial prior knowledge changes these outcomes conditionally. The historical five-model comparison and the separate repeated equilibrium cohort show that locally useful information can accompany either better or worse dilute forecasts (Extended Data Fig. 2). This broadens the evidence beyond one explanation history without assigning all errors to the same process. The historical records lack contemporaneous hypothesis versions, so they establish evidence acquisition and later interpretation rather than the timing of candidate formation.

## The consequences of inquiry for scientific judgment

Explanations must remain useful as research moves beyond the conditions under which they formed. We therefore complement the observed inference paths with a controlled comparison of research opportunity, followed by response-specific checks on how acquired evidence enters new predictions.

The matched research-opportunity comparison asks a more direct question: does access to research change subsequent prediction under common initial scientific information (Fig. 5)? All thirty sessions use Opaque information: the public task, material identities, measurement scales, legal operations and initial state are available, but no instance-specific archival law or fitted relationship is supplied. Each of five equilibrium worlds receives three fresh sessions with twelve source batches and three without source experiments. Scientific inputs and sealed assessments are matched within each world and repeat. All thirty sessions terminate, all 180 planned source batches complete, and twenty-nine sessions provide valid forecasts. One research session loses its forecast and later stages to a network interruption; its observations and scientific account remain retained.

Research substantially improves prediction overall. Averaging valid sessions within each world and then weighting the five worlds equally, normalized macro mean absolute error falls from 0.132086 without research to 0.035632 with research, a 73.0% reduction. The direction holds in all five worlds. Yet for the three prospectively designated extreme-dilution queries, error rises from 0.061467 to 0.103677, a 68.7% increase, with deterioration in four of five worlds. Over the remaining nine queries it falls from 0.155625 to 0.012950. The comparison concerns the total opportunity to conduct research, including experiment selection, feedback and available recording tools; it does not isolate any one component.

Within this comparison, the same research opportunity improves overall prediction while worsening it in a defined region. All three response-specific point errors increase on the dilute subset, but by different amounts: Research-minus-NoResearch MAE is +0.00777 for normalized pH, +0.10795 for dissociation fraction and +0.01092 for precipitation signal. Dissociation accounts for 85.2% of the macro-error increase. Figure 5a,b shows individual sessions and world means; the complete session-by-response and query-level results accompany the manuscript. Equilibrium study A supplies one observed route from a valid local finding to a distant judgment. It does not establish that this route explains every session-level difference or that acquiring more evidence must impair extrapolation.

The result extends beyond point error without yielding a universal story of misplaced confidence. At extreme dilution, research produces lower empirical coverage than no research for all three nominal 80% intervals, while the no-research intervals are substantially wider. An interval score penalizing both width and misses favours research for pH, favours no research for dissociation fraction and is similar for precipitation signal. Across all queries, research improves interval scores for all three responses. A conservative sensitivity analysis allowing the missing session's macro error to take any value in its legal range does not reverse either the overall benefit or the dilute deterioration (Methods).

Crystallization exposes a complementary pattern in the use of evidence already acquired (Fig. 5c,d and Extended Data Fig. 4). For each of 42 valid forecasts, transparent references use only that same session’s source observations. Five-world-equal purity error is 0.03853 for the agent and 0.00510 for the overall source mean, which is more accurate in every valid session. Recovery shows the opposite pattern: agent error is 0.10129 against 0.20443, with the agent outperforming the mean in 39 of 42 sessions. Conditioning on the exact committed solvent set preserves both aggregate directions. Size and fines yield 5/42 and 23/42 wins, respectively. These references diagnose how acquired evidence is used across responses; they are neither no-research controls nor alternative autonomous researchers, and do not estimate the causal benefit of crystallization research.

Reanalysis of the corrected cohort establishes the distribution behind this purity contrast. Across the 42 valid sessions, 504 source assays have mean purity 0.98644 and interquartile range 0.97944–0.99468. The 504 corresponding target-query entries average 0.98463, whereas forecasts average 0.94740; 463 of 504 forecasts fall below their reference means. These are descriptive pooled distributions, with repeated target values retained; the world-equal mean signed error is −0.03754. Thus the discrepancy involves a systematic downward shift from an empirically stable, high-purity region, rather than a few extreme misses. These values are computed from the corrected cohort itself, not imported from the earlier protocol-affected studies.

This response-specific comparison complements the equilibrium result. The challenge is not only to anticipate change beyond an observed region, but also to preserve a relationship when it remains useful. Within the same crystallization histories, predictions extract information about varying recovery more effectively than the source mean, yet fail to match that simple reference for stable purity. Successful evidence use in one response does not establish that all acquired relationships constrain subsequent judgments equally. Across these evidence histories and research opportunities, scientific progress therefore has several observable dimensions: what is achieved experimentally, what the evidence establishes and how well the resulting relationships guide new judgments.

## Discussion

Abductive inquiry gives autonomous science a substantive object of evaluation: the development of explanations through research. The present studies connect the conditions under which agents investigate, the hypotheses and relationships they express, the tests they execute and the judgments they subsequently make. Cross-system comparisons establish that operating success and predictive accuracy can develop differently (Extended Data Fig. 1). Contemporaneous cases show that this divergence can coexist with meaningful scientific activity, including proposing an alternative explanation, testing a local expectation and retracting it when the observation disagrees. The unit of analysis is the explanation in its research history, with the evidence and uses that give it scientific significance.

The results distinguish proposing an explanation, supporting it experimentally and applying it successfully. In equilibrium study A, a plausible candidate motivates an executed test whose physical semantics fail to implement the intended acid-identity contrast. The resulting local regularity remains useful, while the mechanism remains unresolved and later receives stronger evidential wording in a prediction. In crystallization, a successful recipe change establishes a useful intervention while leaving correlated causes unresolved; a failed interpolation supports a warranted retraction. The Astra and Sol/Opaque histories further show that accurate predictions can follow direct observation of a changed response or a conjectural continuation beyond local evidence. These are different scientific achievements. Their distinction is lost when all experiments are treated as interchangeable data collection or all correct predictions as equivalent evidence of discovery.

This perspective places abduction within a continuing interaction with deduction and empirical testing. A candidate explanation opens a possible account of observations; its consequences guide tests; their outcomes can strengthen, delimit or displace it. The empirical records also show where this relationship becomes incomplete: a rejected operation can be remembered as a completed test, a local regularity can dominate an unresolved extrapolation, or an established response can fail to constrain prediction. Discovery and misunderstanding can therefore arise at different points within the same inquiry. Examining those transitions makes it possible to ask what a researcher has reason to conclude, as well as whether its conclusion happens to be correct [@shmueli2010explain; @gneiting2007scoring; @ovadia2019uncertainty].

The controlled comparisons complement this process account. Research goals and prior information reveal different conditions of inquiry; the matched opportunity study examines the total contribution of conducting research under common initial information. Its overall gains and regional reversal show that scientific benefit can depend on the question being asked. They do not isolate an effect of abduction, autonomy, feedback or notebook use. A separate fixed-recipe quench comparison identifies physical consequences of an intervention without providing feedback to an adapting agent (Extended Data Fig. 5). Temporal links between public judgments and actions likewise do not establish a unique causal mechanism for their formation. These distinctions allow different experiments to contribute to a common question without assigning them the same causal claim.

The study concerns selected programmable systems and a small fixed set of worlds; repeated process analysis uses one model configuration. Fresh sessions are not controlled model-seed replications or independent samples of world laws. Public notes do not expose private cognition, and observable explanatory activity does not establish the invention of a novel scientific theory. The framework organizes interpretation of retained evidence rather than a new quantitative test of abductive ability. Cases are retrospectively selected, with single-analyst interpretation and no second-rater reliability estimate. Historical and current protocols retain separate provenance, including corrected crystallization and all failures. These boundaries specify the scale at which the observed formation, testing and use of explanations are supported.

The broader ambition is to understand how a system capable of experimenting can become a system capable of developing scientific explanations. Einstein's example motivates the intellectual demand, while programmable worlds make particular components of that demand accessible to controlled study. ChemWorld connects the explanations an agent proposes to the experiments it chooses and to the evidence that can change its account of the world. Scientific autonomy develops through this continuing responsiveness: the ability to pursue an explanation, determine what it establishes and revise its reach as inquiry proceeds.
