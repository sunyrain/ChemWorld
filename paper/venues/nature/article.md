title: How autonomous research shapes scientific understanding

**Autonomous science promises to turn experimental agency into scientific understanding. Here we introduce ChemWorld, programmable chemical worlds in which agents choose experiments while underlying laws and supplied information remain independently controllable. Across six systems, repeated investigations and controlled comparisons connect research conditions, experimental histories and subsequent judgments. Agents test expectations, revise local relationships and use results to select further experiments; their interpretations also change resource use and subsequent evidence opportunities. In matched worlds, final forecasts reflect differences in chemical response. Research improves crystallization predictions, yet alternative uses of the same observations yield response-dependent advantages. Better point predictions can coexist with poorer uncertainty estimates, while equilibrium research improves overall prediction but worsens extreme-dilution judgments. Autonomous research therefore establishes useful relationships whose predictive value varies across responses and conditions. ChemWorld makes the development and use of these relationships accessible to controlled empirical investigation.**

Scientific discovery introduces ways of understanding phenomena that make new investigations possible. Einstein opened his 1905 paper on special relativity with a tension between the observable effects of magnet–conductor relative motion and the asymmetry of their theoretical descriptions, then developed principles with consequences for time and motion [@einstein1905electrodynamics]. Proposing an explanatory possibility worth investigating is one aspect of this creative activity, captured by the concept of abduction [@douven2025peirce]. More broadly, a developing understanding changes what researchers compare, which results require explanation and where they investigate next.

This connection becomes a practical question as AI scientists and self-driving laboratories assume greater responsibility for organizing research [@tom2024selfdriving; @boiko2023autonomous]. Robot scientists and autonomous laboratories have demonstrated hypothesis testing, materials discovery and reaction optimization [@king2009automation; @burger2020mobile; @macleod2020selfdriving; @szymanski2023alab], while language-model agents connect scientific instructions to laboratory tools and operations [@bran2024augmenting; @dai2024mobile]. Experimental success establishes an achievement, but does not by itself reveal what relationship a researcher learned or how broadly its evidence supports that relationship [@elmachtoub2022spo; @shmueli2010explain].

The evidence available to an autonomous researcher is itself an outcome of inquiry. Goals and prior knowledge influence the experiments selected; those experiments reveal particular phenomena and consume resources; the resulting judgments can redirect further exploration. A successful experiment may support a useful local relationship, while an unsuccessful one may rule out an expectation and change the next decision. Understanding this process requires following both how evidence changes judgments and how judgments change the opportunity to obtain evidence [@platt1964inference; @lindley1956information; @chaloner1995design].

We investigate these connections in ChemWorld. Our question is how agents organize inquiry to develop scientific understanding when their initial information does not determine the answer, and how that understanding shapes subsequent research. Four complementary matrices across six chemical systems connect research goals and information, continuous research histories, access to experimentation and discrimination between matched worlds. We follow specific judgments within complete trajectories and compare their consequences across repeated sessions. The resulting account links the organization of inquiry to the relationships it establishes, revises and later uses.

## Making autonomous inquiry experimentally tractable

ChemWorld combines materials, operations and instruments into stateful chemical worlds governed by thermodynamic, kinetic and coupled process relationships. Reaction, thermal processing, equilibrium, separation and crystallization components connect into multistage procedures. Sample states persist: an upstream reaction or thermal history can change a later separation or measurement. Agents choose quantities, processing sequences, measurements and subsequent experiments within fixed resource allowances. An evaluator independently controls world parameters and public information, records committed operations and obtains withheld reference outcomes (Fig. 1).

This arrangement connects the selection of evidence to its interpretation. Knowledge tests and supplied-data tasks assess important scientific abilities [@mirza2025chembench; @majumder2025discoverybench], and interactive discovery environments already connect action to observation [@wang2022scienceworld; @jansen2024discoveryworld; @gandhi2025boxinggym; @chen2025physgym]. ChemWorld makes these choices inspectable within composable chemical processes. Public records identify what the agent saw and proposed; execution records establish which interventions changed the sample; independent world responses test later predictions. A requested operation can fail without producing the intended experiment, and a relationship supported in one setting may remain unresolved in another.

The design covers 450 planned investigations in four complementary matrices. The objective–information matrix contains ninety electrochemical and ninety reaction-processing sessions. The process matrix contains forty-five sessions each in partitioning, purification, equilibrium and reaction-coupled crystallization. The opportunity matrix compares research with no research in equilibrium and crystallization, thirty sessions per system. Thirty further investigations use matched aqueous worlds (Methods; Extended Data Fig. 2).

Opaque information supplies the task and operating contract without an additional instance dossier; Aligned supplies correct but incomplete information; MisIndexed supplies a specified mismatch. Discovery commissions emphasize investigating relationships, whereas optimization commissions emphasize developing an effective procedure. Worlds, interfaces and allowances are held to each fixed block's design, with three fresh sessions per cell and no controlled model sampling seed. These contrasts specify the conditions of research; the agents organize the actual paths.

After experimentation, researchers seal a scientific account, predict twelve new interventions without laboratory access and reflect without reference-truth feedback. We connect contemporaneous judgments to operations, observations, revisions and later use. All 300 public histories in the new experimental block were read, including incomplete histories and unsuccessful tests; illustrative trajectories were then selected for their explanatory content. Study labels connect the cases below to their full records and critical times in Supplementary Information. The 450-session design establishes coverage; condition comparisons estimate outcomes; selected histories establish particular transitions rather than their prevalence. Throughout, we distinguish world truth, the evidence available at a given time and the judgment adopted.

## The organization and adaptation of scientific inquiry

Research goals and information create different starting conditions, but their consequences depend on what researchers choose to investigate. In the repeated electrochemical matrix, optimization rather than discovery increases world-equal mean retested recommendation score from 0.4813 to 0.5852 and product yield from 0.1310 to 0.4225. Prediction errors nevertheless increase for score (0.1539 to 0.1829), yield (0.0665 to 0.0781) and Faradaic efficiency (0.1677 to 0.2218), each in all five worlds (Fig. 2a,b). Developing a more effective procedure does not ensure better judgment of new interventions.

Information has similarly differentiated consequences. Under reaction optimization, Aligned rather than Opaque information improves recommendation score from 0.2735 to 0.4429 and yield from 0.5502 to 0.7116. Risk prediction error falls from 0.2389 to 0.1226, whereas conversion error rises from 0.0355 to 0.1595; all four directions occur in all five worlds (Fig. 2c,d). Thus the same information package can support more effective interventions and more accurate risk judgments while leaving conversion less accurately predicted.

Purification study A shows directly how a result changes the organization of inquiry (Fig. 3a). A no-wash procedure gives purity 0.8140 and recovery 0.0479. Increasing the organic-phase amount raises recovery but leaves purity at 0.7886, below the required 0.80. Before batch 11, the researcher predicts that a small 0.005 L wash will cross the purity threshold. It also sets a prospective action criterion: “if it passes with recovery above 0.0479, replicate it in batch 12.” This separates an expected scientific response from the condition for pursuing the procedure.

Batch 11 reaches purity 0.8013 but recovery only 0.0441. The purity expectation is met, while the continuation criterion is not. Batch 12 therefore repeats the earlier no-wash procedure, yielding purity 0.8149 and recovery 0.0368. The final account recognizes repeated qualification alongside variation in recovery, and later predictions retain the observed purity–recovery trade-off. The two responses play different roles in the next decision: the wash meets the expected purity threshold, but its recovery determines whether the researcher pursues it.

Purification study B shows how a judgment changes the evidence still obtainable. After four low-recovery batches, the researcher proposes that a feed component was omitted and states that it will add that component in the remaining experiments. The next four batches implement both addition commands, which consume the same shared stock. Stock depletion then prevents the planned temperature experiments. In the final note, the researcher correctly excludes those rejected or blank operations from temperature-response evidence. Acting on an interpretation of the feed thus changes material use and the scope of subsequent inquiry.

Two electrochemical histories connect the repeated goal comparison to different uses of local evidence. Electrochemical study A begins with a matched sign comparison: negative potential produces a response where the tested positive setting is effectively inactive. Subsequent batches refine materials and duration on the negative side. The terminal note explicitly limits the sign result to the tested recipe. Yet later numerical forecasts assign near-background responses to untested positive potentials within the examined magnitude range, with intervals missing the references. A stated boundary and its application in new judgments can therefore diverge.

Electrochemical study B, also commissioned to optimize, makes the scope of polarity dependence an experimental question (Fig. 3c). After ten batches of material and duration investigation, the researcher records that positive-polarity specificity has not undergone a matched reverse-sign test. It reserves that comparison and an exact repeat, writing: “If negative potential retains high yield, the assumed polarity specificity is rejected”. Batch 11 at −1.0 V retains yield 0.384; the positive recipe and its final repeat give approximately 0.911 and 0.905. The terminal account changes from apparent polarity specificity to polarity advantage, and subsequent forecasts retain reverse-polarity production. In this world and information condition, an explanatory test within optimization narrows a relationship while preserving its practical value.

## The formation and revision of scientific understanding

Reaction study A follows an expectation from observation through a new experiment to later use (Fig. 3b). At 390 K and 1,800 s heating, direct termination gives conversion 0.41718. A later batch with the same heating followed by an unquenched 1,800 s wait reaches 0.61847 despite cooling by about 44 K. The researcher treats this as evidence that stopping external heating has not ended the chemical change. Before the final batch, it predicts that quenching before the same wait should preserve conversion near 0.42, rather than the approximately 0.62 unquenched-wait result.

The final batch implements that order and gives conversion 0.42724. The subsequent note retains a local relationship between the quench operation and suppression of further conversion. This evidence establishes which part of the processing history remains chemically consequential. Later forecasts use the relationship in new procedures, while transfer to unobserved solvent and loading conditions remains less accurate. The comparison supports the operational expectation under the tested sequence; its differing cooling histories leave thermal and other effects unresolved (Methods).

The three histories connect judgment to research in different ways. Purification applies an existing continuation criterion to a new observation; reaction processing tests a local operational expectation; electrochemistry revises the content of a scientific relationship. In each, the scientific judgment has a specific consequence for the comparison undertaken or the subsequent use of its result.

The matched-world matrix examines how different underlying chemistry enters the evidence and subsequent judgments (Fig. 4). Five pairs of aqueous worlds share public framing and selected parameters but differ in coupling to an additional neutral ion pair. Replaying chosen action sequences in their paired worlds shows that every one of thirty sessions acquires at least one observation differing by three assay standard deviations in two response channels. The chosen experiments therefore encounter physically distinguishable responses.

Final forecasts also reflect these world differences. A post hoc analysis compares B-minus-A differences in the three-session forecast means with differences in the five-observation reference means at twelve common queries (Fig. 4a–c). Among nonzero reference differences, directions agree in 54/57 pH, 48/57 dissociation and 39/39 precipitation contrasts. The remaining 3, 3 and 21 reference differences are zero. All 180 contrasts remain in the analysis; in the descriptive subset reaching three assay standard deviations, directions agree in 48/48, 10/10 and 27/27 cases, respectively.

The forecasts capture difference magnitude as well as direction (Fig. 4e). Mean absolute contrast errors are 0.002256 for normalized pH, 0.002945 for dissociation and 0.002368 for precipitation. Giving identical forecasts in the two worlds would instead yield errors of 0.026007, 0.007239 and 0.024840. Forecast errors are thus 91.3%, 59.3% and 90.5% lower than this zero-difference reference, with an advantage in all five world pairs for each response. These are properties of the final three-session mean forecasts. The block has no no-research arm and supplies close but nonidentical starter observations, so it does not isolate a research-induced gain.

In matched-world study A, the researcher states that a sharp precipitation onset with a buffered dissolved response would support a solubility-limited interpretation, while smooth changes would also admit an empirical partition description. After six assays it proposes denser concentration sampling, a central repeat and increased loading; the next six batches implement those choices. The twelve actual observations provide the evidence behind the adopted relation (Fig. 4d; Supplementary Information). Its final account retains a smooth monotone relationship, and its forecast explicitly uses that relationship because no sharp threshold was resolved. Matched-world study B predicts a response to diluting a concentrated sample from 0.75 to 0.375 M and then performs the dilution. Normalized pH rises from 0.08045 to 0.10803. The researcher supports approximate reversibility, while the nearest separately prepared comparator is 0.4 M and the precipitation readings come from different instruments. These records connect stated alternatives, chosen comparisons and the relationships subsequently used.

Complementary histories show that learning can also proceed through failed expectations (Extended Data Fig. 8). Stronger mixing defeats a provisional partitioning plateau; a fourth crystallization thermal cycle fails to extend the improvement from the third; further dilution removes recoverable particles and narrows an apparent operating window. These tests contribute through the judgments they revise, even when they do not improve the final procedure. The effects of simultaneously changed factors remain unresolved where the experiments did not separate them.

Interpreting an experimental history also requires determining which operations actually occurred. Some researchers treat rolled-back operations as completed tests, while others explicitly exclude them (Extended Data Fig. 6). In the focal heating case, the public return combines success labels with a rollback message and zero time and temperature changes. The resulting account incorporates this ambiguous execution history into later judgments. These cases expose how interface signals and their interpretation jointly shape the evidence a researcher treats as available.

Across these histories, understanding develops through changes in what is compared, which relationship is supported and how it enters another decision. Historical five-model equilibrium campaigns show a further distinction: accurate predictions can follow either direct exploration of a dilute region or extrapolation from local observations (Extended Data Fig. 7). Similar endpoints can therefore arise through different evidence histories.

## Scientific progress across responses and conditions

The opportunity matrix measures the consequences of access to research. In each of two systems, five fixed worlds and three fresh sessions per arm compare twelve-batch research with no source experimentation under common Opaque information and assessment. The contrast estimates the complete opportunity to choose experiments, receive feedback and record judgments.

The crystallization comparison establishes substantial benefits (Fig. 5a). All thirty sessions provide valid forecasts. Relative to no research, world-equal mean absolute error falls from 0.13853 to 0.01661 for purity and from 0.23619 to 0.07641 for size index. Recovery error falls from 0.13532 to 0.11579 and fines error from 0.37683 to 0.30110. For purity, nominal 80% interval coverage rises from 0.46667 to 0.98333 while width contracts from 0.44744 to 0.09743. Research experience produces more accurate predictions and narrower intervals that cover more reference outcomes.

The same cohort distinguishes better point prediction from better uncertainty estimates (Fig. 5c). For fines, coverage falls from 0.42778 to 0.35556 as interval width contracts from 0.68500 to 0.38450. The interval score, which penalizes width and missed outcomes, worsens from 1.4794 to 1.8047 in the world-equal mean, with worsening in every world. Research reduces average fines prediction error, but the resulting intervals express more certainty than their performance warrants. The benefit and the limitation concern the same response in the same comparison.

A second comparison asks what predictions can be obtained from these same research histories (Fig. 5b). For the fifteen Research sessions, a source-observation mean has purity error 0.00450 and size error 0.01786, below the agent's 0.01661 and 0.07641 in all fifteen sessions. The pattern reverses for recovery: the agent's 0.11579 improves on the reference's 0.18828, with an advantage in fourteen sessions. This alternative prediction rule uses only each session's public source observations, receives no target outcomes for fitting and is scored on the same targets. A constant reference captures stable responses effectively, whereas the agent makes better recovery predictions. Research is valuable, and how its observations are used determines further response-specific advantages. The earlier corrected-crystallization cohort provides a separate supporting comparison (Extended Data Fig. 3).

The equilibrium opportunity comparison reveals a regional boundary (Fig. 5d). Among fourteen valid Research forecasts and fifteen NoResearch forecasts, overall normalized macro error falls from 0.132086 to 0.035632, with improvement in all five worlds. Error on the three prospectively specified extreme-dilution queries instead rises from 0.061467 to 0.103677, worsening in four worlds; the remaining nine improve from 0.155625 to 0.012950. Allowing the missing Research forecast any error in its legal range does not reverse either aggregate direction (Methods). Research improves most predictions while judgments in another region become less reliable. The plateau history shows how one researcher applies a locally supported relation to that unresolved region (Extended Data Fig. 5).

Information comparisons further specify the conditions of progress. Partitioning phase-fraction errors are 0.04654, 0.05621 and 0.07700 under Aligned, Opaque and MisIndexed information; Aligned improves on MisIndexed in all five worlds. Purification purity errors remain similar across conditions, approximately 0.152–0.154, while MisIndexed has the lowest recovery error. Together with the reaction contrast, these results show that an information package can aid one judgment without improving every response. Historical prior and budget comparisons provide further boundaries in separate cohorts (Extended Data Fig. 1; Supplementary Information).

The findings locate scientific progress at several connected points in inquiry. Experiments establish useful local relationships; those relationships inform new interventions and predictions; their value depends on the response and application conditions. Research can improve a procedure and many predictions while leaving other judgments less accurate or more certain than the evidence warrants.

## Discussion

Autonomous research shapes understanding partly by shaping the evidence from which it develops. The purification trajectories make this relationship concrete. One researcher distinguishes a successful purity prediction from the criterion for continuing a procedure and changes its next experiment accordingly. Another acts on an explanation in a way that depletes stock and removes later opportunities to test temperature. These are different consequences of judgments entering research organization. Evidence acquisition depends on the evolving inquiry, including its objectives, interpretations, resources and operation semantics.

The reaction and electrochemical tests show how inquiry develops useful scientific relationships. An observation motivates a prospective comparison; its result supports an operational expectation or changes an apparently categorical relation into a qualified advantage. Matched-world forecasts extend this account beyond selected trajectories: they preserve response differences in both direction and magnitude across the fixed world pairs. Together, these results connect the formation of local relationships with their expression in subsequent judgments.

Research access improves crystallization forecasts, yet alternative uses of the same observations yield response-dependent advantages. Source means predict purity and size more accurately, whereas researchers predict recovery better in fourteen of fifteen sessions. Improved fines point predictions coexist with deteriorating interval scores. Alongside the equilibrium regional reversal, these findings show that progress in one scientific judgment need not extend to another. Evaluating understanding requires examining the relationships formed, their evidential scope and their use under new conditions [@shmueli2010explain; @gneiting2007scoring; @ovadia2019uncertainty].

The comparisons estimate effects of complete goal, information or research-opportunity conditions; process histories locate the judgments and actions through which particular inquiries unfold. These complementary designs connect evidence formation with evidence use. The separate fixed-recipe quench comparison establishes physical consequences of an operation under matched recipes (Extended Data Fig. 4); identifying how an explanation itself changes a researcher’s choices would require manipulating that explanation during continued inquiry.

The repeated matrices concern one model configuration in fixed programmable worlds. Fresh sessions describe variation within those conditions; broader model and chemical generality remain open. Notes record public judgments, and missing intermediate statements limit temporal inference. Core histories were selected retrospectively after full reading, and the seven named cases were checked internally against quotations, operations and later use. Independent domain review remains outstanding. Matched-world forecasts and same-history prediction references measure predictive behavior, rather than uniquely identifying mechanisms or causally decomposing the research effect.

ChemWorld makes the relationship between exploration and understanding an empirical research object. Autonomous researchers shape their evidence through the experiments they choose, and use that evidence to establish, revise and apply scientific relationships. Following this development reveals both substantive advances and where their value changes across responses, conditions and uncertainty judgments. Experimental autonomy can thereby be studied through the knowledge it produces and the further inquiry that knowledge enables.
