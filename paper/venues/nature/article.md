**Autonomous laboratories expand the ability of machines to choose and perform experiments [@tom2024selfdriving; @boiko2023autonomous]. Their scientific value also depends on how self-selected experimental experience becomes a basis for further judgment. Here we use programmable chemical worlds to connect autonomous experimental choices, contemporaneous scientific records and predictions under new interventions. Cross-system studies establish that operating success and predictive accuracy can separate. Repeated equilibrium and crystallization studies then reveal how agents propose discriminating tests, revise expectations and establish bounded working relationships. These achievements can coexist with departures in how the resulting evidence is subsequently used. A local test that weakens one explanation can support an extrapolation that the experiments did not distinguish; a successful process and an explicit account of high purity can coexist with substantially lower purity forecasts. In other cases, rejected operations enter later accounts as completed negative tests. We distinguish these processes from correctly bounded conclusions and from successful extrapolation without direct sampling. The findings locate scientific judgment within the research that supplies its evidence. Experimental autonomy enables the formation of useful relationships, while the evidential scope of those relationships must be examined separately from their subsequent use.**

Scientific research changes what a researcher can justifiably claim about a system. Experiments reveal outcomes, but selecting an explanation, deciding its scope and using it to guide another intervention require further judgments. As machines acquire greater experimental autonomy, these judgments increasingly concern evidence that they have chosen to obtain themselves. The resulting research history determines which possibilities have been tested and which remain open.

Robot scientists and self-driving laboratories have demonstrated hypothesis testing, materials discovery and reaction optimization [@king2009automation; @burger2020mobile; @macleod2020selfdriving; @szymanski2023alab]. Language-model agents extend the connection between scientific instructions, tools and experimental operations [@bran2024augmenting; @dai2024mobile]. Yet an effective procedure does not by itself identify what the researcher has learned, and a correct prediction does not uniquely identify the reasoning or evidence supporting it [@elmachtoub2022spo; @shmueli2010explain]. Understanding autonomous science therefore requires following judgments through the research process in which they acquire and exercise evidential authority.

ChemWorld provides a setting for this investigation. Agents organize experiments on evolving virtual samples, while an evaluator independently controls compatible process laws and public information. We connect broad operating and predictive comparisons to repeated studies with contemporaneous notebooks. These records expose useful revisions, unresolved alternatives and specific departures between what was tested and what was subsequently treated as established. The question is how experimental experience becomes a scientific relationship, and what happens when that relationship is used beyond the evidence that produced it.

## Research as a traceable experimental process

ChemWorld combines materials, operations and instruments into programmable chemical systems with persistent state. Researchers choose quantities, operation sequences, measurements, repetitions and subsequent experiments; operations can alter samples irreversibly, consume resources or be rejected before changing physical state. Evaluators retain the executed transitions and underlying responses. These are controlled virtual chemical worlds rather than claims about experimentally validated substances in a physical laboratory.

This arrangement extends the evidence available from knowledge tests and supplied-data discovery tasks [@mirza2025chembench; @majumder2025discoverybench]. Interactive discovery environments already connect action and observation [@wang2022scienceworld; @jansen2024discoveryworld; @gandhi2025boxinggym; @chen2025physgym]. Here, composable chemical operations make the same researcher's choice of evidence, evolving public account and use of acquired relationships jointly inspectable (Fig. 1).

The retained broad study comprises 240 campaigns across six system families and 60 additional equilibrium campaigns from four other model configurations. It supplies the operating and predictive context. In electrochemistry, optimization delivers the higher-scoring recommendation in 26 of 30 matched comparisons, but its predictions are better in 13 and worse in 13 of those comparisons. Research resources and prior information also have positive effects in particular settings: increasing the partitioning budget reduces organic-phase-fraction prediction error from 0.08291 to 0.02379, and aligned reaction priors improve prediction in four of five worlds under each of two research goals. These are comparisons between independent campaigns, not within-researcher learning curves.

To examine judgment formation during experimentation, a separate cohort covers equilibrium and crystallization, each in five worlds, three information conditions and three fresh sessions per condition. Agents receive no extra instance information, an aligned partial prior, or a specified mismatched prior. All sessions use the same Sol-medium model configuration and a twelve-batch research allowance. The cohort contains 90 terminal sessions, 84 complete research-and-assessment chains and 284 notebook revisions. Failures remain in the planned denominator. After experimentation, the agent seals its scientific account, predicts fixed new interventions without further laboratory access, and then reflects without seeing prediction truth. World parameters and observation mappings are fixed across repeats; model sampling randomness is not controlled.

## Experiments establish and revise local relationships

The notebooks show researchers making testable commitments and revising them after outcomes. In one equilibrium study with a mismatched prior, a nearly flat reagent-loading response motivates an alternative: another accessible component might be the active coordinate. Before the final two experiments, the agent predicts that this component should produce a substantial response if the explanation is correct. It then measures a component-only sample and a large component addition to a small reagent charge. Both remain near the observed plateau. The subsequent note withdraws support for the tested mapping while retaining several explanations for the plateau (Fig. 2a). This is an executed test of a publicly stated conjecture, although it does not uniquely identify the hidden mechanism.

A crystallization study provides a more direct prospective test. After a concentrated recipe fails the fines requirement, the researcher proposes increasing seed mass to rescue particle quality. The two relevant batches share the principal preparation and cooling operations, with successful seed additions of 0.02 and 0.05 g. Both return a fines fraction of 1.0. The next note explicitly withdraws the rescue hypothesis for the tested condition (Fig. 2b). The agent ultimately recommends another recipe that passes independent retesting. A separate study withdraws an expected monotonic loading effect after a lower charge worsens fines. Scientific revision is therefore observable even when the proposed route to better operation fails.

Experimental relationships can also become working guides for further inquiry. In another crystallization session, successes and failures motivate a volume-window search. A note proposes a test at 32.5 ml after obtaining a feasible process at 35 ml. The new test retains the other principal operations and remains feasible; the following experiment repeats 35 ml, and the recommendation is independently retested (Fig. 2c). This sequence establishes usable recipes and motivates a subsequent experiment. Earlier failures at lower volumes also change seed or thermal history, so they do not independently identify a concentration threshold.

These records include appropriate limits as well as revisions. Agents often distinguish an observed recipe trend from an isolated temperature coefficient, a supported region from a global solvent ranking, or an unresolved small difference from a reproducible improvement. Such limitations are themselves part of the scientific judgment being formed. They provide the context for asking whether the same limitations remain effective when the researcher later makes a different kind of decision.

## Local evidence supports divergent extrapolations

The equilibrium mapping study also shows how a valid local revision can be followed by an unsupported extension. Its final account retains buffering, saturation and unidentified couplings as competing explanations. For an extreme dilute query, however, the agent explicitly invokes the component-only result to justify shrinking its forecast towards the experimental baseline. It predicts a dissociation fraction of 0.092 with an 80% interval of 0.043–0.155; the reference mean is 0.51730 (Fig. 3a). The local component test weakens one accessible-coordinate explanation but does not establish whether the plateau persists under extreme dilution. In retrospective review, the agent acknowledges that the interval insufficiently represents the unresolved alternatives.

This transition is more specific than a failure to notice uncertainty. The uncertainty appears in the account, while the numerical forecast assigns greater practical weight to one continuation of the observed relationship. The public rationale connects the earlier observation to that choice; it does not establish a causal effect of writing the note or reveal private reasoning.

The repeated cohort places this example in a broader outcome distribution. Aligned priors reduce five-world mean error on the other nine queries from 0.00907 to 0.00512, but increase error on the three most dilute queries from 0.09880 to 0.13205 (Fig. 3b). The local improvement occurs in all five worlds; the dilute deterioration occurs in four. Empirical coverage of nominal 80% intervals in the dilute group falls from 73.85% to 19.48%. The mismatched-prior arm has corresponding errors of 0.00531 and 0.14848, with 14.89% dilute coverage. It was collected in the later extension period and is not a concurrently randomized comparison with the earlier arms. The earlier study's approximately 8.7-fold prior-benefit reversal is consequently not a stable effect magnitude reproduced here; the present aligned-to-opaque ratio is about 1.34.

All 45 equilibrium sessions remain above 1 mM in their positively loaded source assays. Nevertheless, similar plateau observations support different forecast constructions. In repeated studies of the same world, opaque sessions produce either a near-plateau estimate or a much larger, accurate dissociation forecast, whereas the aligned sessions consistently remain closer to the plateau (Fig. 3c). Another opaque session introduces an effective soluble-concentration cap when answering the prediction questions, obtains an accurate dilute forecast and subsequently acknowledges that the mechanism was only one candidate in its earlier account.

Direct sampling is therefore not a requirement for legitimate or successful extrapolation. In the separate five-model study, Sol/Opaque extrapolates accurately without reaching the dilute region, while an Astra campaign first observes a large dissociation increase at low concentration and then predicts a new dilute condition accurately. These paths preserve the distinction between a correct forecast, evidence acquired during research and experimental discrimination among explanations. Prediction scores alone collapse these differences.

## Useful processes can coexist with unreliable transfer

Crystallization exposes a complementary issue: a researcher may correctly recognize a stable response but substantially alter it when predicting new conditions. In three sessions under the same world and mismatched information, each agent finds a recipe that passes recommendation retesting. Each also identifies high crystal purity in its scientific account. Mean source purities are 99.09%, 98.18% and 99.09%, yet mean forecasts for the new recipes are 86.92%, 86.33% and 89.92%; their common reference mean is 98.45% (Fig. 4a).

The first session offers a particularly clear within-research contrast. Its source account describes both substantial impurity rejection and a bounded feasible recipe region. In forecasting, it introduces adjustments for solvent, impurity occlusion, concentration and thermal history. Its recovery mean absolute error is 0.04371, but purity error is 0.11529, and none of its twelve purity intervals covers the reference. New recipes legitimately require judgment about changed conditions; the evidence does not imply that repeating the source mean is always correct. Here, the adjustments depart substantially from the actual world despite an accurate public description of the source purity level.

Across the 45 crystallization sessions, recommendations meet both quality and recovery requirements in 9 of 15 opaque, 7 of 15 aligned and 14 of 15 mismatched-prior sessions. Their five-world mean purity errors are 0.01537, 0.04120 and 0.05964, respectively (Fig. 4b,c). The mismatched arm's high operating success cannot be interpreted as a benefit of false knowledge: it swaps only two solvent descriptions, leaves the frequently used solvent S2 unchanged and was collected later. Its lower recovery error and higher purity error instead illustrate why response-specific judgments must remain visible. Correct prediction of the designated thermal fines contrast also varies, occurring in 7, 9 and 6 of 15 sessions.

These results extend the earlier crystallization finding: 335 of 360 purity forecasts are below reference, although source and reference purity are both close to 98.5%. Contemporaneous records now show that useful process knowledge and explicit recognition of high purity can coexist with inaccurate transfer in the same researcher. They do not identify forgetting, context loss or a common mechanism as the cause.

## The evidential status of a judgment can itself be mistaken

Some departures occur before transfer. One agent proposes testing about 2 g of cumulative seed, interpreting the dose limit as a per-operation rather than a per-batch constraint. It requests forty additions of 0.05 g, but only the first succeeds; the other thirty-nine are rejected. A later note nonetheless states that raising seed from 0.05 to about 2 g did not reduce fines and excludes seed mass as the limiting control. The final scientific account and retrospective review retain that conclusion (Extended Data Fig. 1).

The claimed dose comparison was never completed. Its failure cannot provide negative experimental evidence about a large dose. This trajectory locates the conversion of an intended test into an apparently established exclusion. Another agent correctly records repeated seed-addition rejection and limits subsequent dosing accordingly. Execution failures therefore do not inevitably become scientific misstatements. The first case concerns interpretation of execution state; it does not by itself explain other forms of generalization error or prove that the mistaken note caused subsequent actions.

Similarly, recognizing uncertainty need not settle an operating choice. One researcher states a preference for fines margin but ultimately selects the highest observed feasible recovery near the boundary, which fails noiseless retesting. Another sacrifices some observed recovery for margin and passes (Extended Data Fig. 2). The task rewards observed feasible recovery, so a boundary choice is not automatically irrational. Its relation to the researcher's own stated decision rule remains informative.

## Scientific judgment as an outcome of research

The findings give empirical content to a central promise of autonomous science: that experimental freedom can support the development of scientific understanding. Agents formulate tests, revise expectations and establish bounded working relationships. Following those same relationships into later accounts and predictions reveals distinct departures: an unresolved continuation acquires numerical authority, a recognized stable response is insufficiently preserved, or an unexecuted test becomes a stated exclusion. These outcomes can coexist within a research history.

The common object is the relation between evidence and its use, rather than a single failure mechanism. Experimental design and strong inference provide principles for choosing informative tests [@lindley1956information; @chaloner1995design; @platt1964inference], but the present results also concern how completed tests are represented and how uncertainty enters subsequent judgment [@gneiting2007scoring; @ovadia2019uncertainty]. A matched quench intervention independently verifies an operation's total effect in retained virtual recipes (Extended Data Fig. 3); it does not establish why an agent adopted its explanation. Explaining those transitions causally requires targeted comparisons, not an assumption that all errors maintain themselves through selective sampling.

The process cohort uses one model configuration, repeated fixed worlds and a notebook-enabled workflow. Case interpretation is retrospective and by a single analyst; public statements are not direct measurements of internal beliefs. The earlier cross-model evidence concerns research paths and endpoints, not a completed cross-model analysis of contemporaneous judgment formation. These limits define the scope of the result: autonomous research can produce experimentally supported local knowledge while the evidence claimed for a relationship, its stated applicability and its use in a new judgment remain separable. Making those transitions observable provides a basis for studying how experimental autonomy becomes scientific understanding.
