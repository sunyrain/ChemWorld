**Autonomous science promises to turn experimental agency into advances in scientific understanding. Yet how knowledge forms through an agent's own inquiry remains poorly understood. Here we introduce ChemWorld, programmable chemical worlds combining autonomous experimentation with independent control of scientific laws and supplied information. Across six systems, repeated investigations and controlled comparisons examine how goals and prior knowledge shape exploration, and how emerging judgments guide its continuation. Agents turn expectations into experimental comparisons, establish or revise useful relationships, and use them in further investigation and prediction. Research can improve predictions while leaving the application of local knowledge to other responses and conditions unresolved. ChemWorld makes knowledge formation accessible within the research that produces it, providing an experimental basis for studying how exploration generates knowledge and developing knowledge reshapes exploration.**

An autonomous researcher shapes the experience from which its scientific judgments develop. By choosing experiments and measurements, it determines which phenomena become visible, which possibilities are tested and which results guide the next investigation. As AI scientists and self-driving laboratories undertake increasingly complex experimental cycles [@tom2024selfdriving; @boiko2023autonomous], this organization of inquiry becomes part of the scientific activity delegated to machines.

Robot scientists and autonomous laboratories have demonstrated hypothesis testing, materials discovery and reaction optimization [@king2009automation; @burger2020mobile; @macleod2020selfdriving; @szymanski2023alab]. Language-model agents extend the connection between scientific instructions, tools and laboratory operations [@bran2024augmenting; @dai2024mobile]. These advances make a broader question experimentally accessible: how does scientific knowledge form within autonomous research, and how does developing knowledge reshape further inquiry? An effective procedure and an accurate prediction establish important outcomes, but neither alone reveals how a judgment arose, what supports it or how it guides later work [@elmachtoub2022spo; @shmueli2010explain].

Scientific knowledge can organize observations, introduce a new expectation or change which comparison is worth pursuing. In autonomous inquiry, these roles are intertwined with evidence acquisition. Goals and prior knowledge guide experimental choices; the resulting observations may support a relationship, challenge an expectation or motivate a new explanation. Drawing on existing principles and emerging patterns, the researcher decides what to compare next [@platt1964inference; @lindley1956information; @chaloner1995design]. Those choices shape both the evidence and the opportunities still available. Understanding knowledge formation therefore requires following how judgments develop within research and how they participate in its continuation.

We investigate this process in ChemWorld. In these programmable chemical worlds, agents choose materials, operations and measurements while an evaluator independently controls scientific laws and supplied information. Repeated investigations across six systems trace experimental choices, acquired evidence and evolving judgments. Controlled comparisons examine the consequences of research goals, prior information and access to experimentation. Together, these studies follow the organization of inquiry, the formation and revision of scientific relationships, and their use in further exploration and prediction.

## Studying autonomous scientific inquiry

ChemWorld combines materials, operations and instruments into stateful chemical worlds governed by thermodynamic, kinetic and coupled process relationships. Reaction, thermal processing, equilibrium, separation and crystallization components connect into multistage procedures. Sample states persist: an upstream reaction or thermal history can change a later separation or measurement. Agents choose quantities, processing sequences, measurements and subsequent experiments within fixed resource allowances. An evaluator independently controls world parameters and public information, records committed operations and obtains withheld reference outcomes (Fig. 1).

This arrangement connects the selection of evidence to its interpretation. Knowledge tests and supplied-data tasks assess important scientific abilities [@mirza2025chembench; @majumder2025discoverybench], and interactive discovery environments already connect action to observation [@wang2022scienceworld; @jansen2024discoveryworld; @gandhi2025boxinggym; @chen2025physgym]. Recent agent studies also examine the revision of chemical structure hypotheses and the construction of explanatory representations [@han2026molquest; @li2026ultradiscovery]. ChemWorld follows inquiry through composable chemical processes. Public records identify what the agent saw and proposed; execution records establish which interventions changed the sample; independent world responses test later predictions. A requested operation can fail without producing the intended experiment, and a relationship supported in one setting may remain unresolved in another.

The design covers 450 planned investigations in four complementary matrices. The objective–information matrix contains ninety electrochemical and ninety reaction-processing sessions. The process matrix contains forty-five sessions each in partitioning, purification, equilibrium and reaction-coupled crystallization. The opportunity matrix compares research with no research in equilibrium and crystallization, thirty sessions per system. Thirty further investigations use matched aqueous worlds (Methods; Extended Data Fig. 2).

Opaque information supplies the task and operating contract without an additional instance dossier; Aligned supplies correct but incomplete information; MisIndexed supplies a specified mismatch. Discovery commissions emphasize investigating relationships, whereas optimization commissions emphasize developing an effective procedure. Worlds, interfaces and allowances are held to each fixed block's design, with three fresh sessions per cell and no controlled model sampling seed. These contrasts specify the conditions of research; the agents organize the actual paths.

After experimentation, researchers seal a scientific account, predict twelve new interventions without laboratory access and reflect without reference-truth feedback. We connect contemporaneous judgments to operations, observations, revisions and later use. All 300 public histories in the new experimental block were read, including incomplete histories and unsuccessful tests; illustrative trajectories were then selected for their explanatory content. Study labels connect the cases below to their full records and critical times in Supplementary Information. The 450-session design establishes coverage; condition comparisons estimate outcomes; selected histories establish particular transitions rather than their prevalence. Throughout, we distinguish world truth, the evidence available at a given time and the judgment adopted.

## The organization of scientific inquiry

Organizing inquiry involves choosing which conditions to compare, which procedures to repeat and when to move to a new question. These choices use limited materials and experimental opportunities while determining which observations become available. We first compare the outcomes of different goals and information, then follow how judgments enter the choices made during individual studies.

In the repeated electrochemical matrix, optimization rather than discovery increases world-equal mean retested recommendation score from 0.4813 to 0.5852 and product yield from 0.1310 to 0.4225. Prediction errors nevertheless increase for score (0.1539 to 0.1829), yield (0.0665 to 0.0781) and Faradaic efficiency (0.1677 to 0.2218), each in all five worlds (Fig. 2a,b). Developing a more effective procedure does not ensure better judgment of new interventions.

Information has similarly differentiated consequences. Under reaction optimization, Aligned rather than Opaque information improves recommendation score from 0.2735 to 0.4429 and yield from 0.5502 to 0.7116. Risk prediction error falls from 0.2389 to 0.1226, whereas conversion error rises from 0.0355 to 0.1595; all four directions occur in all five worlds (Fig. 2c,d). Thus the same information package can support more effective interventions and more accurate risk judgments while leaving conversion less accurately predicted.

Continuous records show how a result enters the next experimental choice. In purification study A, the researcher distinguishes the response it expects from the result required to continue a procedure (Fig. 3a). A no-wash procedure gives purity 0.8140 and recovery 0.0479. Increasing the organic-phase amount raises recovery but leaves purity at 0.7886, below the required 0.80. Before batch 11, the researcher predicts that a small 0.005 L wash will cross the purity threshold. It also sets a prospective action criterion: “if it passes with recovery above 0.0479, replicate it in batch 12.” This separates an expected scientific response from the condition for pursuing the procedure.

Batch 11 reaches purity 0.8013 but recovery only 0.0441. The purity expectation is met, while the continuation criterion is not. Batch 12 therefore repeats the earlier no-wash procedure, yielding purity 0.8149 and recovery 0.0368. The final account recognizes repeated qualification alongside variation in recovery, and later predictions retain the observed purity–recovery trade-off. The two responses play different roles in the next decision: the wash meets the expected purity threshold, but its recovery determines whether the researcher pursues it.

Purification study B shows how a judgment changes the evidence still obtainable. After four low-recovery batches, the researcher proposes that a feed component was omitted and states that it will add that component in the remaining experiments. The next four batches implement both addition commands, which consume the same shared stock. Stock depletion then prevents the planned temperature experiments. In the final note, the researcher correctly excludes those rejected or blank operations from temperature-response evidence. Acting on an interpretation of the feed thus changes material use and the scope of subsequent inquiry.

## Scientific understanding in the making

Research can establish empirical relationships, refine explanations and resolve specific uncertainties about an intervention. The following histories show these forms of progress taking shape through comparisons that the researchers themselves propose and carry out.

Reaction study A follows an expectation from observation through a new experiment to later use (Fig. 3b). At 390 K and 1,800 s heating, direct termination gives conversion 0.41718. A later batch with the same heating followed by an unquenched 1,800 s wait reaches 0.61847 despite cooling by about 44 K. The researcher treats this as evidence that stopping external heating has not ended the chemical change. Before the final batch, it predicts that quenching before the same wait should preserve conversion near 0.42, rather than the approximately 0.62 unquenched-wait result.

The final batch implements that order and gives conversion 0.42724. The subsequent note retains a local relationship between the quench operation and suppression of further conversion. This evidence establishes which part of the processing history remains chemically consequential. Later forecasts use the relationship in new procedures, while transfer to unobserved solvent and loading conditions remains less accurate. The comparison supports the operational expectation under the tested sequence; its differing cooling histories leave thermal and other effects unresolved (Methods).

Electrochemical study B, commissioned to optimize, makes the scope of polarity dependence an experimental question (Fig. 3c). After ten batches of material and duration investigation, the researcher records that positive-polarity specificity has not undergone a matched reverse-sign test. It reserves that comparison and an exact repeat, writing: “If negative potential retains high yield, the assumed polarity specificity is rejected”. Batch 11 at −1.0 V retains yield 0.384; the positive recipe and its final repeat give approximately 0.911 and 0.905. The terminal account changes from apparent polarity specificity to polarity advantage, and subsequent forecasts retain reverse-polarity production. The revised relationship remains useful while becoming more precise: progress here consists in replacing apparent exclusivity with a supported advantage.

The three histories connect judgment to research in different ways. Purification applies an existing continuation criterion to a new observation; reaction processing tests a local operational expectation; electrochemistry revises the content of a scientific relationship. In each, the scientific judgment has a specific consequence for the comparison undertaken or the subsequent use of its result.

Electrochemical study A provides a contrasting path from a local result to subsequent judgment. It begins with a matched sign comparison: negative potential produces a response where the tested positive setting is effectively inactive. Subsequent batches refine materials and duration on the negative side. The terminal note explicitly limits the sign result to the tested recipe. Yet later numerical forecasts assign near-background responses to untested positive potentials within the examined magnitude range, with intervals missing the references. A stated boundary and its application in new judgments can therefore diverge.

The matched-world matrix examines how different underlying chemistry enters the evidence and subsequent judgments (Fig. 4). Five pairs of aqueous worlds share public framing and selected parameters but differ in coupling to an additional neutral ion pair.

In matched-world study A, the researcher states that a sharp precipitation onset with a buffered dissolved response would support a solubility-limited interpretation, while smooth changes would also admit an empirical partition description. After six assays it proposes denser concentration sampling, a central repeat and increased loading; the next six batches implement those choices. The twelve actual observations provide the evidence behind the adopted relation (Fig. 4d; Supplementary Information). Its final account retains a smooth monotone relationship, and its forecast explicitly uses that relationship because no sharp threshold was resolved. Matched-world study B predicts a response to diluting a concentrated sample from 0.75 to 0.375 M and then performs the dilution. Normalized pH rises from 0.08045 to 0.10803. The researcher supports approximate reversibility, while the nearest separately prepared comparator is 0.4 M and the precipitation readings come from different instruments. These records connect stated alternatives, chosen comparisons and the relationships subsequently used.

Across the matrix, replaying chosen action sequences in their paired worlds shows that every one of thirty sessions acquires at least one observation differing by three assay standard deviations in two response channels. The chosen experiments therefore encounter physically distinguishable responses.

Final forecasts also reflect these world differences. A post hoc analysis compares B-minus-A differences in the three-session forecast means with differences in the five-observation reference means at twelve common queries (Fig. 4a–c). Among nonzero reference differences, directions agree in 54/57 pH, 48/57 dissociation and 39/39 precipitation contrasts. The remaining 3, 3 and 21 reference differences are zero. All 180 contrasts remain in the analysis; in the descriptive subset reaching three assay standard deviations, directions agree in 48/48, 10/10 and 27/27 cases, respectively.

The forecasts capture difference magnitude as well as direction (Fig. 4e). Mean absolute contrast errors are 0.002256 for normalized pH, 0.002945 for dissociation and 0.002368 for precipitation. Giving identical forecasts in the two worlds would instead yield errors of 0.026007, 0.007239 and 0.024840. Forecast errors are thus 91.3%, 59.3% and 90.5% lower than this zero-difference reference, with an advantage in all five world pairs for each response. These are properties of the final three-session mean forecasts. The block has no no-research arm and supplies close but nonidentical starter observations, so it does not isolate a research-induced gain.

Complementary histories show that learning can also proceed through failed expectations (Extended Data Fig. 8). Stronger mixing defeats a provisional partitioning plateau; a fourth crystallization thermal cycle fails to extend the improvement from the third; further dilution removes recoverable particles and narrows an apparent operating window. These tests contribute through the judgments they revise, even when they do not improve the final procedure. The effects of simultaneously changed factors remain unresolved where the experiments did not separate them.

Interpreting an experimental history also requires determining which operations actually occurred. Some researchers treat rolled-back operations as completed tests, while others explicitly exclude them (Extended Data Fig. 6). In the focal heating case, the public return combines success labels with a rollback message and zero time and temperature changes. The resulting account incorporates this ambiguous execution history into later judgments. These cases expose how interface signals and their interpretation jointly shape the evidence a researcher treats as available.

Across these histories, understanding develops through changes in what is compared, which relationship is supported and how it enters another decision. Historical five-model equilibrium campaigns show a further distinction: accurate predictions can follow either direct exploration of a dilute region or extrapolation from local observations (Extended Data Fig. 7). Similar endpoints can therefore arise through different evidence histories.

## Scientific understanding in use

The preceding histories show judgments guiding experiments while research is still under way. We now examine their use after experimentation, when agents face new questions without further laboratory access. The opportunity matrix compares the resulting predictions with those of agents given no research opportunity. In each of two systems, five fixed worlds and three fresh sessions per arm compare twelve-batch research with no source experimentation under common Opaque information and assessment. The contrast estimates the complete opportunity to choose experiments, receive feedback and record judgments.

The crystallization comparison establishes substantial benefits (Fig. 5a). All thirty sessions provide valid forecasts. Relative to no research, world-equal mean absolute error falls from 0.13853 to 0.01661 for purity and from 0.23619 to 0.07641 for size index. Recovery error falls from 0.13532 to 0.11579 and fines error from 0.37683 to 0.30110. For purity, nominal 80% interval coverage rises from 0.46667 to 0.98333 while width contracts from 0.44744 to 0.09743. Research experience produces more accurate predictions and narrower intervals that cover more reference outcomes.

A second comparison asks what predictions can be obtained from these same research histories (Fig. 5b). For the fifteen Research sessions, a source-observation mean has purity error 0.00450 and size error 0.01786, below the agent's 0.01661 and 0.07641 in all fifteen sessions. The pattern reverses for recovery: the agent's 0.11579 improves on the reference's 0.18828, with an advantage in fourteen sessions. This alternative prediction rule uses only each session's public source observations, receives no target outcomes for fitting and is scored on the same targets. A constant reference captures stable responses effectively, whereas the agent makes better recovery predictions. Research is valuable, and how its observations are used determines further response-specific advantages. The earlier corrected-crystallization cohort provides a separate supporting comparison (Extended Data Fig. 3).

The same cohort distinguishes better point prediction from better uncertainty estimates (Fig. 5c). For fines, coverage falls from 0.42778 to 0.35556 as interval width contracts from 0.68500 to 0.38450. The interval score, which penalizes width and missed outcomes, worsens from 1.4794 to 1.8047 in the world-equal mean, with worsening in every world. Research reduces average fines prediction error, but the resulting intervals express more certainty than their performance warrants. The benefit and the limitation concern the same response in the same comparison.

The equilibrium opportunity comparison reveals a regional boundary (Fig. 5d). Among fourteen valid Research forecasts and fifteen NoResearch forecasts, overall normalized macro error falls from 0.132086 to 0.035632, with improvement in all five worlds. Error on the three prospectively specified extreme-dilution queries instead rises from 0.061467 to 0.103677, worsening in four worlds; the remaining nine improve from 0.155625 to 0.012950. Allowing the missing Research forecast any error in its legal range does not reverse either aggregate direction (Methods). Research improves most predictions while judgments in another region become less reliable.

Equilibrium study A makes this transition continuous in time (Extended Data Fig. 5). An initial loading-dependent expectation gives way to an observed local plateau. The researcher changes addition commands while considering alternative explanations; both commands add the same species, so the evidence establishes a local regularity without resolving the proposed chemical-identity contrast. The terminal account retains several explanations. In the later extreme-dilution forecast, it nevertheless favours a fixed acidic background and predicts dissociation 0.085, with an 80% interval of [0.035, 0.350], against reference mean 0.716032. The relationship acquired through research is useful locally; the subsequent judgment gives that experience weight in a region the experiments left unresolved.

Using acquired knowledge leaves further questions: which observations should constrain judgment, which relationships can be extended, and what uncertainty remains. Research advances depend on these subsequent uses. Further comparisons appear in Supplementary Information and Extended Data Fig. 1.

## Discussion

Scientific understanding develops through investigations that agents help organize. In purification, a continuation criterion turns a new observation into a choice of procedure. In reaction processing, a judgment about continued chemical change motivates a prospective test. In electrochemistry, an untested boundary becomes a comparison that revises a relationship. The matched-world history connects explanatory alternatives to further sampling and later forecasts. These records show knowledge taking part in the conduct of research: judgments organize the comparisons from which further understanding can emerge.

The scientific gains have specific content. A quench expectation acquires experimental support; polarity specificity becomes a qualified advantage; a sampled concentration response becomes a relationship used in prediction. Such progress can consist in establishing, revising or delimiting a relationship. It need not strengthen a claim, and a useful empirical relationship can develop while its underlying mechanism remains open. The matched-world comparisons add evidence that final predictions reflect physical differences across worlds, while historical campaigns show that similar accuracy can arise through different evidence histories.

The use of acquired knowledge raises further questions. Crystallization research improves predictions, yet a simple average of the same observations predicts stable properties more accurately, whereas agents predict recovery better. In equilibrium, a locally supported pattern is extended into an unresolved region. Point accuracy and uncertainty quality can also change in different directions. Distinguishing a judgment's correctness, its evidential support and its use helps locate these developments within a research history [@shmueli2010explain; @gneiting2007scoring; @ovadia2019uncertainty]. It allows a study's local achievements and the open questions in their application to be considered together.

Abduction highlights the creative role of proposing explanations worth investigating [@douven2025peirce]. Their significance lies in the questions and comparisons they open, and in how deduction and empirical testing develop or revise them. Einstein's 1905 reconsideration of electromagnetic descriptions illustrates how changing explanatory premises can reshape the understanding of time and motion [@einstein1905electrodynamics]. Autonomous inquiry also involves empirical generalization, inference from established relationships and decisions about further experimentation. ChemWorld places these activities within the continuing investigations in which they acquire scientific consequences.

The controlled comparisons and continuous records examine complementary parts of this development. Condition contrasts estimate the effects of goals, information or research opportunity; histories locate the judgments and operations through which particular inquiries unfold. The fixed-recipe quench comparison separately establishes physical effects of an operation (Extended Data Fig. 4). Intervening on a candidate explanation during continued inquiry would allow its effect on subsequent choices to be distinguished from these broader research effects.

The repeated matrices concern one model configuration in fixed programmable worlds. Fresh sessions describe variation within those conditions; broader model and chemical generality remain open. Notes record public judgments, and missing intermediate statements limit temporal inference. Core histories were selected retrospectively after full reading, and the seven named cases were checked internally against quotations, operations and later use. Independent domain review remains outstanding. Matched-world forecasts and same-history prediction references measure predictive behavior, rather than uniquely identifying mechanisms or causally decomposing the research effect.

ChemWorld establishes an experimental basis for studying how scientific knowledge develops within autonomous inquiry. It brings research organization, reasoning from observations and the later use of knowledge into a common empirical setting. Scientific understanding can thus be studied as something formed and transformed through research: what an agent comes to know changes the exploration it undertakes, and that exploration changes what it can come to know.
