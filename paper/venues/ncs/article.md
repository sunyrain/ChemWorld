abstract: |
  Autonomous scientific research requires learning relationships and judging their applicability under new interventions. Evaluating this ability is difficult when researchers select their own evidence and underlying mechanisms are unknown. We introduce ChemWorld, a programmable chemical environment coupling autonomous experimentation to evaluator control over process laws and supplied information. Across 240 campaigns by one agent configuration in six system families, larger research envelopes and relevant partial information improve prediction in several settings. Yet higher recommendation scores do not consistently accompany lower prediction errors. Two complementary failures locate a remaining difficulty: aligned equilibrium forecasts extend an observed plateau into dilute conditions where the response changes, whereas crystallization forecasts unnecessarily depart from stable observed purity. These findings motivate evaluating the conditions that support an experimental relationship, alongside operating performance and uncertainty. Autonomous laboratories and language-model agents increasingly support continuous sequences of experimental decisions [@boiko2023autonomous; @bran2024augmenting; @szymanski2023alab]. Agents choose conditions, execute procedures, read measurements and adapt subsequent experiments. Their scientific promise includes turning those observations into relationships that remain useful under new interventions. Finding a productive condition is one outcome; predicting what happens after changes in materials, process history or operating range also requires judging which observed relationships remain applicable and how much uncertainty is warranted.

Studying this ability requires both autonomous evidence acquisition and controlled comparisons. Physical laboratories provide essential empirical evidence, but incompletely known mechanisms, changing material states and limited reproducibility make matched research histories difficult to obtain. Optimization benchmarks offer repeatable objectives [@felton2021summit; @hase2021olympus], while interactive chemistry and discovery environments support sequences of scientific actions [@beeler2024chemgymrl; @jansen2024discoveryworld; @gandhi2025boxinggym]. Our focus is on controlling the worlds and information in which those research processes unfold. Prior information can influence both experiment selection and interpretation, so a prediction error may reflect missing evidence, observations compatible with competing explanations, or an unsupported extrapolation. A correct prediction may likewise follow a simple regularity without identifying its mechanism.

ChemWorld combines composable process and observation models with persistent experimental state and a common laboratory interface. The agent chooses experiments and receives their consequences; the evaluator controls private process laws, material properties and supplied information. Within compatible interfaces, the evaluator can vary the physical world or hold it fixed while changing the agent's information. ChemWorld thus serves both as an environment for autonomous experimental learning and as an instrument for studying that learning. Retained operations, observations and subsequent assessments connect evidence acquisition to the original researcher's use of that evidence.

We use one autonomous researcher configuration in 240 campaigns across six chemical system families. Research objectives produce system-dependent operational and predictive outcomes. Larger research envelopes improve electrochemical and partitioning predictions, and aligned partial information helps in reaction processing, demonstrating useful responses to added resources and information. These successes coexist with two complementary failures: aligned equilibrium forecasts extend an observed plateau into dilute conditions where the response changes, whereas crystallization forecasts depart from purity that remains stable. Together, these findings make the applicability of experimental relationships a concrete evaluation target and motivate connecting experiment choice, the conditions supporting a relationship and predictive uncertainty in future autonomous systems.

# Results

## A programmable experimental instrument separates laws, evidence and information

ChemWorld constructs executable worlds from compatible process and observation components. Each world specifies persistent material states, legal operations, instruments, finite resources and private parameters. Operations can change composition, phase allocation, thermal history and remaining material; measurements expose declared quantities rather than the full simulator state. Within a compatible public interface, the evaluator can change private physics while preserving agent-visible descriptions, or hold the world fixed while changing supplied information (Fig. 1). The agent's evidence is generated by its chosen operations and measurements, linking these controlled conditions to an autonomous research history.

The research programme contains 240 campaigns, each belonging to a five-world, three-arm study block. Opaque supplies usable public contracts and anonymous identifiers. Aligned adds the tested nominal information; MisIndexed supplies its fixed mismatch or reassignment. Information can concern entity associations, effective parameters or structural relationships. All arms retain the same operational interface and general task background within a block; the contrast concerns additional instance-specific information. Aligned information remains partial, and Opaque does not remove general chemical knowledge.

Each campaign follows the original agent from experiment selection to scientific assessment. Within a finite research envelope, it chooses conditions, repetitions and measurements and adapts subsequent experiments to the observations. Where a task requires an operating recommendation, that recommendation is sealed before the scientific account (K1) and evaluated by an independent retest. The same agent then predicts withheld conditions and uncertainty intervals (Q), retaining its full research context but receiving neither laboratory access nor reference feedback. Retrospective questions (K2) follow without revealing the reference outcomes. This chronology distinguishes the acquired observations, the account stated in K1 and what becomes explicit during prediction; K2 cannot revise sealed Q.

The agent freely allocates 12 or 24 batches and a matching allowance of additional measurements. The larger condition uses an independent session with increased experimental and computational allowances. Objectives are tailored to the system: discovery and optimization are separate commissions in electrochemistry and reaction processing; partitioning and equilibrium emphasize characterization; crystallization and purification seek recovery subject to product-quality constraints. All 720 report, prediction and retrospective stages completed. Two campaigns fell short of their final-assay budgets, leaving 238 conforming chains and 3,597 of 3,600 planned source assays. Both remain in the results.

Finite-domain qualification tests reference worlds, generated compositions and operation boundaries for consistent state transitions, resource accounting and replay (Methods). These checks establish execution consistency for the tested configurations; study-specific adapters have separate checks. They do not establish wet-laboratory validity, and replay does not reconstruct unrecorded reasoning. Recorded actions, observations and resource consequences allow operating performance, prediction and uncertainty to be evaluated separately within the same research process.

## Research objectives yield different patterns of operational and predictive performance

We asked whether different research objectives produced the expected specialization in operating performance and prediction. Discovery commissions prioritized developing and testing an explanatory, predictive account; optimization commissions prioritized finding a high-scoring procedure. Both allowed autonomous experimentation. Independent campaigns were matched within world, information condition, budget and prior locus, with recommendation retests and predictions evaluated separately.

The observed outcomes did not follow a uniform division of labour (Fig. 3a,b). Optimization commissions produced higher recommendation retest scores in 26 of 30 electrochemical pairs: thirteen also had lower score-prediction MAE, whereas thirteen had higher MAE. In reaction processing, discovery commissions performed better on both endpoints in seventeen of thirty pairs. Without presuming either commission superior, the higher-retest campaign had worse predictions in fourteen electrochemical and nine reaction-processing pairs. These are descriptive orderings within five reused worlds per system, not evidence of statistical independence or a causal trade-off between the endpoints.

A selected 12-batch electrochemical pair connects these outcomes to the original research process (Fig. 3c). The discovery campaign compared materials and operating conditions before extending duration and increasing the current cap. The optimization campaign refined a promising solvent–electrolyte combination, raising its observed score from 0.675 in batch 3 to 0.749 in batch 12. Independent recommendation retests were 0.718 and 0.756 for discovery and optimization, respectively, whereas score-prediction MAEs were 0.088 and 0.229.

The optimization agent's public prediction rationale assigned low activity to several combinations because cross-pair experiments had performed poorly. For one previously tested combination under new conditions, it predicted 0.070 with an 80% interval of 0–0.240, against a reference score of 0.494 (Fig. 3d). Another material combination was accurately predicted, at 0.570 versus 0.572. All twelve forecasts are retained. The failed transfer involved several changes in conditions and does not isolate a single causal factor or establish that the acquired evidence was sufficient. It shows why a useful operating result does not, by itself, validate the applicability of an experimental judgment. ## Larger research envelopes improve generalization selectively

We next compare 12- and 24-batch research envelopes under matched worlds, information arms and commissions. The larger condition is a new autonomous session with increased experimental and computational allowances, rather than a continuation of the smaller campaign.

Increasing the research envelope from 12 to 24 batches substantially improves several predictive outcomes (Fig. 4). Electrochemical score mean absolute error (MAE) decreases from 0.17378 to 0.11222 under discovery and from 0.17811 to 0.10818 under optimization. Eleven and twelve of the respective fifteen matched campaign pairs improve. All six electrochemical responses improve in aggregate, and their mean directions persist when each world is omitted in turn.

Phase-partitioning organic-fraction MAE decreases from 0.08291 to 0.02379, with improvement in 13 of 15 pairs. Correct process-choice answers increase from 25 of 30 to 30 of 30. Nominal 90% interval coverage rises from 78.3% to 98.9% while mean width narrows from 0.25122 to 0.18553. This combination supports better prediction, although coverage above the nominal level is not perfect calibration.

Crystallization behaves differently. Net-recovery MAE decreases from 0.10433 to 0.07937, but quality-eligible recommendations remain 12 of 15 at each budget and mean retested recovery decreases from 0.42054 to 0.39939. Fines-fraction interval coverage improves from 35.6% to 43.9%, still below the nominal 80% in every campaign.

The positive electrochemical and partitioning results show that the agent can turn a larger research envelope into better forecasts. The crystallization results show that this benefit does not extend uniformly to every response or to delivery. These comparisons neither isolate the value of twelve additional observations nor rule out missing discriminating evidence. Alongside resources, supplied scientific information can also change the path from experimental choice to prediction.

## Prior information helps within some regimes but fails across others

Prior information has demonstrable benefits. In the reaction parameter study, aligned information lowers discovery macro MAE from 0.10186 to 0.05701 and optimization error from 0.13893 to 0.08384, beating Opaque in four of five worlds under each commission (Supplementary Fig. S2a,b). Across other blocks, however, there is no common ordering of the three arms. Close aggregate means in the equilibrium entity and structural studies are not evidence of equivalence.

We examine applicability by comparing the conditions sampled during research with withheld interventions, the response actually observed there and the original forecasts. This describes whether a forecast preserves or revises an experimental relationship appropriately in the tested domain. It is not a complete annotation or numerical score for every relationship the agent might have formed.

The equilibrium parameter study gives a sharper pattern. Overall macro MAE is 0.01214 for Opaque, 0.04314 for Aligned and 0.03928 for MisIndexed. Opaque outperforms both dossier conditions in all five worlds. A post hoc grouping by public recipe concentration locates the reversal (Table 1). On the nine questions other than the three most dilute, Aligned improves MAE from 0.01008 to 0.00465. On the three most dilute, its MAE instead rises from 0.01833 to 0.15862. Both directions occur in every world. The remaining nine questions include boundary conditions and should not all be called interpolation.

Aligned intervals also fail selectively: coverage is 91.3% on the other nine questions but 2.2% on the dilute three, versus 90.7% and 93.3% for Opaque. The dilute recipes have nominal concentrations near $10^{-4}$ to $10^{-5}$ M; all fifteen source campaigns remain above 0.018 M. Their measured dissociation occupies a low-response plateau, while the dilute tests require leaving it.

Four Opaque accounts explicitly use piecewise weak-acid extrapolation and the fifth permits a qualitative departure from the plateau. The equilibrium contrast concerns the entire autonomous process: information can influence both the evidence acquired and the way it is used. The observed pattern does not identify prior anchoring as its cause. It instead raises a complementary question: when a relationship does remain stable under the withheld interventions, do the agent's predictions preserve it?

## Generalization can fail by both preserving and revising the wrong relationships

Crystallization provides the complementary problem: do forecasts preserve an observed response when it remains stable under new conditions? Source and withheld purity average 98.53% and 98.51%, respectively, whereas agent predictions average 93.54% at 12 batches and 94.89% at 24 (Fig. 6a). Of 360 query forecasts, 335 are too low. The mean within-world withheld-purity span is only 2.442 percentage points. This is stability within the tested conditions, not a claim that purity is invariant under arbitrary interventions.

For each campaign, we construct a baseline that predicts the mean of its own public final assays for every withheld recipe. It acquires no additional experiments and uses no withheld outcomes. Agent recovery forecasts outperform this baseline in 26 of 30 campaigns and fines forecasts in 21, whereas size forecasts win in four and purity forecasts in none (Fig. 6b). Mean recovery MAE is 9.18 percentage points for the agent versus 17.14 for the baseline; purity MAE is 4.35 versus 0.48. These response-specific results argue against interpreting the purity error as a general inability to use data. The narrow withheld-purity range explains the baseline's strength without establishing mechanism discovery. Full error magnitudes and a nearest-neighbour reference using public recipe features are reported in Supplementary Table E1.

The same information condition can help one response and hurt another (Fig. 6c). At 12 batches, both Aligned and MisIndexed improve recovery MAE while worsening purity MAE relative to Opaque in every crystallization world, including all four both-conforming pairs for each comparison. At 24 batches, the joint direction occurs in only two and three of five worlds, respectively. The contrast is therefore neither a universal penalty for supplied information nor a budget-invariant law.

Reported uncertainty does not consistently accommodate the purity errors (Fig. 6d). At 12 batches, nominal 80% interval coverage is 98.3% for Opaque, 60.0% for Aligned and 23.3% for MisIndexed; at 24 batches it is 95.0%, 73.3% and 55.0%. Aligned and MisIndexed intervals are wider on average than Opaque intervals at both budgets, so their poorer coverage cannot be described simply as narrower reported uncertainty. Conversely, Opaque's high coverage alone does not establish calibrated or sharp intervals (Supplementary Table E2).

The equilibrium and crystallization findings expose opposite errors in using an observed relationship (Figs. 5 and 6). Aligned equilibrium forecasts extend a plateau beyond its applicable regime. Crystallization forecasts depart from a response that remains approximately stable over the tested conditions. In both cases the world's process laws remain fixed; the intervention changes the conditions under which those laws generate a response. Preservation and revision here describe forecasts relative to the source observations, not directly measured changes in internal belief.

The dilute-equilibrium coverage failure and the response-specific crystallization intervals make uncertainty part of this applicability problem. A selected crystallization account further illustrates that acknowledging limited evidence need not yield calibrated predictions (Supplementary Information, section E.2).

Figure 2 shows how one agent turned an unfavorable particle-size history into a quality-feasible operating recipe while retaining unresolved causal questions. A separate, selected W05 crystallization pair then forecasts the wrong direction for a withheld thermal intervention (Supplementary Fig. S4). Those sessions subsequently acknowledge limitations in K2 without receiving reference feedback; their proposed follow-up remains unexecuted. Useful operating adaptation and retrospective explanation therefore remain distinct from a correct sealed prediction.

Together, these patterns motivate checking not only which relationships a researcher expresses, but where its predictions treat those relationships as applicable. Successful Opaque equilibrium extrapolations, the mixed MisIndexed account and the recovery advantage over the empirical mean remain counterexamples to uniform failure. These observations do not establish a common internal cause.

# Discussion

ChemWorld connects autonomous evidence acquisition to controlled worlds and information conditions. It supports studying how a researcher moves from experimental choices and observations to procedures, explanations and forecasts. The present campaigns show useful responses to larger research envelopes and relevant partial information, alongside limits that depend on the response and intervention regime. The goal comparison does not establish an intrinsic advantage of discovery or optimization: assigned objectives, delivered procedures and predictive generalization are distinct aspects of the same research process.

The equilibrium and crystallization results illustrate why an observed success cannot support a universal prediction rule. Copying the observed mean works unusually well for crystallization purity but fails badly on dilute equilibrium tests. Mechanistic extrapolation supports successful Opaque predictions in equilibrium, yet a plausible explanation is not itself a guarantee of predictive accuracy. What matters is how experimental evidence constrains the conditions over which a proposed relationship is used, including uncertainty about alternatives compatible with the observed data.

These observations suggest a design direction for autonomous researchers: couple procedure improvement to experiments that distinguish plausible explanations, and retain the conditions under which each proposed relationship has been tested. Predictions could then be accompanied by an explicit account of which supporting conditions are preserved or changed by a new intervention. Uncertainty would need to reflect unresolved alternatives as well as measurement noise. These are proposals motivated by the results; the present study does not establish that any such strategy repairs the observed failures.

The comparisons assess complete research processes and do not isolate the causal contributions of evidence acquisition and interpretation. Larger resource envelopes and correct partial information do not establish that discriminating evidence was sufficient. Full-context prediction does not test report-compression loss, and retrospective public explanations do not reveal internal cognitive causes. The evidence is limited to one agent configuration, five reused world instances per study, correlated readouts and some post hoc analyses. The finite model domain, absence of wet-laboratory validation and protocol differences across blocks constrain broader claims. These limits apply equally to the observed successes and failures.

Evaluation should follow the original researcher from the experiments it chooses to the forecasts it seals, measuring operating performance, applicability and uncertainty separately. A useful procedure, an articulated explanation and reliable predictions under new interventions are distinct achievements. ChemWorld makes their relationship accessible to controlled study.

# Methods

## World construction and execution

A world is specified by public experimental contracts and private process parameters. Process components declare state, domains, dependencies and compatible interfaces. Operation and observation models compile these declarations into a laboratory interface. The participant can act through that interface but cannot inspect the simulator, private parameters, evaluator records or repository. Instruments may consume samples and operations consume declared material, time and action allowances. Candidate transitions are validated before commitment; rejection preserves committed physical state while retaining the declared attempt consequences. Replay concerns the environment and recorded operations, not regeneration of language-model responses.

## Platform qualification

The frozen platform qualification passed 64 task-world units, 1,786 boundary and categorical recipes, 192 invalid-action probes and 52 generated compositions. The generated cases comprise 18 previously absent topologies, eight new task-world identities using a registered topology and 26 additional coverage cases. Component and interface checks passed 32 module probes, seven interface paths and seven invalid compositions. These finite-domain checks establish internal model and execution consistency; they do not establish agreement with arbitrary wet-laboratory chemistry. Exact environment replay reconstructs executed actions and resource consequences, including rejection and rollback.

Qualification distinguishes internal physical and accounting consistency from successful research. It checks executable domains, resources, information separation, valid query references and exact replay. Invalid actions and incompatible compositions have explicit expected rejection or rollback behaviour. Subsequent study adapters have their own checks; the frozen platform qualification is not an automatic certificate for later execution code. The reported autonomous campaigns retain their development-evidence status and are not promoted to formal validation by this analysis.

## Study units and objectives

The eight blocks comprise electrochemistry with entity priors (60 campaigns), partitioning with entity priors (30), reaction processing with parameter and structural priors (60), equilibrium with entity, parameter and structural priors (15 each), crystallization with entity priors (30) and purification with entity priors (15). Every block uses five worlds and three information arms. Electrochemistry and reaction processing have discovery and optimization commissions. Electrochemistry, partitioning and crystallization have 12- and 24-batch conditions; other blocks use 12. Parameter-prior P and purification P denote different concepts.

Electrochemistry varies materials, potential, current limits and duration; its optimization objective is a public balanced-efficiency score. Reaction processing varies materials, catalyst loading and thermal histories. Partitioning studies allocation under material, volume and process changes. Equilibrium studies amount, volume, concentration, staged additions, dissociation and precipitation. Crystallization seeks recovery net of seed mass subject to purity at least 0.80, fines fraction at most 0.50 and particles present. Purification seeks original-charge recovery subject to purity at least 0.80. Recovery denominators differ and are not combined.

## Agent and information conditions

All source campaigns use GPT-5.6 Sol with medium reasoning effort in independent sessions. Within a block, arms share the physical world, operations, resource contract and withheld questions. The treatment modifies the public dossier according to the block's fixed entity, parameter or structural design. Opaque still exposes the operational interface and general task background. Aligned is a partial nominal description, such as a true effective-pKa interval in the equilibrium parameter study. MisIndexed applies the specified mismatch rather than supplying arbitrary misinformation.

Agents may choose conditions, repetitions, measurement allocations and experiment grouping, and may use the bounded public numerical tool. They are not required to choose from mechanism templates, emit a fixed equation or reflect after each batch. Batches, operations, provider responses and decisions made after feedback are different counts. Each budget permits a matching number of additional measurements and a final assay per completed batch; other resources remain separately bounded. The larger budget includes a larger execution and computation envelope. Retained early electrochemistry and partitioning assessments have an eight-call calculator allowance; later blocks disclose up to 128 attempts. Outputs are requested in English, but retained reaction and equilibrium assessment prompts include Chinese instructions requesting English responses. These differences remain part of the historical protocol.

## Sealed assessments and retests

Where required, a completed-batch recommendation is sealed before assessment and its procedure retested independently with a different observation seed in the same world. There are 165 such retests. K1 requests an unrestricted scientific account including relationships, evidence, applicability and unresolved alternatives. Q then requests point predictions and intervals for twelve fixed new-batch recipes without laboratory access or target feedback. K2 follows the sealed predictions with retrospective questions about support, limitations and discriminating experiments. The short version has three questions; reaction and equilibrium use seven themes. The bounded equilibrium parameter block adds fifteen structured supplements.

The original research context is available during every stage. Q can use the source history in addition to K1, and K2 cannot revise Q. Consequently, neither report-only knowledge transfer nor a uniform mechanism-correctness score is an endpoint. The bounded equilibrium review described below covers all fifteen campaigns in that block; complete mechanism annotation across the 240-campaign programme has not been performed.

## Outcome definitions and references

For each response, campaign MAE averages absolute point error across the twelve questions. Interval coverage measures the fraction of specified reference targets contained in the reported intervals; width is their average span. Partitioning uses nominal 90% intervals and the other blocks use 80%. Partitioning and crystallization point references use underlying pre-final-assay response values. Electrochemistry and purification use fixed seeded observations. Reaction and equilibrium point references average five observations; their coverage is evaluated against the individual observations. Macro MAE averages response-specific MAEs equally within the designated block. These targets and scales are not pooled across systems.

The crystallization mean baseline uses only the source campaign's public final assays. Nearest-neighbour prediction additionally uses public recipe features. A parser correction excludes resource-denied attempts from physical recipe features while preserving those attempts in the record. It restores two baseline evaluations; the other twenty-eight remain exactly unchanged. No agent or simulator is rerun for the correction. The equilibrium source-mean diagnostic is a separate post hoc reference. Its failure on dilute queries confirms that copying the plateau is insufficient for those questions; it is not a strong system-identification comparator.

## Descriptive comparisons and retained failures

Goal comparisons match world, information arm, budget and prior locus. Budget comparisons match world, arm and goal, comparing independent sessions. Arm comparisons match the other factors within each study. Means weight campaigns equally. Five world clusters recur across arms, goals and budgets; questions, response metrics and batches are correlated observations. We report magnitudes, paired directions, all-arm tables and selected sensitivity analyses rather than inferential tests treating queries as independent trials.

The comparison oriented by observed retest performance is a post hoc re-expression of the original goal pairs, not an additional experiment. Within each pair, we identify the campaign with the higher recommendation retest score and compare its score-prediction MAE with that of its counterpart. All pairs and exact effect magnitudes are retained; no minimum-effect threshold is applied. The electrochemical illustration is retrospectively selected and does not estimate the prevalence of its expressed prediction rule. ## Retrospective analysis of the autonomous equilibrium process

We include all fifteen effective parameter-prior equilibrium sources from their retained source and trajectory indexes: five worlds, three arms, 180 batches, 945 operations and 45 canonical assessment stages. Nominal batch concentration is the sum of reagent additions divided by the sum of solvent additions; it is not inferred dissolved concentration. Original question scores and reference observations remain unchanged. No additional model, simulator or judge calls are used.

A single author review assigns descriptive categories to the public Q explanation: piecewise weak-acid extrapolation, qualitative departure from the plateau, plateau or weak-trend extension, and mixed weak-acid/plateau explanation. Exact excerpts and prediction values accompany the classifications; they are not an independent mechanism assessment. K1 evidence is distinguished from hypotheses first explicit in Q. K2 future-experiment choices are classified separately: eleven of fifteen propose trace-load experiments after seeing Q conditions, without receiving reference feedback. These proposals were not executed and do not establish spontaneous discovery or repair during the source campaign. The exported decision-reason fields are unavailable for all 945 operations, so no contemporaneous reasoning sequence is reconstructed from them.
