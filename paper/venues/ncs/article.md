# Introduction

Self-driving laboratories extend experimental science through automated execution and adaptive experimental planning [@hase2019selfdriving; @tom2024selfdriving]. Robot scientists have generated and experimentally tested biological hypotheses [@king2009automation], while subsequent platforms have demonstrated autonomous materials search and reaction optimization [@burger2020mobile; @macleod2020selfdriving; @shields2021bayesian; @szymanski2023alab]. Mobile robots also support exploratory synthesis using complementary analytical instruments [@dai2024mobile]. Language-model agents connect scientific instructions, tools and laboratory operations [@boiko2023autonomous; @bran2024augmenting], and multi-agent research teams have designed biomolecules subsequently validated in experiments [@swanson2025virtual]. As systems gain the freedom to choose operation sequences, intermediate measurements and subsequent experiments, they increasingly shape their own research paths. The scientific opportunity extends from executing procedures and optimizing conditions to deciding which evidence to acquire and what that evidence implies.

Greater experimental freedom also makes scientific learning harder to evaluate. Different research paths can support the same operating success while exposing different aspects of a chemical system. A productive recipe may follow from a transferable explanation or from a locally effective empirical rule. Decision-focused learning likewise distinguishes prediction error from the quality of decisions made using predictions [@elmachtoub2022spo]. Physical laboratories offer rich possibilities for discovery, while each implemented workflow has practical limits imposed by equipment, materials and procedures. Unknown mechanisms, evolving material states and the cost of repeated experiments complicate controlled comparisons of how autonomous researchers acquire and use evidence. Understanding their progress therefore requires connecting experimental achievements to the histories and judgments that produced them.

We study scientific learning by connecting knowledge acquired through autonomous experiments to predictions under new interventions. The same researcher's experimental history and public scientific account reveal which evidence was acquired and how it was extended to unmeasured conditions. Information-gain criteria and adaptive design policies formalize the selection of informative experiments [@lindley1956information; @chaloner1995design; @foster2021adaptive], while strong inference emphasizes experiments that discriminate competing hypotheses [@platt1964inference]. These traditions motivate evaluating evidence acquisition together with the conclusions drawn from it.

Existing benchmarks evaluate complementary parts of this process. ChemBench assesses chemical knowledge and reasoning through question answering [@mirza2025chembench], and DiscoveryBench evaluates hypothesis discovery from supplied datasets [@majumder2025discoverybench]. Optimization benchmarks offer repeatable objectives [@felton2021summit; @hase2021olympus]. Interactive chemistry and discovery environments support multi-step experimentation [@beeler2024chemgymrl; @wang2022scienceworld; @jansen2024discoveryworld]; BoxingGym connects experimental design to model discovery and explanation-based prediction [@gandhi2025boxinggym]. Science-Gym evaluates active data collection and equation discovery [@cerrato2026sciencegym], while PhysGym explicitly varies prior knowledge in interactive physics discovery [@chen2025physgym].

ChemWorld brings this investigation into composable chemical processes with persistent samples and flexible operations. Evaluator control over compatible world laws and supplied information supports matched comparisons of research conditions. We follow the same researcher from self-chosen experimental operations to independently retested recommendations and sealed predictions under new interventions. Complete operational histories connect these outcomes to the evidence the researcher actually acquired.

Our results connect two features of autonomous scientific learning. Experimental success and predictive accuracy can rank the same researchers differently. Moreover, researchers facing the same chemical worlds acquire different evidence and follow distinct routes to extrapolation: some extend a local plateau into conditions where it fails, others extrapolate accurately from local observations, and others experimentally encounter a new response before predicting it. Crystallization reveals the complementary difficulty of preserving an experimentally established relationship when it remains stable. Following these paths makes it possible to study how autonomous researchers decide which relationships to retain and which to revise.

# Results

## Tracing autonomous experimentation in controlled chemical worlds

ChemWorld constructs chemical processes as composable, executable experimental worlds. Components for reaction, thermal processing, phase equilibrium, separation and crystallization connect through compatible interfaces to maintain material, energy and sample states. An environment can support characterization of a single process or connect reaction with extraction, washing, concentration or crystallization in a multistage workflow, allowing upstream operations to affect downstream processing and final measurements. Configuring process components, their connections, model parameters and available instruments creates different research environments; adding process models and observation components extends the platform.

Agents organize their own experiments in these worlds: they select materials and loadings, adjust operating parameters and procedure order, and use intermediate results to decide whether to continue processing, repeat an experiment or terminate it. Sample states persist between operations, so reaction, thermal and separation histories jointly determine subsequent outcomes. Measurements must also be actively selected. Observation channels differ in information, noise and resource cost, requiring researchers to consider both experimental progress and evidence acquisition. Evaluators independently configure compatible process laws and public information, supporting comparisons of research conditions within the same world or changes to hidden laws under a shared public interface. Complete operation, observation and resource records connect choices to consequences and support exact replay of environment trajectories (Fig. 1).

The cross-system study uses GPT-5.6 Sol with medium reasoning effort for 240 autonomous campaigns across six chemical system families. The equilibrium parameter task includes a further 60 campaigns from four model configurations to compare evidence acquisition and extrapolation. Each study block contains five worlds and three information conditions: Opaque supplies no additional instance-specific prior, Aligned supplies correct but incomplete information, and MisIndexed supplies a fixed mismatch; all arms retain the public operational contract needed to conduct experiments. Where required, sealed recommendations receive an independent retest. The original researchers retain their experimental histories and subsequently provide scientific accounts, sealed predictions under new conditions and retrospective reflections, without reference-outcome feedback. Operating outcomes and predictive judgments can thus be traced to evidence acquired by the same researcher. The complete matrix, resources and assessment protocols are specified in Methods.

A twelve-batch crystallization campaign illustrates how a researcher adjusts an experimental procedure (Fig. 2). The first four batches fail because of excessive fines. Batch 5 changes several conditions, including cooling and seeding order, and first obtains a feasible result. Recovery reaches 45.5% at batch 8; the sealed recipe achieves 43.9% on independent retest while continuing to meet the quality constraints. A subsequent solvent reduction again violates those constraints. By choosing successive interventions, the researcher shapes both its experimental outcomes and the evidence available for subsequent scientific judgments.

## Operational and predictive gains need not coincide

We first ask whether researchers that deliver better experimental procedures also predict new conditions more accurately. In electrochemical and reaction tasks that allow material selection and adjustment of electrical or thermal conditions, researchers prioritize either developing and testing scientific understanding (discovery) or obtaining high-scoring procedures (optimization). Independent campaigns under matched conditions are evaluated through recommendation retests and score predictions under new conditions (Fig. 3). Optimization achieves a higher retest score in 26 of 30 electrochemical pairs: prediction mean absolute error (MAE) is also lower in 13, but higher in the other 13. In reaction processing, discovery obtains both better retests and better predictions in 17 of 30 pairs. The two endpoints distinguish achievements that a single measure of experimental success would conflate.

One electrochemical pair connects this contrast to actual research paths (Fig. 3c,d). Discovery first compares materials and conditions, then increases duration and the current cap; optimization screens material combinations before adjusting operating conditions for a promising pair. Optimization ultimately delivers the higher-scoring retested procedure, 0.756 versus 0.718 for discovery, but also has higher prediction MAE, 0.229 versus 0.088. After several cross-pair experiments perform poorly, the optimization researcher's public prediction rationale classifies these combinations as low-activity. For one previously tested combination under new conditions, it predicts 0.070 against a reference of 0.494; for another combination, prediction and reference are 0.570 and 0.572. At the end of two independent campaigns, operating and predictive outcomes therefore give opposite rankings: optimization's recommendation has the higher retest score, while discovery predicts new conditions more accurately.

Increasing research resources likewise does not synchronize the two benefits. Independent 12- and 24-batch campaigns show improvements in some electrochemical, partitioning and crystallization predictions, but the number of feasible crystallization recommendations does not increase, nor does mean retested recovery (Supplementary Fig. S4). Experimental resources can improve prediction without simultaneously improving the final procedure delivered.

Relevant scientific knowledge can also provide substantial help. In the reaction parameter-prior study, Aligned reduces macro prediction MAE from 0.102 to 0.057 for discovery and from 0.139 to 0.084 for optimization, outperforming Opaque in four of five worlds under each commission (Supplementary Fig. S2a,b). Resources and knowledge therefore offer concrete routes to predictive improvement, alongside the procedural achievements revealed by retesting. The next question is how researchers carry experimentally acquired relationships into conditions beyond their observations. The equilibrium task makes this transition directly visible.

## The same chemical task elicits distinct routes to extrapolation

Researchers can vary loading, dilution and addition order in the equilibrium task, then predict pH, dissociation fraction and precipitation response under new conditions. The process laws remain fixed, but further dilution changes the relative contributions to charge balance, taking dissociation away from its local high-concentration plateau. Across Sol's fifteen campaigns, all 180 source assays have nominal concentrations above 0.018 M and dissociation fractions of 5.5–9.2%; reference means at the lowest-concentration test condition reach 44.8–71.6% across the five worlds (Fig. 4a). This separates relationships the researcher actually observed from the extrapolation required by new conditions.

In the Sol campaigns, correct prior information reverses its predictive benefit across test regions. Aligned reduces macro MAE on the remaining nine queries from Opaque's 0.01008 to 0.00465, but raises error on the three lowest-concentration queries from 0.01833 to 0.15862, approximately 8.7-fold; both directions occur in all five worlds (Fig. 4b; Supplementary Table F2). Nominal 80% interval coverage on the three lowest-concentration queries also falls from 93.3% for Opaque to 2.2% for Aligned. Original answers show Opaque extrapolating an increase in dissociation without directly sampling dilute solutions, while Aligned mainly extends the plateau or a weak local trend (Supplementary Fig. S8c). One Aligned answer acknowledges that the plateau need not persist towards zero loading, yet predicts 8.04% [5.30%, 10.80%] against a reference mean of 65.46%. The explanation acknowledges a boundary to applicability, but the sealed numerical forecast continues to use the plateau level.

The five model configurations exhibit distinct patterns of generalization under the same scientific conditions (Fig. 4c; Supplementary Table F3). We compare Sol with the medium configurations of GPT-5.5, GPT-5.6 Luna, GPT-5.6 Terra and GPT-6 Astra in the same five worlds, three information conditions and twelve-batch envelope. Whereas Sol shows a pronounced reversal of prior benefit, Luna and Terra obtain lower mean error with Aligned in both query groups, and GPT-5.5 changes little. Astra maintains low error across all three information arms on the lowest-concentration queries, with MAE of 0.01857–0.04040 and original interval coverage of 82.7–92.4%. A shared task and information protocol thus elicit different responses to scientific knowledge, from gains across both query groups to a reversal between them.

The experimental histories reveal a corresponding difference in evidence acquisition. Eleven of Astra's fifteen campaigns assay positively loaded samples at nominal concentrations no greater than 1 mM, compared with zero of fifteen for each of the other four configurations (Fig. 5a). Sol/Opaque nevertheless predicts the dilute response accurately from its local observations, achieving MAE of 0.01833 on the three lowest-concentration queries (Fig. 5b,c). Accurate extrapolation therefore occurs even in campaigns that never directly sample this regime.

An Astra campaign shows the complementary route of acquiring new evidence before extrapolation (Fig. 5d). In World 3/MisIndexed, dissociation is 6.3–7.4% in the first three batches. Batch 5 lowers concentration to 18.5 μM and observes 53.8%; later batches return to higher concentrations and explore staged dilution, additional loading and longer waiting. The subsequent public scientific account distinguishes the high-concentration plateau from the low-concentration response. The researcher then seals a forecast of 58.80% [54.20%, 63.40%] at 13.3 μM, against a reference mean of 57.97%. The recorded sequence connects autonomous observation of a changed response to its recognition in the scientific account and an accurate prediction at a new condition.

Prediction accuracy alone obscures these different research paths. Sol/Opaque extrapolates accurately beyond its observations, while the Astra case first acquires evidence outside the plateau and subsequently predicts the changed response. Linking forecasts to experimental histories reveals these distinct routes to a successful outcome alongside the local-rule extension that fails under dilution.

## Predictions systematically underestimate stable purity

The equilibrium studies expose the challenge of anticipating a response that changes beyond the observed region. Sol's crystallization campaigns reveal the complementary difficulty of preserving a relationship that remains stable. Mean purity is 98.53% in source observations and 98.51% in withheld references; the mean within-world range across withheld conditions is only 2.44 percentage points. Yet mean predictions are 93.54% and 94.89% at 12 and 24 batches, respectively, with 335 of 360 predictions below their reference values (Fig. 6a).

Within the same campaigns, the recovery–purity comparison shows that this departure is response-specific. A baseline repeats each campaign's own public final-assay mean for every withheld condition, using no additional experiments or withheld outcomes. Agent recovery predictions outperform this baseline in 26 of 30 campaigns, whereas purity predictions outperform it in none (Fig. 6b); mean purity MAEs are 4.35 and 0.48 percentage points, respectively. The same researcher can predict changing recovery more effectively than the mean baseline, yet fail to preserve the stable purity level already established by its experiments.

Purity errors predominantly reflect an overall downward shift. Mean signed error is −4.29 percentage points, and campaign-level offsets account for 87.6% of total squared error (Supplementary Table G2). Information conditions and research budgets alter the relative performance of recovery and purity forecasts, while prediction intervals fail to accommodate the purity errors reliably (Fig. 6c,d). Aligned and MisIndexed report wider mean intervals than Opaque yet achieve poorer coverage. The main error is thus a departure from the empirically established purity level, accompanied by errors in predicting its smaller variations across conditions.

# Discussion

Experimentally acquired relationships can be extended too far or insufficiently preserved. In Sol/Aligned equilibrium campaigns, predictions extend a local plateau into dilute conditions where the response changes substantially. In crystallization, predictions fall systematically below the stable purity jointly established by experiments and withheld outcomes. These opposite departures make scientific generalization concrete: which experimental relationships should change with an intervention, and which should continue to hold [@kramer2026discovery]?

The model comparison reveals distinct scientific behaviours within the same experimental setting. Sol/Opaque extrapolates successfully from local observations, while Astra frequently acquires evidence at lower concentrations and, in the selected campaign, observes the changed response before accurately predicting it. Similar predictive success can therefore accompany different evidence histories. Conversely, one Sol/Aligned answer describes a boundary to the plateau in words but retains its level numerically: articulating an applicability limit and applying it in a quantitative judgment are distinct achievements. ChemWorld makes these differences visible by connecting experimental choices, acquired evidence, public accounts and sealed forecasts. Evaluating scientific learning requires this connection between research process and outcome, alongside tests of uncertainty under changing conditions [@gneiting2007scoring; @ovadia2019uncertainty].

These observations suggest a design principle for scientific agents: make the conditions under which a learned relationship persists or changes an explicit target of experiment selection. Experiments can then be chosen to distinguish competing explanations [@platt1964inference]. The Astra trajectory provides an executed example of observing a response beyond the local plateau before sealing predictions. Some Sol researchers propose trace-loading experiments only during retrospective review, and a separate crystallization pair proposes a thermal test at the same later stage (Supplementary section C.4 and Fig. S5); these proposals remain unexecuted. The design opportunity is to bring these judgments forward, so they guide evidence acquisition before a forecast is sealed.

Cross-system results cover one model configuration across six families; the five-model equilibrium comparison reuses worlds and readouts, with one campaign per model–world–information condition and exploratory subgroup analyses. Separating the contributions of sampling, prior use and interpretation, and testing transfer to physical laboratories, remain future work. Our measures assess predictions under new interventions and public scientific accounts; complete mechanistic understanding and internal belief revision require further evidence [@shmueli2010explain]. Programmable worlds make autonomous research behaviour an object of controlled investigation, connecting what researchers achieve to the evidence they acquire and how they extend it to new conditions.

# Methods

## World construction and execution

Each world is defined by a public experimental contract and private process parameters. Process components declare states, domains, dependencies and compatible interfaces; operation and observation models compile those declarations into a laboratory interface. Participants act through that interface but cannot inspect the simulator, private parameters, evaluator records or repository. Instrument measurements may consume samples, and operations consume contract-defined materials, time and action allowances. Candidate transitions are validated before commitment. Rejected operations leave committed physical state unchanged while retaining contract-defined attempt consequences. Replay concerns the environment and recorded actions, not regeneration of language-model responses.

## Equilibrium and crystallization process models

The equilibrium parameter study first solves monoprotic acid–base speciation, accounting for water self-ionization and preset background-ion inventories, followed by sequential 1:1 precipitation. Before ion-amount caps become active, the net background charge is 0.07 times the acid loading. Charge balance therefore permits a dissociation plateau near 7% when hydrogen- and hydroxide-ion contributions are small relative to total concentration; dilution changes their relative contributions. The solver uses current ledger amounts and volumes, whereas reported nominal concentrations describe recipe loadings. Supplementary section H gives the executed equations and effective pKa range.

The crystallization model couples temperature-dependent solubility to supersaturation-dependent nucleation, growth and impurity transfer. Crystal populations and phase inventories persist through cooling, seeding and filtration. Purity is the amount of solid target divided by total target and impurity solids. Full-process final-assay recovery excludes seed contributions and is normalized by initial reagent loading. The model used here has a linear supersaturation-dependent impurity-incorporation law. Near-constant purity is an observed property of the source and withheld outcomes, not a property fixed by the score definition. Supplementary section H records constitutive relations and parameters.

## Platform qualification

Platform validation covers material and resource accounting, state transitions, component compatibility, invalid-action handling and information separation. Trajectory replay checks the consistency of recorded actions and their consequences. Study adapters undergo corresponding checks separately. Supplementary Methods provide the complete validation scope and results.

## Study units and objectives

The 240 cross-system campaigns comprise eight study blocks: entity-prior electrochemistry (60), entity-prior partitioning (30), parameter- and structural-prior reaction processing (60 combined), entity-, parameter- and structural-prior equilibrium (15 each), entity-prior crystallization (30), and entity-prior purification (15). Each block uses five worlds and three information arms. Electrochemistry and reaction processing include discovery and optimization commissions. Electrochemistry, partitioning and crystallization have 12- and 24-batch conditions; other blocks use twelve batches. P for parameter priors is distinct from P for purification.

Electrochemistry varies materials, potential, current cap and duration; optimization targets a public balanced-efficiency score. Reaction processing varies materials, catalyst loading and thermal history. Partitioning examines allocation under material, volume and process changes. Equilibrium examines amounts, volumes, concentrations, staged additions, dissociation and precipitation. Crystallization seeks seed-excluded recovery subject to purity of at least 0.80, fines of at most 0.50 and particles being present. Purification seeks original-charge recovery subject to purity of at least 0.80. Their recovery definitions differ and are not pooled.

## Agents and information conditions

All 240 cross-system source campaigns use GPT-5.6 Sol with medium reasoning effort in independent sessions. Targeted equilibrium comparisons additionally include GPT-5.6 Luna, GPT-5.6 Terra, GPT-5.5 and GPT-6 Astra, all requested at medium; this shared setting does not imply equal realized computation across models. Within a study block, arms share physical worlds, operations, resource contracts and withheld questions. Treatments vary public information according to the block's fixed entity-, parameter- or structure-prior design. Opaque retains operational interfaces and general task context. Aligned provides partial nominal descriptions, such as a correct effective-pKa interval in the equilibrium parameter study. MisIndexed applies the specified mismatch rather than arbitrary misinformation.

The targeted comparison uses the same equilibrium parameter protocol, five worlds, three arms and twelve batches per campaign, with fifteen campaigns for each of four configurations. Each campaign permits at most 180 operation attempts, twelve intermediate measurements, twelve final assays, 0.24 mol reagent and 0.72 L solvent. Prompts, priors, queries, resources and scoring rules are fixed within the block, with one campaign per model–world–information condition. Some World 1 sessions were inspected as pilots; sensitivity analyses excluding that world are reported separately. Supplementary Methods specify the pilot scope.

For a concrete equilibrium example, the first world's Aligned input provides an effective-pKa interval of [4.6094, 4.7094] and a local dilution-response statement; MisIndexed provides [5.3294, 5.4294] and the corresponding statement from the fifth world. Both use the same anchor, confidence level and qualification that the relationship is local. Opaque omits these instance-specific fields. Supplementary Table H1 reproduces numerical excerpts from the frozen input configuration and serializer.

Agents choose conditions, repetitions, measurement allocation and experiment grouping, and may use a bounded public numerical tool. They are not required to select mechanism templates, emit a fixed equation or reflect after every batch. Batches, operations, provider responses and decisions made after feedback are different counting units. Each budget permits a matching number of additional measurements and one final assay per completed batch; other resources are separately bounded. Larger budgets also include larger execution and computation allowances.

## Sealed assessments and retests

Where required, the agent submits and seals one completed batch as its recommendation before assessment. Its operating procedure is independently retested in the same world using a different observation seed; 165 such retests are performed. K1 requests an unconstrained scientific account covering relationships, evidence, applicability and unresolved alternatives. Q then requests point forecasts and intervals for twelve fixed new-batch recipes, without laboratory access or target feedback. After predictions are sealed, K2 asks retrospective questions about evidence, limitations and discriminating experiments. Short versions contain three questions; reaction and equilibrium studies use seven themes. All fifteen Sol equilibrium parameter campaigns and sixty targeted campaigns complete structured supplemental questions after K2.

Every stage retains the original research context. Q uses both K1 and the source experimental history, and K2 follows the sealed Q without modifying it. The evaluation therefore follows the researcher from its own experiments to subsequent predictions. The equilibrium parameter comparison includes fifteen Sol campaigns and sixty campaigns from the four targeted configurations, for 75 in total; all include original-session K1, Q, K2 and structured supplemental questions. Complete mechanism annotation has not been conducted for the whole study pool.

## Outcome definitions and references

For each response, campaign MAE averages absolute point error across twelve questions. Interval coverage is the fraction of specified reference targets contained in reported intervals, and width is their mean span. Partitioning uses nominal 90% intervals; other blocks use 80%. Partitioning and crystallization point references use underlying pre-final-assay response values. Electrochemistry and purification use fixed seeded observations. Reaction and equilibrium point references average five observations, while coverage is evaluated against each observation. Macro MAE equally averages response-specific MAEs within the designated block. Targets and scales are not pooled across systems.

Crystallization mean baselines use only public source final assays; nearest-neighbour predictions additionally use public recipe features. Neither uses additional experiments or withheld outcomes. The equilibrium source-mean diagnostic is a post hoc reference for testing forecasts that repeat the source plateau. Supplementary Methods record the recipe-feature parsing correction.

Two exploratory diagnostics use only retained predictions and references (Supplementary section G). Response-specific MAEs in the Sol equilibrium parameter study retain all 540 point predictions and exactly reproduce regime macro means. Crystallization purity analysis retains all 360 predictions and decomposes each campaign's mean squared error (MSE) into squared mean error plus centred MSE. With equal query counts, the pooled offset share is the sum of squared campaign biases divided by the sum of campaign MSEs. Centring uses withheld references for diagnosis, not to construct a corrected predictor or claim improved predictive performance.

## Statistical analysis and inclusion

Goal comparisons match world, information arm, budget and prior locus. Budget comparisons match world, information arm and goal, comparing independent sessions. Information-arm comparisons match other factors within each study. Means equally weight campaigns. Five world clusters recur across arms, goals and budgets; questions, responses and batches are correlated observations. We report effect magnitudes, paired directions, tables retaining all arms and selected sensitivity analyses, without inferential tests treating queries as independent trials. Figure error bars show sample standard deviations across worlds; descriptive bars and sample standard deviations are not confidence intervals. Goal-comparison quadrant counts use strict signs of paired differences, with no minimum-effect or significance threshold. Baseline comparisons are performed separately for each crystallization response.

Orienting comparisons by observed retest performance is a post hoc re-expression of the original goal pairs, not an additional experiment. Within each pair, we identify the campaign with the higher recommendation retest score and compare its score-prediction MAE with its counterpart's. All pairs and exact effect magnitudes are retained without a minimum-effect threshold. The sensitivity subset excludes any recorded campaign recovery, including assessment-only recovery, rather than source restarts alone.

The equilibrium dilution grouping is selected post hoc using public concentrations of the three lowest-concentration recipes, approximately $1.33\times10^{-4}$, $1.33\times10^{-5}$ and $1.67\times10^{-4}$ M. Grouped scores reproduce each original twelve-question macro score when weighted 3:9. The joint crystallization recovery–purity comparison is also exploratory. Both analyses preserve the original query banks and all outcomes. The remaining nine conditions include boundary and unexplored recipes and are not an in-distribution control group. Source observations plotted against concentration come from self-selected recipes and processing histories, rather than a controlled concentration sweep.

All 240 cross-system campaigns and their 720 scientific-account, prediction and retrospective stages are included. Of these, 238 chains conform to the protocol, and 3,597 of 3,600 planned source final assays are completed. One crystallization source completes eleven rather than twelve final assays after exhausting solvent. One purification source uses twelve starts but completes ten final assays and discards two vessels. Both are marked nonconforming and neither is replaced. Infrastructure retries retain earlier attempts and resource accounting. Supplementary tables report the complete matrix, targets, qualification domains and response-specific arm means. Original protocol variation and excluded historical pilots are recorded separately from this pool.

The four targeted configurations complete 60/60 research chains, 720/720 batches, 240/240 original-session assessment stages and 2,160 scalar predictions, all with exact environment replay. They share 300 reference batches (five worlds × twelve queries × five observations), used only for evaluation. Supplementary Methods describe infrastructure recoveries and rejected operations.

## Retrospective analysis of autonomous equilibrium research

Using retained source and trajectory indexes, the Sol parameter-prior equilibrium analysis includes all fifteen effective sources: five worlds, three arms, 180 batches, 945 operations and 45 standard assessment stages, plus fifteen structured supplemental responses. Nominal batch concentration is total reagent added divided by total solvent added, not an inferred dissolved concentration. Trajectory analysis adds no model, simulator or judge calls; original question scores and reference observations are unchanged.

A single author reviews public Q explanations and assigns descriptive categories: piecewise weak-acid extrapolation, qualitative departure from the plateau, plateau or weak-trend extension, and mixed weak-acid/plateau explanation. Original excerpts and prediction values accompany these categories, but they are not an independent mechanism assessment. K1 evidence is distinguished from hypotheses first explicit in Q. K2 future-experiment choices are classified separately: eleven of fifteen campaigns propose trace-loading experiments after exposure to Q conditions, without reference feedback. These proposals are unexecuted and do not establish spontaneous source-stage discovery or repair. Exported decision-reason fields are unavailable for all 945 operations; no contemporaneous reasoning sequence is reconstructed from them.

Cross-model source coverage is summarized by each campaign's lowest nominal concentration among positively loaded final assays, excluding zero-loading blanks. The post hoc descriptive 1 mM threshold is neither a physical critical concentration nor a success criterion. The Astra case is selected retrospectively; its K1 account follows experimentation and precedes Q, and does not represent contemporaneous internal reasoning or unique mechanism identification. Sol explanation categories are not automatically generalized to other models. For the four targeted configurations, the 80% interval score is width plus ten times the distance from an out-of-interval reference to the nearest endpoint, averaged over five reference observations; lower is better. It is reported separately from coverage. Campaign minima describe the lowest sampled point rather than continuous coverage. Boxplot quartiles are calculated on the original concentration scale before logarithmic display; coincident quartiles appear as a line.
