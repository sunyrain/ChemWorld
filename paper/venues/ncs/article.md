abstract: |
  Self-driving laboratories increasingly design procedures and pursue research paths. Equipment and workflow constraints, experimental costs, safety requirements and unknown mechanisms make their scientific learning difficult to study systematically. We introduce ChemWorld, programmable chemical worlds where agents compose experiments on evolving samples while evaluators independently control process laws and supplied information. Self-driving laboratories are extending the reach of experimental science through automated execution, continuous feedback and efficient searches for useful conditions. Language-model agents further connect scientific instructions, tools and laboratory operations, opening possibilities for more flexible research workflows [@boiko2023autonomous; @bran2024augmenting; @szymanski2023alab]. As systems gain the freedom to choose operation sequences, intermediate measurements and subsequent experiments, they increasingly shape their own research paths. The scientific opportunity extends from executing procedures and optimizing conditions to deciding which evidence to acquire and what that evidence implies.

Greater experimental freedom also makes scientific learning harder to evaluate. Different research paths can support the same operating success while exposing different aspects of a chemical system. A productive recipe may follow from a transferable explanation or from a locally effective empirical rule. Physical laboratories offer rich possibilities for discovery, while each implemented workflow has practical limits imposed by equipment, materials and procedures. Unknown mechanisms, evolving material states and the cost of repeated experiments complicate controlled comparisons of how autonomous researchers acquire and use evidence. Understanding their progress therefore requires connecting experimental achievements to the histories and judgments that produced them.

We examine an observable component of scientific understanding: how autonomous researchers use evidence they acquire to predict outcomes under new interventions. Research histories, public scientific accounts and sealed predictions connect experimental achievement to judgments about where relationships remain applicable. Prediction is one window onto this capacity; it does not by itself establish complete mechanistic understanding.

ChemWorld provides a computational setting for this investigation. Optimization benchmarks offer repeatable objectives [@felton2021summit; @hase2021olympus], and interactive chemistry and discovery environments support multi-step experimentation and knowledge assessment [@beeler2024chemgymrl; @jansen2024discoveryworld; @gandhi2025boxinggym]. ChemWorld combines composable chemical processes, persistent experimental state and flexible operations with evaluator control over world and information conditions. The researcher can organize experiments within executable chemical domains; the evaluator can configure compatible worlds and compare research conditions while retaining their complete operational histories. This combination makes autonomous evidence acquisition and its subsequent use accessible to controlled study.

We examine 240 campaigns across six chemical system families, following the original researcher from experiment selection to operating recommendations, scientific accounts and sealed predictions. Agents develop adaptive procedures and benefit from additional resources and relevant knowledge, but operating and predictive improvements do not consistently coincide. More strikingly, correct prior information reverses its predictive benefit across equilibrium response regimes, whereas crystallization forecasts unnecessarily depart from stable purity despite predicting recovery effectively. These findings motivate applicability judgments as a critical dimension of scientific learning: determining when an observed relationship should persist, when it should change and which experiments could resolve the difference.

# Results

## Tracing autonomous experimentation in controlled chemical worlds

ChemWorld constructs executable worlds from compatible process and observation components. Operations change composition, phase allocation, thermal history and remaining material, whereas measurements expose only declared quantities. Operations act on persistent samples, and their history determines the state available for subsequent measurements and decisions. Researchers choose valid procedures, repetitions and intermediate measurements within chemical and resource constraints; evaluators independently configure compatible process laws, private parameters and supplied information. A world can therefore change behind a common public interface, or remain fixed while the researcher's information changes. Because observations arise from the experiments actually chosen, supplied information can affect both evidence acquisition and interpretation (Fig. 1). State transitions, resource accounting and replay are qualified separately (Methods).

The study comprises 240 campaigns in five-world, three-arm blocks. Opaque provides usable public experimental contracts and anonymous identifiers, Aligned adds correct partial nominal information and MisIndexed supplies a fixed mismatch or reassignment. The same agent configuration conducts each campaign; budget comparisons use independent 12- and 24-batch sessions in the systems with both envelopes. Where required, a sealed recommendation receives an independent retest. The original session then provides a scientific account (K1), predictions for withheld conditions (Q) and retrospective evidence review (K2), retaining its research history. Neither Q nor K2 receives reference feedback, and K2 cannot revise Q. All campaigns remain included, including two with incomplete final-assay budgets.

## Operational and predictive gains need not coincide

Research commissions change priorities without prescribing experimental paths. Discovery prioritizes developing and testing an explanatory, predictive account, whereas optimization prioritizes a high-scoring procedure. We compare independent campaigns matched within world, information condition, budget and prior locus, evaluating recommendation retests and withheld predictions separately (Fig. 3). Optimization has the higher retest score in 26 of 30 electrochemical pairs; thirteen of these pairs also have lower score-prediction mean absolute error (MAE), whereas thirteen have higher MAE. In reaction processing, discovery performs better on both endpoints in seventeen of thirty pairs. Thus neither commission has a predetermined overall advantage, and operating and predictive outcomes require separate evaluation.

A selected electrochemical pair makes this separation concrete (Fig. 3c,d). Discovery compares materials and conditions before extending duration and increasing the current cap. Optimization refines a promising solvent-electrolyte combination, raising its observed score from 0.675 in batch 3 to 0.749 in batch 12. Recommendation retests are 0.718 and 0.756 for discovery and optimization, respectively, but prediction MAEs are 0.088 and 0.229. After poor cross-pair experiments, the optimization agent's public prediction rationale assigns low activity to several combinations. Under new conditions it predicts 0.070 [0, 0.240] for one previously tested combination, against a reference of 0.494, while retaining an accurate forecast of 0.570 against 0.572 for another. The records connect experimental evidence, an expressed transfer rule and sealed predictions; they do not show prediction quality changing after each optimization step.

The converse separation also occurs: predictive gains need not accompany better delivery. The 24-batch condition is an independent session with larger experimental and computational allowances (Fig. 4). Electrochemical score MAE improves in 11 of 15 discovery pairs and 12 of 15 optimization pairs. Phase-partitioning organic-fraction MAE improves in 13 of 15 pairs, decreasing from 0.08291 to 0.02379. Crystallization recovery MAE also decreases, from 0.10433 to 0.07937, yet quality-eligible recommendations remain 12 of 15 and mean retested recovery decreases from 0.42054 to 0.39939. Fines-interval coverage rises from 35.6% to 43.9% but remains below nominal 80% in every campaign. More research opportunities can therefore improve selected predictions without improving every delivered outcome.

Relevant scientific information provides another route to improvement. In the reaction parameter study, Aligned information lowers discovery macro MAE from 0.10186 to 0.05701 and optimization error from 0.13893 to 0.08384, outperforming Opaque in four of five worlds under each commission (Supplementary Fig. S2a,b). Across other blocks, no information arm has a common advantage. These gains raise a more specific question: can the value of correct information reverse when prediction moves beyond the conditions that supported it?

## Correct prior information can reverse its predictive benefit across regimes

The equilibrium study reveals a regime-dependent reversal in the value of correct partial information. Aligned supplies a correct effective-pKa interval and local dilution response, rather than complete model equations (Methods; Supplementary Table H1). Relative to Opaque, it lowers macro MAE from 0.01008 to 0.00465 on nine queries but raises error from 0.01833 to 0.15862 on the three most dilute---approximately 8.7 times Opaque's error in that group (Fig. 5b; Table 1). Both directions occur in all five worlds. The concentration grouping is post hoc, and the other nine queries include boundary conditions rather than only interpolation.

The acquired evidence does not enter the dilute response regime. All fifteen campaigns sample nominal concentrations above 0.018 M, and dissociation spans 5.5-9.2% across 180 source assays. At the most dilute test, reference means instead span 44.8-71.6% (Fig. 5a). In the executed model, background-ion charge balance produces a local plateau; dilution changes the balance without changing the process laws. One Aligned campaign spans a ninety-fold concentration range and its Opaque counterpart a fourfold range, yet neither enters the dilute regime (Supplementary C). Broad input exploration can therefore leave the transition between response regimes unobserved.

The reversal is response-specific but not confined to dissociation. Aligned's benefit on the other nine queries comes mainly from precipitation, whereas pH and dissociation have mixed directions; on the dilute three, all three responses worsen in every world (Supplementary Table G1). Its original 80% intervals cover 91.3% of reference observations on the other nine queries but only 2.2% on the dilute three, compared with 90.7% and 93.3% for Opaque. Reported uncertainty therefore does not adequately represent the transfer error.

The complementary question is whether forecasts preserve a relationship that remains stable under new conditions.

## Useful recovery predictions coexist with systematic purity underestimation

Crystallization exposes the complementary applicability failure: a response remains approximately stable, yet forecasts depart from it. Source and withheld purity average 98.53% and 98.51%, whereas forecasts average 93.54% at 12 batches and 94.89% at 24. Of 360 forecasts, 335 are too low (Fig. 6a), and the mean within-world span of withheld purity is only 2.442 percentage points. The model tracks target and impurity in the solid separately: supersaturation-dependent nucleation and growth alter recovery and particle populations, while modest impurity transfer permits high purity. Its near-constant level is therefore an observed result over the tested conditions, not an imposed constant (Methods; Supplementary H).

The departure is response-specific. A baseline that repeats each campaign's own public final-assay mean uses no additional experiments or withheld outcomes. Agent forecasts outperform it for recovery in 26 of 30 campaigns but for purity in none (Fig. 6b). Mean purity MAE is 4.35 percentage points for the agent versus 0.48 for the baseline. The same researchers can therefore predict changing recovery more effectively than this baseline while failing to preserve near-constant purity. Fines, particle-size and nearest-neighbour comparisons are reported in Supplementary Table E1.

A retained-data decomposition separates a campaign-wide purity offset from error in variation across conditions (Supplementary Table G2). Mean signed bias is -4.29 percentage points, and squared campaign biases account for 87.6% of total squared error. Overall underestimation therefore dominates, but centred RMSE still exceeds reference standard deviation in 29 of 30 campaigns. Both a systematic level shift and residual errors in variation contribute. This output-level diagnosis does not identify an internal revision process.

Information conditions further constrain the interpretation. At 12 batches, Aligned and MisIndexed improve recovery MAE while worsening purity MAE relative to Opaque in every world; at 24 batches, this joint direction occurs in only two and three of five worlds, respectively (Fig. 6c). Prediction intervals also fail to accommodate the purity errors reliably (Fig. 6d). Aligned and MisIndexed intervals are wider on average than Opaque intervals at both budgets, so poorer coverage cannot be attributed simply to narrower reported uncertainty; conversely, high Opaque coverage alone does not establish calibration or sharpness. The effects depend on both response and research envelope.

# Discussion

Across the tested campaigns, useful procedures and improved predictions did not ensure successful transfer to new conditions. Aligned equilibrium forecasts extended a local dissociation plateau into conditions where the response changed, whereas crystallization forecasts departed from purity that remained approximately stable. Following the original researcher from self-chosen experiments to sealed predictions connects these departures to the evidence acquired during research. Together, the findings motivate applicability judgment---deciding which relationships to preserve and which to revise---as an observable dimension of scientific learning. They do not establish that the two failures share an internal cause.

Evaluation should therefore connect experimental histories, public explanations, predictions and uncertainty. Acknowledging that a relationship has limits does not establish that those limits are reflected in forecasts. Some researchers proposed potentially informative follow-up experiments during retrospective review, after predictions were sealed and without reference feedback, but those experiments remained unexecuted (Supplementary C.4; Supplementary Fig. S4). This suggests a focused next test: whether evidence acquired at a diagnostic boundary improves the original researcher's subsequent predictions. Such an intervention could test whether acquiring discriminating evidence turns recognition of a boundary into better prediction.

The current evidence concerns one agent configuration, five reused worlds per study and several exploratory comparisons with correlated readouts. It does not isolate the contributions of evidence acquisition and interpretation or establish transfer to physical laboratories. Programmable worlds make these distinctions experimentally accessible while retaining the researcher's freedom to choose procedures. Further work can test whether the observed patterns recur across agents and whether targeted evidence acquisition changes them. Progress should be assessed through both useful experimental outcomes and predictions that reflect where acquired relationships remain applicable.

# Methods

## World construction and execution

A world is specified by public experimental contracts and private process parameters. Process components declare state, domains, dependencies and compatible interfaces. Operation and observation models compile these declarations into a laboratory interface. The participant can act through that interface but cannot inspect the simulator, private parameters, evaluator records or repository. Instruments may consume samples and operations consume declared material, time and action allowances. Candidate transitions are validated before commitment; rejection preserves committed physical state while retaining the declared attempt consequences. Replay concerns the environment and recorded operations, not regeneration of language-model responses.

## Equilibrium and crystallization process models

The equilibrium parameter study solves monoprotic acid-base speciation with water autoionization and prescribed background-ion inventories, followed by a sequential 1:1 precipitation calculation. Before the ion-amount caps apply, net background charge is 0.07 times the acid amount. Charge balance therefore permits a dissociation plateau near 7% when the hydrogen and hydroxide contributions are small relative to total concentration; their relative contributions change upon dilution. The solver uses current ledger amounts and volume, whereas the reported nominal concentrations describe recipe additions. Supplementary H gives the executed equations and effective-pKa range.

Crystallization couples temperature-dependent solubility to supersaturation-dependent nucleation, growth and impurity transfer. Crystal population and phase inventories persist through cooling, seeding and filtration. Purity is solid target divided by total target-plus-impurity solids. The full-process final-assay recovery removes seed contribution and uses initial reagent charge as denominator. The retained model uses linear supersaturation-dependent impurity incorporation. Near-constant purity is established by these source and withheld outcomes, rather than fixed by the scoring definition. Supplementary H records the constitutive relations and parameters.

## Platform qualification

The frozen platform qualification passed 64 task-world units, 1,786 boundary and categorical recipes, 192 invalid-action probes and 52 generated compositions. The generated cases comprise 18 previously absent topologies, eight new task-world identities using a registered topology and 26 additional coverage cases. Component and interface checks passed 32 module probes, seven interface paths and seven invalid compositions. These finite-domain checks establish internal model and execution consistency; they do not establish agreement with arbitrary wet-laboratory chemistry. Exact environment replay reconstructs executed actions and resource consequences, including rejection and rollback.

Qualification distinguishes internal physical and accounting consistency from successful research. It checks executable domains, resources, information separation, valid query references and exact replay. Invalid actions and incompatible compositions have explicit expected rejection or rollback behaviour. Subsequent study adapters have their own checks; the frozen platform qualification is not an automatic certificate for later execution code. The campaign pool combines study blocks collected under their recorded protocols, with differences in reference construction, assessment prompts and recovery handling disclosed below. Post hoc comparisons remain labelled exploratory; this analysis neither changes their execution status nor turns platform checks into validation of scientific understanding.

## Study units and objectives

The eight blocks comprise electrochemistry with entity priors (60 campaigns), partitioning with entity priors (30), reaction processing with parameter and structural priors (60), equilibrium with entity, parameter and structural priors (15 each), crystallization with entity priors (30) and purification with entity priors (15). Every block uses five worlds and three information arms. Electrochemistry and reaction processing have discovery and optimization commissions. Electrochemistry, partitioning and crystallization have 12- and 24-batch conditions; other blocks use 12. Parameter-prior P and purification P denote different concepts.

Electrochemistry varies materials, potential, current limits and duration; its optimization objective is a public balanced-efficiency score. Reaction processing varies materials, catalyst loading and thermal histories. Partitioning studies allocation under material, volume and process changes. Equilibrium studies amount, volume, concentration, staged additions, dissociation and precipitation. Crystallization seeks recovery net of seed mass subject to purity at least 0.80, fines fraction at most 0.50 and particles present. Purification seeks original-charge recovery subject to purity at least 0.80. Recovery denominators differ and are not combined.

## Agent and information conditions

All source campaigns use GPT-5.6 Sol with medium reasoning effort in independent sessions. Within a block, arms share the physical world, operations, resource contract and withheld questions. The treatment modifies the public dossier according to the block's fixed entity, parameter or structural design. Opaque still exposes the operational interface and general task background. Aligned is a partial nominal description, such as a true effective-pKa interval in the equilibrium parameter study. MisIndexed applies the specified mismatch rather than supplying arbitrary misinformation.

For a concrete equilibrium example, the first world's Aligned input provides an effective-pKa interval of [4.6094, 4.7094] and a local dilution-response claim; MisIndexed provides [5.3294, 5.4294] and the corresponding claim from the fifth world. Both use the same anchor, confidence and qualification as a local relationship; Opaque omits these instance-specific fields. Supplementary Table H1 gives the numerical excerpt reproduced from the frozen input configuration and serializer.

Agents may choose conditions, repetitions, measurement allocations and experiment grouping, and may use the bounded public numerical tool. They are not required to choose from mechanism templates, emit a fixed equation or reflect after each batch. Batches, operations, provider responses and decisions made after feedback are different counts. Each budget permits a matching number of additional measurements and a final assay per completed batch; other resources remain separately bounded. The larger budget includes a larger execution and computation envelope. Retained early electrochemistry and partitioning assessments have an eight-call calculator allowance; later blocks disclose up to 128 attempts. Outputs are requested in English, but retained reaction and equilibrium assessment prompts include Chinese instructions requesting English responses. These differences remain part of the historical protocol.

## Sealed assessments and retests

Where required, a completed-batch recommendation is sealed before assessment and its procedure retested independently with a different observation seed in the same world. There are 165 such retests. K1 requests an unrestricted scientific account including relationships, evidence, applicability and unresolved alternatives. Q then requests point predictions and intervals for twelve fixed new-batch recipes without laboratory access or target feedback. K2 follows the sealed predictions with retrospective questions about support, limitations and discriminating experiments. The short version has three questions; reaction and equilibrium use seven themes. The bounded equilibrium parameter block adds fifteen structured supplements.

The original research context is available during every stage. Q can use the source history in addition to K1, and K2 cannot revise Q. Consequently, neither report-only knowledge transfer nor a uniform mechanism-correctness score is an endpoint. The bounded equilibrium review described below covers all fifteen campaigns in that block; complete mechanism annotation across the 240-campaign programme has not been performed.

## Outcome definitions and references

For each response, campaign MAE averages absolute point error across the twelve questions. Interval coverage measures the fraction of specified reference targets contained in the reported intervals; width is their average span. Partitioning uses nominal 90% intervals and the other blocks use 80%. Partitioning and crystallization point references use underlying pre-final-assay response values. Electrochemistry and purification use fixed seeded observations. Reaction and equilibrium point references average five observations; their coverage is evaluated against the individual observations. Macro MAE averages response-specific MAEs equally within the designated block. These targets and scales are not pooled across systems.

The crystallization mean baseline uses only the source campaign's public final assays. Nearest-neighbour prediction additionally uses public recipe features. A parser correction excludes resource-denied attempts from physical recipe features while preserving those attempts in the record. It restores two baseline evaluations; the other twenty-eight remain exactly unchanged. No agent or simulator is rerun for the correction. The equilibrium source-mean diagnostic is a separate post hoc reference. Its failure on dilute queries confirms that copying the plateau is insufficient for those questions; it is not a strong system-identification comparator.

Two additional exploratory diagnostics use only retained predictions and references (Supplementary G). Equilibrium response-specific MAEs retain all 540 point forecasts and reproduce the regime macro means exactly. Crystallization purity error retains all 360 predictions and decomposes each campaign's MSE into squared mean error plus centred MSE. The pooled offset share is the sum of squared campaign biases divided by the sum of campaign MSEs, with equal query counts. Centring uses the withheld references for diagnosis, not to construct a corrected predictor or claim a performance improvement.

## Descriptive comparisons and retained failures

Goal comparisons match world, information arm, budget and prior locus. Budget comparisons match world, arm and goal, comparing independent sessions. Arm comparisons match the other factors within each study. Means weight campaigns equally. Five world clusters recur across arms, goals and budgets; questions, response metrics and batches are correlated observations. We report magnitudes, paired directions, all-arm tables and selected sensitivity analyses rather than inferential tests treating queries as independent trials.

The comparison oriented by observed retest performance is a post hoc re-expression of the original goal pairs, not an additional experiment. Within each pair, we identify the campaign with the higher recommendation retest score and compare its score-prediction MAE with that of its counterpart. All pairs and exact effect magnitudes are retained; no minimum-effect threshold is applied. The electrochemical illustration is retrospectively selected and does not estimate the prevalence of its expressed prediction rule. The sensitivity subset excludes any recorded campaign recovery, including assessment-only recovery, rather than source restarts alone.

The equilibrium dilution grouping was selected post hoc using the public concentrations of the three most dilute recipes, approximately $1.33\times10^{-4}$, $1.33\times10^{-5}$ and $1.67\times10^{-4}$ M. Grouped scores reproduce each original twelve-question macro score when weighted 3:9. The joint recovery–purity crystallization comparison is also exploratory. Neither changes the original query bank or replaces an unfavourable outcome. Retrospectively selected trace examples are distinguished from cohort-level findings.

All 240 campaigns and all 720 scientific-account, prediction and retrospective stages remain included. There are 238 conforming chains and 3,597 of 3,600 planned source assays. One crystallization source has eleven rather than twelve final assays after solvent exhaustion. One purification source consumes twelve starts but has ten final assays and two discarded vessels. Both are marked nonconforming; neither is replaced. Infrastructure retries retain previous attempts and resource accounting. The supplementary tables report the complete matrix, targets, qualification domains and every response-specific arm mean. Original protocol variation and excluded historical pilots are documented separately from this pool.

## Retrospective analysis of the autonomous equilibrium process

We include all fifteen effective parameter-prior equilibrium sources from their retained source and trajectory indexes: five worlds, three arms, 180 batches, 945 operations and 45 canonical assessment stages. Nominal batch concentration is the sum of reagent additions divided by the sum of solvent additions; it is not inferred dissolved concentration. Original question scores and reference observations remain unchanged. No additional model, simulator or judge calls are used.

A single author review assigns descriptive categories to the public Q explanation: piecewise weak-acid extrapolation, qualitative departure from the plateau, plateau or weak-trend extension, and mixed weak-acid/plateau explanation. Exact excerpts and prediction values accompany the classifications; they are not an independent mechanism assessment. K1 evidence is distinguished from hypotheses first explicit in Q. K2 future-experiment choices are classified separately: eleven of fifteen propose trace-load experiments after seeing Q conditions, without receiving reference feedback. These proposals were not executed and do not establish spontaneous discovery or repair during the source campaign. The exported decision-reason fields are unavailable for all 945 operations, so no contemporaneous reasoning sequence is reconstructed from them.
