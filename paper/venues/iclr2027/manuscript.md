title: "ChemWorld: Task Success Does Not Ensure Scientific Generalization"
abstract: |
  Evaluating an autonomous scientist requires more than measuring the best experiment it finds. Its chosen observations must also support predictions of new interventions. We introduce ChemWorld, a programmable framework that separates private process laws, public experimental interfaces, and supplied information while preserving persistent state, finite resources, and exact environment replay. We study 240 autonomous research campaigns across six chemical system families with system-specific goals and Opaque, Aligned, and MisIndexed information conditions. Operational and predictive outcomes diverge: electrochemical optimization improves independently retested recommendations in 26 of 30 matched comparisons, but prediction in only 14. Doubling research budgets improves electrochemical and partitioning prediction without uniformly resolving crystallization errors. Information effects are also conditional. In aqueous equilibrium, Aligned improves prediction on nine questions but worsens it on the three most dilute conditions in all five worlds. Conversely, crystallization purity forecasts lose to the mean of their own observations in all thirty campaigns, while recovery forecasts beat it in twenty-six. These complementary patterns motivate evaluating whether agents know when an observed relationship should persist or change. Complete outcome records, response-specific metrics, and selected trajectory analyses support this diagnosis without equating successful operation, plausible explanation, or a single prediction score with scientific understanding.

tldr: "ChemWorld separates task achievement from scientific generalization and reveals response- and regime-dependent failures in autonomous chemical research."

# Introduction

An autonomous scientist must decide both what to do and what its observations justify. A productive recipe is valuable, but need not support accurate predictions after changing material, process history, or operating regime. Conversely, an immediately unproductive experiment may distinguish explanations that matter for later inference. Task achievement and scientific generalization are therefore connected capabilities whose relationship must be measured, rather than assumed.

Language-model agents can plan chemical experiments, use specialist tools, and interact with automated laboratories [@boiko2023autonomous; @bran2024augmenting; @szymanski2023alab]. Evaluating the resulting research is difficult because prior information influences which experiments are selected, and those experiments influence the subsequent account. Physical mechanisms are rarely fully known, while rerunning a campaign under matched starting conditions is expensive. A final optimization score cannot determine whether an agent acquired discriminating evidence or made warranted use of it.

We introduce **ChemWorld**, a composable experimental environment with separately controlled process laws, observations, and public information. Agents act through laboratory operations with persistent physical consequences and finite resources. The evaluator can reconstruct the exact evidence available before a scientific claim or prediction. Our protocol retains autonomous experiment selection and unrestricted mechanism expression, then measures operating recommendations, withheld-condition predictions, and uncertainty separately.

Across 240 campaigns in six system families, we find a selective separation between operational achievement and predictive knowledge. Additional resources and informative dossiers help in several settings, but neither produces a uniform improvement across responses. A finer analysis reveals complementary errors: equilibrium predictions can preserve an observed plateau outside its applicable regime, whereas crystallization predictions can depart unnecessarily from stable observed purity. These are behavioural findings from one agent configuration, not a universal deficit or an identified psychological mechanism.

Our contributions are (i) a programmable instrument with internal qualification and exact environment replay, (ii) an autonomous research protocol comparing matched information conditions without prescribing the form of the mechanism, and (iii) complete selected cohorts and response-specific analyses showing why task scores alone are insufficient for evaluating scientific generalization. The platform spans nine task families; the present agent evidence covers six. Negative results and source-budget shortfalls remain visible.

# ChemWorld and the research protocol

## Physical control with restricted observations

A world comprises a public experimental contract and private process parameters, $\mathcal W=(W_{\mathrm{pub}},\theta)$. Compatible process components declare states, parameter domains, dependencies, and interfaces; operation and observation models turn these declarations into an executable laboratory. Chemistry components include reaction, thermal, phase, separation, crystallization, distillation, flow, and electrochemical processes. Task families can share components and are not independent laws.

Agents access declared operations and measurements, not $\theta$, simulator code, or evaluator records. Sampling can consume material, separation changes its location, and thermal history persists. A validated transition commits to the state; a rejected action retains the committed state and records its declared attempt consequences. Replay reconstructs the environment and executed actions, including rejection and rollback. It does not imply deterministic model responses.

The evaluator can change a private law while preserving an interface, or intervene on public information while holding physics fixed. The present three-arm study uses the latter control. Platform qualification passes 64 task-world units, 1,786 boundary and categorical recipes, 192 invalid-action probes, and 52 generated compositions. The latter include 18 new topologies, eight new task-world identities on a registered topology, and 26 coverage cases. Module and interface tests cover 32 probes, seven valid paths, and seven invalid compositions. These are finite-domain internal checks, not wet-laboratory validation or agent discoveries.

## System-specific commissions and three information arms

Every study block has five worlds and three matched arms. **Opaque** exposes usable contracts and anonymous material identifiers without the tested instance-specific dossier. **Aligned** adds the nominal information associated with the intended entities, effective parameters, or process relationships. **MisIndexed** applies the block's fixed mismatch or reassignment. Aligned is partial information rather than complete simulator truth; Opaque does not remove general chemical knowledge. The treatment locus—entity (E), parameter (P), or structure (S)—is distinct from the three arms.

Electrochemistry (EC) and reaction/thermal processing (RX) separately assign discovery and public-score optimization. Phase partitioning (PA) and aqueous equilibrium (EQ) emphasize characterization. Crystallization (C) seeks seed-excluded recovery subject to purity and fines constraints. Purification seeks original-charge recovery subject to purity. The latter system abbreviation P is distinct from the parameter-prior locus. Tasks are not forced into one yield objective.

The matrix contains EC/E 60 campaigns, PA/E 30, RX/P and RX/S 60, EQ/E, EQ/P, and EQ/S fifteen each, C/E 30, and purification/E 15. EC, PA, and C compare 12 and 24 batches; other blocks use twelve. All campaigns use GPT-5.6 Sol with medium reasoning effort in independent sessions. A budget supplies the same number of optional additional measurements and a final assay per completed batch. Other operational, material, time, and computation limits are explicit. Agents choose repetitions, measurement allocation, and grouping; a batch is not necessarily a separate model response or feedback-conditioned decision. No fixed equation family or per-batch reflection rule is imposed.

## Separate assessments of achievement, prediction, and explanation

For EC, RX, C, and purification, a completed-batch recommendation is sealed before assessment and its procedure independently retested in the same world using a different observation seed. Each campaign then produces **K1**, a free-form mechanistic account; **Q**, point predictions and intervals for twelve fixed new-batch recipes without laboratory access or target feedback; and **K2**, retrospective questions about evidence, alternatives, and discriminating experiments. An additional equilibrium parameter supplement yields fifteen submissions. The original research context is retained throughout. Thus Q can use more than K1, while K2 cannot change Q or establish what the agent believed earlier.

For campaign $c$ and response $m$, error is

$$
\mathrm{MAE}_{cm}=\frac{1}{12}\sum_{q=1}^{12}|\hat y_{cqm}-y^{\mathrm{ref}}_{qm}|.
$$

Coverage and interval width are reported alongside MAE. PA uses nominal 90% intervals; others use 80%. EC and purification use fixed seeded reference observations, PA and C underlying pre-assay values, and RX/EQ five-repeat means for point error with individual repeats for coverage. Macro error averages responses within the specified block. Different scales and reference targets preclude a pooled cross-system accuracy ranking.

Goal pairs match world, arm, budget, and locus; budget pairs compare independent sessions matched on the other factors. The larger budget includes greater computation and operation allowances, so it is a resource-envelope intervention. There is one realization per cell and five reused world clusters per study. We report descriptive magnitudes and paired directions without counting correlated questions, responses, or arms as independent replicates.

All 720 canonical assessments and 165 retests completed. One C source has eleven assays after solvent exhaustion; one purification source has ten assays and two discards from twelve starts. The pool therefore has 238 conforming chains and 3,597/3,600 planned source assays. Both shortfalls are retained. Infrastructure attempts are accounted for separately, and source outcomes are not replaced for being unfavourable. These agent cohorts retain their development-evidence status, distinct from frozen platform qualification.

# Operational success does not ensure better prediction

EC optimization improves recommendation retests in **26/30** pairs, but score-prediction error in **14/30**. Thirteen pairs combine improved retests with worse prediction, distributed across all five worlds (Figure 2). Of the remaining pairs, thirteen improve both endpoints. Restricting to the seventeen pairs without infrastructure-driven source restarts retains fifteen retest improvements, eight prediction improvements, and seven discordances. The association therefore does not arise solely from the retained restart asymmetry.

![Matched optimization versus discovery outcomes. EC shows frequent improvement in recommendation performance without better score prediction. RX supplies a contrasting system in which the optimization commission itself often fails to improve retests. Each point represents a matched pair, with world reuse across arms and budgets; scales are specific to each system.](../../figures/integrated-results/goals.pdf){width=100%}

RX gives a different pattern: optimization improves retests in 9/30 pairs, score prediction in 8/30, and the original six-response macro error in 6/30. The optimization instruction is therefore not an oracle for finding better experiments. These contrasts concern complete research policies; they do not causally separate experimental acquisition from subsequent interpretation.

Constrained delivery adds another distinction. Purification recommendations are purity-eligible in 3/5 Aligned worlds and 0/5 in each other arm, although mean recovery is lowest for Aligned: 0.04455 versus 0.12373 for Opaque and 0.32105 for MisIndexed. All procedures are legal. A high recovered fraction can therefore coexist with failure to deliver the requested quality. One Opaque retest reaches purity 0.7992 against a 0.80 threshold, also illustrating why one noisy retest should not be equated with an exact reliability estimate.

# Larger budgets help, but do not resolve every response

The 24-batch condition reduces EC mean score MAE from 0.17378 to 0.11222 under discovery and from 0.17811 to 0.10818 under optimization. Eleven and twelve of the respective fifteen pairs improve. Aggregate error decreases on all six EC responses, with mean directions preserved under leave-one-world-out checks. Prediction is therefore sensitive to the available research envelope; failure at twelve batches cannot automatically be attributed to reasoning alone.

PA supplies a particularly strong positive result. Organic-fraction MAE falls from 0.08291 to 0.02379 (13/15 pairs improve), and process-choice accuracy rises from 25/30 to 30/30. Nominal 90% coverage increases from 78.3% to 98.9% while width narrows from 0.25122 to 0.18553. High coverage exceeds its nominal target and is not evidence of perfect calibration. In a selected aligned campaign, coverage of material pairings increases from ten of sixteen to all sixteen, followed by eight process experiments. Two other aligned world pairs worsen, so this successful trajectory is not a universal account of the budget effect.

C improves mean recovery MAE from 0.10433 to 0.07937 but retains 12/15 quality-eligible recommendations at each budget. Retested recovery falls from 0.42054 to 0.39939. Fines coverage remains below nominal in every campaign, rising only from 35.6% to 43.9% overall. Furthermore, the small aggregate size-error improvement reverses when budget pairs containing the nonconforming source are omitted. Full response tables and paired sensitivities accompany the supplementary budget figure. The result is selective resource benefit, not evidence that more experiments cannot help or that data insufficiency has been ruled out.

# Prior value depends on the regime and response

Aligned information can help. In RX/P, discovery macro MAE falls from 0.10186 under Opaque to 0.05701 under Aligned; optimization error falls from 0.13893 to 0.08384. Aligned beats Opaque in four of five worlds under either goal. EC also changes ordering with its commission: MisIndexed has the lowest mean score error under discovery at both budgets, while Aligned has the lowest under optimization. Final rankings do not reveal whether a dossier claim was accepted, rejected, or revised.

EQ/P shows a consistent aggregate disadvantage: Opaque macro MAE is 0.01214, versus 0.04314 for Aligned and 0.03928 for MisIndexed, beating both in every world. Aligned supplies a true effective-pKa interval, not the complete coupled law. A post hoc partition of questions by public concentration reveals a more informative reversal. Aligned **improves on the other nine questions but worsens on the three most dilute in all five worlds** (Figure 3). MAEs change from 0.01008 to 0.00465 on the other nine, and from 0.01833 to 0.15862 on the dilute three. The other nine include boundary questions and cannot all be called in-distribution tests.

![Conditional information effects. EQ/P: mean macro error and interval coverage for the three most dilute recipes and the other nine, with nominal 80% coverage marked. C/12: dossier-minus-Opaque changes in recovery and purity error for every world. Negative recovery and positive purity changes show a benefit and cost in the same pair; crosses include the nonconforming source. The query grouping and joint-response contrast are exploratory, with no independent-query significance test.](../../figures/venue-results/regimes.pdf){width=100%}

Aligned coverage is 91.3% on the other nine questions but 2.2% on the dilute three; Opaque obtains 90.7% and 93.3%. The source campaigns all remain above 0.018 M and observe a dissociation plateau near 0.06–0.09. The dilute questions instead reach approximately $10^{-4}$ to $10^{-5}$ M. One aligned campaign spans a ninety-fold concentration range while Opaque spans only fourfold, but neither leaves the source response regime. On a selected dilute query, true dissociation is 0.65463: Opaque predicts 0.629 [0.45, 0.80], Aligned 0.0804 [0.053, 0.108]. Input-range breadth thus need not measure coverage of distinct response regimes. This pattern supports a behavioural diagnosis; it does not identify a causal anchoring mechanism.

C gives a response-level reversal. At twelve batches, both Aligned and MisIndexed improve recovery error while worsening purity error relative to Opaque in all five worlds, including all four both-conforming pairs for each comparison. At 24 batches the joint direction holds in only two and three worlds, respectively. No single arm ranking describes both responses and budgets. Across EQ/E, EQ/S, PA, and RX/S, mixed directions and tail errors further caution against aggregating all priors into a common treatment effect. Different locus studies also change their specific scientific questions, so comparisons between P and S are not isolated effects of prior type.

# When should an observed relationship be preserved?

To connect prediction to acquired evidence, we compare every C campaign with two empirical references fitted to its public final observations: the response mean and nearest-neighbour prediction from public recipe features. They obtain no additional experiments or hidden truth during fitting. They are simple references, not strong system-identification baselines or replicas of the agent's full information and computational policy.

Agent recovery predictions beat the public mean in **26/30** campaigns. Purity predictions lose in **30/30**, and size predictions in **26/30** (Figure 4). Mean purity error is 0.05029 and 0.03679 at the two budgets, compared with 0.00475 and 0.00489 for the observation mean. Source and target purity are both approximately 0.985, while predicted means are 0.93544 and 0.94893. In total, 335/360 query predictions are too low. The low within-world target span, about 0.02442, explains why the constant reference is strong; its success does not establish mechanism discovery.

![C forecasts versus the mean of their own source observations. Above-diagonal points favour the public mean. A selected misindexed C campaign explicitly acknowledges in K1 that it tested only one solvent. Yet its purity predictions average 0.86833 against 0.98566 in the references, and none of twelve nominal 80% intervals covers the target. The example, selected for high purity error, shows a possible disconnect between stated limitations and quantified uncertainty; it does not estimate its prevalence. Likewise, selected EC traces link restricted potential coverage to poor predictions in the untested regime, but counterexamples show that sparse sampling alone does not determine failure.

EQ and C require opposite uses of empirical stability. An EQ source-mean diagnostic fails badly on dilute questions because the plateau should break; the C mean is effective because purity remains stable on its test distribution. The common evaluation question is whether an agent can identify **when to retain a relationship and when to revise it**. This synthesis is a testable interpretation of the observed patterns, not a demonstrated single cause. Predictions retain full context, so none of these comparisons establishes information loss through report compression.

# Related work, limitations, and implications

Chemistry agents and autonomous laboratories demonstrate planning and execution [@boiko2023autonomous; @bran2024augmenting; @szymanski2023alab]. Summit and Olympus support repeatable optimization studies [@felton2021summit; @hase2021olympus]; ChemGymRL supports interactive chemistry [@beeler2024chemgymrl]. DiscoveryWorld and the BoxingGym preprint study scientific experimentation in controlled environments [@jansen2024discoveryworld; @gandhi2025boxinggym]. ChemWorld's particular contribution is to combine composable processes, persistent material and observation histories, and matched information interventions with separate measurements of task achievement and prediction. The distinction between decision quality and prediction error also connects to decision-focused learning [@elmachtoub2022spo].

The evidence is limited to one model configuration and five worlds per study, with correlated readouts and no repeated independent model seeds within a cell. There is no wet-laboratory validation. The EQ grouping and trace examples are post hoc; the C baseline exploits a low-variation response. Early EC/PA calculator limits and retained RX/EQ prompt language differ from later blocks. These differences constrain cross-system interpretation. Complete mechanism annotation, strong classical sequential comparators, and causal evidence-matched interventions remain open work, rather than implied results.

The findings motivate evaluation along three linked axes: what the agent achieves, what it predicts under new interventions, and whether its uncertainty matches its evidence. Improvements on one axis should not substitute for measurements on the others. ChemWorld provides the controls and records needed to study their relationship while preserving autonomous scientific behaviour.
