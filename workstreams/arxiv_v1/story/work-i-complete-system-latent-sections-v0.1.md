## Results — Lifecycle completion does not specify terminal policy

### Complete systems separated closure from assay commitment

We next used the qualified apparatus to compare terminal behavior from two **distinct
complete agent systems**, treating each model, scaffold, decision transport, evidence
interface, retry behavior, and source identity as part of the system. The same ten
physical world-by-information cells were matched on world, material, observation,
scoring, workflow, and campaign-resource contracts. This is an apparatus-portability
and behavior-profile comparison, not a model-backend intervention.

Both systems closed all 60 of their assigned batch lifecycles. Across the resulting
**120 closed lifecycles, 84 ended in final assays and 36 ended in explicit discards**
(**Figure 3a–b**). The Codex-based system committed all 60 closed batches to final
assay. The DeepSeek-based primitive-control system committed 24 to final assay and 36
to discard. Thus equal closure did not imply equal assay commitment: closure records
that a lifecycle reached an explicit terminal decision, while assay and discard record
different terminal policies.

The separation was not explained by the mere presence or absence of non-final
measurement. The two complete systems made 164 and 163 non-final instrument uses,
respectively, while attempting 815 and 889 primitive operations. These totals are
repeated-event accounting within ten system cells, not independent observations.
Similar instrument-use totals also do not establish equivalent evidence quality,
interpretation, or evidence-conditioned action.

Within the DeepSeek system, the five anonymous-nominal-properties cells contained 16
assays and 14 discards, whereas the five opaque-code cells contained 8 assays and 22
discards. The nominal arm also contained 67 more attempted operations and 17 more
non-final instrument uses than the opaque arm. These are paired five-world descriptive
profiles within one complete system. They do not identify a population information
effect, and they cannot be used to attribute the cross-system difference to a model
backend.

### The frozen discarded-state audit remained unresolved

The 36 discards motivated a preregistered finite-population question: what terminal
score would the exact pre-discard state receive if only its original `discard_batch`
were replaced by the evaluator's final assay? Before formal execution, the contract
fixed all 36 identities, the 60-lifecycle selection table, score support, thresholds,
estimands, denominators, same-identity replay, missingness rules, and entry gate.
Reconstructability and preflight both covered 36/36 registered checkpoints without
reading a latent score or calling an agent/provider.

The frozen formal execution did not pass its entry gate (**Figure 3c–d**). It retained
all 36 registered receipts, of which 6 resolved and 30 remained unresolved. Eleven
unresolved receipts reported a prefix-identity mismatch, 18 reported a runtime-versus-
authoritative resource-state mismatch, and one failed the registered final-assay
precondition boundary. The original on-disk trajectories and resource ledgers were
unchanged, and provider calls remained zero, but the original-resource-ledger execution
gate failed. No formal shadow result was repaired, selected, or replaced.

Under the frozen missingness rule, terminal quality is therefore **unresolved**. All
latent-dependent primary point estimates, the 60-lifecycle point classification table,
and any arm point contrast are withheld. The six resolved receipts remain only inside
the registered observed-only diagnostics and sharp finite-population bounds; they are
not a complete-case analysis.

The registered score-support bounds remain reportable. Across the fixed 36-discard
population, the mean latent-terminal-score bound was `[0.0000859, 0.833419]`; the mean
discard-minus-observed-best bound was `[-0.276951, 0.556382]`; and the mean positive-
discard-regret bound was `[0, 0.599173]`. Decision-time regret retained 34 eligible
discards, with two pre-assay discards explicitly null, and had mean bound
`[0, 0.595351]`. Campaign-oracle regret remained defined over the nine cells with a
discard opportunity and had mean bound `[0, 0.707673]`; the no-opportunity cell stayed
null rather than being assigned zero. These are sharp bounds under the registered score
support and fixed population, not confidence intervals.

At the primary threshold `q_c = 0.90 B_c`, the false-discard fraction was bounded by
`[0/36, 30/36]`. Assay-commitment precision was determined by the 24 observed assays and
therefore had the degenerate bound `[13/24, 13/24]`, while recall was bounded by
`[13/43, 13/13]`. The same all-zero/all-one censoring construction was reported at
relative thresholds 0.80 and 1.00 and at the fixed absolute threshold 0.58. The exact
observed-assay precision is an accounting surface, not permission to promote the failed
latent-terminal result to the main claim.

The complete-system headline does not depend on this failed gate. The observed result
remains that complete lifecycle closure and terminal commitment are distinct measured
coordinates. The audit cannot establish whether the discarded states were generally
promising or poor, whether discard saved laboratory resources, whether the shadow assay
was an agent choice, or whether either complete system was more rational.

### Primitive-control records expose complete experimental lifecycles

The terminal census is interpretable because the apparatus records the full path to
closure rather than only the best endpoint. **Figure 5a–d** should open one immutable
primitive-control lifecycle into its ordered actions, public observations, transaction
outcomes, resource events, and explicit terminal decision. A committed assay or discard
closes a started vessel; a rejected or rolled-back action may remain an attempted
operation without installing the candidate state change. Evidence acquisition,
continued process investment, resource use, termination, assay, and discard therefore
remain separable process coordinates.

Figure 5 must not repeat Figure 3's 84/36 headline as its main job. Its role is to show
why that terminal partition and the failed counterfactual audit are auditable from
identity-bound trajectories and ledgers. The records are executable-world histories,
not physical batch records, and their operations are repeated within campaigns rather
than independent samples.

## Methods — Complete-system terminal policies and latent-state censoring

### Complete-system comparison and matching

The comparison unit was one complete agent system operating in one matched
world-by-information cell. Ten cells crossed five world seeds with two material-
information arms. For each matched cell, the systems shared world identity and family,
mechanism, material family and instance, scoring contract, workflow mode, observation
mode, namespace and seed, and campaign-resource-card identity. Model identifier,
decision transport, provider retry behavior, source code, and run configuration were
intentionally not matched. We therefore report system-specific profiles and paired
within-system information descriptions but no causal backend effect.

A closed lifecycle was a started vessel followed by one committed terminal action:
`final_assay` or `discard_batch`. Assay and discard counts partitioned the closed
lifecycle count. An admitted environment action counted as one operation attempt even
when validation, rollback, or resource rejection prevented a state transition. A
non-final instrument use required a committed public measurement before the terminal
decision. Counts were first constructed within campaign cells and then summarized at
the complete-system or matched-world level; operations and instrument uses were not
treated as inferential replicates.

The Codex system used a native MCP experiment session per vessel, whereas the DeepSeek
system returned one schema-validated JSON decision per primitive operation. These
interfaces supplied different action authority and interaction structure. The result
must consequently use “distinct complete agent systems,” not “independently configured
models” or an equivalent backend-only label.

### Frozen latent-terminal population and counterfactual

The latent audit population was the DeepSeek system's complete set of 60 original
lifecycle terminals: 24 observed final assays and 36 committed discards across ten
cells. One latent unit was one registered discard. For each unit, the intended replay
reconstructed the exact environment immediately before the original discard, retaining
all prior actions, public observations, keyed noise, hidden state, and historical
resource prefix. The only counterfactual replacement was an evaluator-only final assay.
It could bypass the agent-facing “final assay requires termination” readiness check but
could not add process operations, repair state, call the agent/provider, charge the
original ledger, or count as an original experiment or agent assay decision.

The contract fixed four classification thresholds before outcome access: relative
thresholds `0.80 B_c`, `0.90 B_c`, and `1.00 B_c`, plus absolute score 0.58. Equality
counted as near-best. The primary classification threshold was `0.90 B_c`, where `B_c`
was the best observed assay score in campaign cell `c`. The classification population
remained all 60 original lifecycles. Continuous latent score, discard-to-best delta,
positive discard regret, false-discard fraction, assay precision/recall, campaign-
oracle regret, and decision-time regret used their exact frozen lifecycle or cell
denominators.

### Qualification, execution gate, and immutable failure handling

The outcome-blind reconstructability audit independently replayed every source
trajectory and reproduced all 36 pre-discard identity hashes. Synthetic qualification
then tested terminal replacement, exact same-identity noise/evaluation replay,
fail-closed identity probes, and all registered analysis/missingness surfaces on worlds
disjoint from the formal worlds. The formal protocol and 36/36 preflight were committed
and pushed before the first formal score was read.

Main-text eligibility required 36 valid scores, 36 exact same-identity replays, zero
agent/provider calls, and no mutation of the original trajectories or resource ledgers.
The formal run produced a complete 36-receipt report but only 6 valid scores and 6
passing same-identity receipts. Because the resource-ledger gate failed, the result was
retained as `incomplete_full_report_required`. The run was not altered or repeated to
obtain a favorable population.

### Missingness, bounds, and display rules

Every unresolved unit remained in its original denominator. For a missing latent score
`S_i`, the registered support was `[0,1]`. Delta and regret bounds additionally used the
cell's observed best score; decision-time regret used the strictly prior assayed
incumbent and left pre-assay discards null; campaign-oracle bounds used the same nine
discard-opportunity cells. Order-statistic and empirical-CDF bounds, finite-population
micro summaries, cell-macro summaries, five paired-world arm contrasts, four threshold
rows, and missingness by arm/world/cell were all retained in the machine report.

No super-population p-value or confidence interval was primary. When any score was
unresolved, point estimates depending on latent outcomes were withheld. Observed-only
summaries were diagnostic and could not replace the finite-population estimand. At each
threshold, unresolved discards were assigned to the all-zero and all-one score endpoints
to derive the registered classification bounds while preserving the 60-lifecycle and
36-discard denominators.

## Locked interpretation boundaries

- Say **120 closed lifecycles: 84 final assays and 36 explicit discards** on first use.
- Say **distinct complete agent systems**; do not collapse systems to model backends.
- Do not call the six resolved shadow scores a complete-case result or a population
  terminal-quality estimate.
- Do not say discard was good, bad, rational, irrational, efficient, or cost-saving.
- Do not say a shadow assay was selected, observed, or learned from by the agent.
- Do not infer a general information effect or a causal cross-system/backend effect.
- Do not count shadow evaluations, deterministic replays, primitive operations, or
  resource events as original agent experiments or independent samples.
- Keep all claims inside virtual/executable chemical worlds; no physical-laboratory
  executability, calibration, safety, or transfer claim is established here.
