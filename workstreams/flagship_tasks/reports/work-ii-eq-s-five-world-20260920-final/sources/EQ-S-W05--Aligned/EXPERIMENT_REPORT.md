MECHANISTIC REPORT

1. Experimental basis

The completed campaign comprised 12 aqueous batches at approximately 297.2 K and atmospheric pressure. All batches used water, no catalyst, no heating, and no deliberate waiting period. The design crossed four reagent amounts (0.005, 0.015, 0.025, and 0.035 mol) with three nominal water volumes (0.040, 0.060, and 0.080 L). Thus nominal analytical loading C_T = n/V ranged from 0.0625 to 0.875 mol L^-1. Every batch received one pH-meter measurement before termination and one final assay after termination. The final assays are the principal evidence because their declared standard deviations were smaller: 0.002 for normalized pH, 0.006 for acid-dissociation fraction, and 0.006 for precipitation signal, versus 0.004, 0.015, and 0.020 for the pH meter.

2. Direct observations

At every fixed volume, increasing reagent loading lowered normalized pH and generally increased the precipitation proxy. At every fixed reagent amount, dilution raised normalized pH and lowered precipitation. For example, the 0.005 mol series progressed from batch 1 at 0.040 L (pH_norm 0.201654; precipitation 0.014293), through batch 5 at 0.060 L (0.224252; 0.008946), to batch 9 at 0.080 L (0.238010; 0.003142). The same pattern was especially clear for 0.035 mol: batches 4, 8, and 12 changed from 0.095445/0.055837 to 0.117065/0.044486 and then 0.130803/0.028369 as volume increased.

Nominal concentration organizes much of the response. Batches 3 and 8 used different amounts and volumes but had nearby concentrations, 0.625 and 0.583 M; their normalized pH values were 0.113235 and 0.117065, and their precipitation signals were 0.045060 and 0.044486. This near agreement is evidence that loading and dilution are substantially coupled through concentration rather than exerting wholly independent effects. It does not prove exact concentration-only behavior.

A descriptive least-squares relation over the tested points is

pH_norm approximately 0.08642 - 0.05505 ln(C_T / 1 M).

Equivalently, because pH = 14 pH_norm,

pH approximately 1.2099 - 0.7708 ln(C_T / 1 M)
     approximately 1.2099 - 1.7747 log10(C_T / 1 M).

This is an empirical interpolation, not an asserted acid-dissociation law. A similarly descriptive linear summary of the precipitation channel is

P approximately 0.00300 + 0.06448 C_T,

with C_T in mol L^-1. The observed proxy remained small, from 0.003142 to 0.055837, so this fit does not establish a precipitation threshold, a solid yield, or linearity beyond the studied interval.

The reported free acid-dissociation fraction was far less resolved. Final values ranged from 0 to 0.011183, had a mean near 0.00527, and were comparable to the final-assay standard deviation of 0.006. Two final estimates were clipped at zero. There was no defensible monotonic sequence: batch 3 gave zero at 0.625 M, whereas batch 4 gave 0.008848 at 0.875 M. Intermediate measurements reinforce the caution rather than resolving it. For example, the pH meter reported precipitation 0.068814 in batch 3 and 0.090627 in batch 8, while their final assays reported 0.045060 and 0.044486. The declared pH-meter precipitation uncertainty was 0.020. Thus the pH and precipitation trends are clear at campaign scale, but fine structure in the dissociation fraction and individual precipitation readings should not be mechanistically overinterpreted.

3. Plausible common equilibrium network

A compact network consistent with the supplied structural prior is:

HA(aq) <=> H+(aq) + A-(aq)
M+(aq) + A-(aq) <=> MA(s).

Here HA is the dissolved weak acid form, A- is its free conjugate base, M+ is an unobserved precipitating counter-species, and MA(s) is a solid sink. The dose supplies total acid equivalents, but the experiment did not directly quantify any of these species.

For an idealized formulation,

K_a = a_H a_A / a_HA,
pH_norm = -log10(a_H) / 14,
Q_sp = a_M a_A.

If solid is present, Q_sp = K_sp; without solid, Q_sp <= K_sp. A complementarity representation is

S >= 0,
K_sp - Q_sp >= 0,
S(K_sp - Q_sp) = 0,

where S is the amount of solid.

The acid-equivalent balance is

n_added / V = [HA] + [A-] + S/V,

assuming one A unit per formula unit of solid. A corresponding metal balance, if the world contains a fixed initial metal inventory, is

C_M,T = [M+] + S/V.

Charge balance requires a more general expression such as

[H+] + [M+] + other cations = [A-] + [OH-] + other anions,

with K_w = a_H a_OH. The background ions are not identified by the public measurements and therefore cannot be omitted from a quantitative fit merely for convenience.

A natural definition for the measured free dissociation fraction is

alpha_free = [A-] / ([HA] + [A-]),

although the exact hidden mapping from species to the public channel was explicitly unavailable. The precipitation observation is only a normalized proxy and should be represented as

P = clip(g(S/V, crystal state, or another solid-related quantity) + measurement error, 0, 1),

not equated directly to moles or mass of MA(s).

This coupled network can qualitatively explain all three public channels. Greater C_T increases the acid inventory and lowers pH. Increasing free A- raises the ion product with M+, promoting the solid branch. Removal of A- into solid can draw additional HA through dissociation, coupling precipitation back to proton activity. Consequently, pH can change strongly while the instantaneous free A- fraction remains small. Dilution reverses the ion-product drive, reduces the precipitation proxy, and raises pH.

The observed empirical pH slope is much steeper than the familiar ideal, unbuffered weak-acid approximation pH = 0.5(pK_a - log10 C_T). That discrepancy does not by itself reject the network. Precipitation coupling, activity coefficients, an unmeasured metal/background-ion balance, and the normalized synthetic response can all invalidate the simple isolated-acid approximation. It does mean that a single textbook weak-acid equation without these couplings is not an adequate quantitative account of the observations.

4. Can one network explain all three channels?

Yes, one acid-dissociation plus precipitation network is a defensible unified explanation at the qualitative and semi-quantitative level. The pH and precipitation responses co-vary with concentration in the direction predicted by this network, and the very small free dissociation fraction is compatible with most acid equivalents remaining as HA or being removed from the free pool by precipitation.

However, the experiment does not demonstrate that a single fitted parameter set quantitatively predicts all three channels. The dissociation-fraction observations are near their measurement floor and do not provide a robust concentration law. Moreover, the precipitation proxy lacks an absolute calibration to solid amount. Therefore the strongest justified claim is that the three channels are mutually compatible with one coupled equilibrium network, not that the network has been uniquely identified or fully parameterized.

The environment's equilibrium_confidence channel is not my confidence in this mechanism. It is an environment diagnostic. Its final values, approximately 0.639 to 0.717, and the generally small reported equilibrium residuals do not replace experimental uncertainty or establish chemical truth.

5. Topology claim versus parameter refitting

The topology claim concerns which species and edges exist: HA dissociates directly into H+ and free A-, and free M+ plus A- forms a solid, with no distinct aqueous MA intermediate. Parameters within that topology include K_a, K_sp, total metal inventory, activity coefficients, background charge, and the offset, gain, and nonlinear shape of the precipitation proxy.

Refitting those constants can alter the location and sharpness of the responses without changing the graph. The present factorial data constrain an effective concentration response, but they do not separately identify K_a, K_sp, metal loading, activity corrections, and sensor calibration. A successful refit inside the proposed graph would therefore show adequacy, not prove the topology.

In particular, the claimed absence of MA(aq) is not observable here. An alternative topology

M+ + A- <=> MA(aq)
MA(aq) <=> MA(s)

can reduce to nearly the same effective free-ion and solid relationships if the aqueous-complex step is fast or weakly populated. Conversely, a direct solid step with concentration-dependent activities can mimic an apparent intermediate. Distinguishing these alternatives would require an independent dissolved-complex measurement or perturbation that changes complexation and precipitation differently.

6. Effect of the supplied dossier

No instance-specific prior and no task-specific nominal property dossier were supplied. The material record explicitly stated that experimental evidence was authoritative. A structural prior from an independent bounded mechanism study was supplied: HA(aq) <=> H+(aq) + A-(aq), followed by M+(aq) + A-(aq) <=> MA(s), with no distinct aqueous MA species.

The factorial observations support the broad acid-plus-precipitation organization of that prior: increasing concentration lowered pH and increased the precipitation proxy, while dilution reversed both effects. Batches 1-4 first established the loading trend at 0.040 L. Batches 5-8 showed that the pattern persisted at 0.060 L. Batches 9-12 then confirmed the dilution response and supplied low-concentration endpoints. The close pair of batches 3 and 8 particularly supported concentration as a common coupling variable.

The data did not verify the dossier's stronger topological assertion that precipitation occurs directly from free ions with no aqueous MA intermediate. That assertion remains plausible but untested. The near-floor dissociation-fraction results also forced a revision in evidential strength: I retain the network as a parsimonious effective model, but I do not claim a quantitatively established free-A- law or a uniquely proven species graph.

7. Identifiable and unidentifiable quantities

Identifiable within this campaign:
- The sign and approximate magnitude of the normalized-pH response to loading and dilution in water.
- The sign and approximate magnitude of the precipitation-proxy response over 0.0625-0.875 M.
- The fact that nominal concentration explains a substantial part of the joint response.
- The fact that the free dissociation fraction is small on the public scale and poorly resolved relative to assay noise.

Not identifiable:
- Absolute K_a or pK_a.
- Absolute K_sp or the onset concentration for a true solid phase.
- The metal inventory and identities of HA, A-, M+, or MA.
- Activity coefficients and background electrolyte composition.
- The conversion between precipitation signal and solid amount, particle number, or crystal state.
- Whether an aqueous MA intermediate exists.
- Kinetics, equilibration time, hysteresis, nucleation barriers, or reversibility, because no time-course, wait, seeding, or redissolution experiment was performed.
- Temperature, solvent, or catalyst dependence, because these factors were not varied.
- Whether loading and volume have small independent effects beyond C_T; the design suggests approximate concentration collapse but does not fully isolate all such effects.

8. Competing explanations

First, an uncoupled model could use one empirical acid response for pH and an independent concentration-sensitive precipitation sensor. Because both outputs depend on C_T, their covariance alone does not prove causal coupling.

Second, precipitation could proceed through MA(aq), ion pairs, or oligomers. With only pH, a noisy fraction channel, and a normalized solid proxy, these graphs may be observationally equivalent to direct free-ion precipitation.

Third, changing ionic strength and activity coefficients across a fourteen-fold concentration interval could generate the steep pH response without requiring a large change in intrinsic K_a.

Fourth, an unmeasured buffer, counterion, or finite metal reservoir could control both proton activity and the precipitation threshold. Such hidden totals can trade off strongly against fitted K_a and K_sp.

Fifth, the precipitation signal may be a nonlinear or morphology-sensitive proxy rather than a direct measure of equilibrium solid quantity. The elevated but low-valued signals do not establish stoichiometric precipitation.

These alternatives are reasonable and are not rejected by the completed campaign. The direct two-step network is preferred only as the simplest account aligned with the supplied structural prior, not as a uniquely demonstrated mechanism.

9. Scope: observation, interpolation, extrapolation, and conjecture

Observation: The tabled final-assay values and the fixed-loading dilution comparisons are direct public observations. Within the tested water matrix, higher n/V produced lower normalized pH and generally higher precipitation signal. The dissociation fraction stayed near zero and was noisy.

Interpolation: The empirical pH-log(concentration) and precipitation-linear(concentration) relations summarize behavior inside 0.0625-0.875 M, 0.005-0.035 mol, and 0.040-0.080 L at approximately 297.2 K. They may be used cautiously between tested points in the same matrix. They are descriptive rather than mechanistic parameter estimates.

Extrapolation: Predictions below 0.0625 M, above 0.875 M, at other temperatures, in other solvents, with catalysts, after long equilibration, or under different background-ion conditions are unsupported. The linear precipitation summary must not be extrapolated to infer unlimited growth or a threshold.

Conjecture: The identities of the acid and precipitating species, direct formation of MA(s), the absence of MA(aq), and the detailed feedback by which precipitation steepens the pH response are mechanistic conjectures consistent with the data. The most defensible conclusion is a bounded effective network: acid dissociation and a concentration-dependent precipitation sink can jointly account for the observed channels, while its constants and finer topology remain underdetermined.

## K2 — sealed seven-part retrospective

1. Supplied structural claims

No instance-specific prior and no task-specific nominal-property dossier were supplied. The supplied information was a structural prior from an independent bounded study: HA(aq) <=> H+(aq) + A-(aq), followed by M+(aq) + A-(aq) <=> MA(s), with no distinct aqueous MA intermediate.

The specific direct-free-ion topology was not established. In particular, the claimed absence of MA(aq) was untested because no dissolved-complex-selective measurement was available. The free dissociation fraction also did not independently validate a detailed species balance: final estimates were only 0-0.011183, comparable to their stated 0.006 standard deviation, and batches 3 and 6 were clipped at zero.

The structural dossier itself had not asserted the rejected ideal parameterization.

2. Experiments that formed or changed the account

The 4-by-3 loading-volume factorial was chosen before its outcomes were known. The supplied topology motivated measuring pH, free dissociation fraction, and precipitation together rather than optimizing one output. Batches 1-4 established the loading response at 0.040 L: pH_norm fell from 0.201654 to 0.095445 while precipitation rose from 0.014293 to 0.055837. This was the first strong support for a coupled concentration response.

Batches 5-8 tested whether that response persisted at 0.060 L. Batch 8 also exposed measurement limitations: its pH meter reported precipitation 0.090627, whereas its final assay reported 0.044486. That difference, considered with the pH-meter uncertainty of 0.020, changed the account from treating individual readings literally to emphasizing campaign-scale trends and final assays.

Batches 9-12 supplied the most dilute observed endpoints. Batch 9 at 0.0625 M gave pH_norm 0.238010 and precipitation 0.003142, strengthening the inference that dilution suppresses the precipitation proxy. The close response of batches 3 and 8 despite different amounts and volumes strengthened—but did not prove—the concentration-dominant interpretation.

The noisy dissociation-fraction results caused the largest revision in evidential strength. Rather than claim that all three channels had quantitatively identified one parameter set, K1 judged only that they were mutually compatible with one network. The direct topology remained a parsimonious effective model, not a uniquely identified mechanism.

The design choice itself relied partly on the dossier: all batches were aqueous and targeted the proposed acid/precipitation slice. The fixed factorial and use of final assays relied on the characterization objective rather than later outcomes. The concentration-only transfer used in parts of K1 and Q relied on accumulated comparisons such as batches 3 and 8. Instantaneous equilibrium, scale invariance at fixed concentration, and absence of important path dependence were untested assumptions; no waiting, redissolution, or true replicate experiment was performed.

3. Strongest competing explanations

The strongest competing reaction network is

HA <=> H+ + A-
M+ + A- <=> MA(aq)
MA(aq) <=> MA(s).

A rapidly equilibrating or weakly populated MA(aq) pool could yield essentially the same observed pH, free-A-, and precipitation-proxy responses as direct M+ + A- <=> MA(s). The campaign cannot distinguish these graphs because it had no selective measurement of dissolved MA.

The strongest parameter-only explanation retains the supplied topology but allows concentration-dependent activity coefficients, a finite hidden M inventory, background charge or buffering, and a nonlinear mapping from solid amount to precipitation signal. Those parameters can steepen pH and reshape precipitation without adding a species. K1 therefore distinguished topology from constant refitting: fitting Ka, Ksp, activities, metal inventory, and proxy gain within one graph can establish adequacy but cannot validate the graph itself.

An even less mechanistic alternative is that pH and precipitation are separate empirical functions of concentration. Their covariance does not by itself prove causal coupling. The evidence can distinguish these accounts from a concentration-independent response and from a simple ideal weak-acid-only equation, but it cannot distinguish direct precipitation from an aqueous-complex pathway, identify absolute Ka or Ksp, or convert the proxy into solid mass.

4. One additional complete experiment

I would run a path-dependence and redilution experiment. I would prepare the batch-4 corner—0.035 mol reagent in 0.040 L water—measure it with the pH meter, then add another 0.040 L water to reach 0.4375 M, wait at the existing ambient temperature with controlled stirring, measure again with the pH meter, terminate, and obtain the final assay. The final concentration would match batch 12, while the material would have first traversed the high-concentration state represented by batch 4.

If the post-dilution final assay matched batch 12 near pH_norm 0.130803 and precipitation 0.028369, that would support a reversible, state-function-like equilibrium account and make concentration-only transfer more credible. If it retained batch-4-like low pH or elevated precipitation near 0.055837, I would revise the account to include hysteresis, slow redissolution, nucleation memory, or an effectively irreversible solid sink. An intermediate result would motivate a kinetic extension rather than immediate rejection of the equilibrium topology. Even a matching result would not distinguish direct precipitation from an MA(aq) intermediate; it would primarily test equilibrium reversibility and path independence. This experiment is proposed only and was not executed.

5. Objective-driven trade-offs

The objective was characterization, not score improvement. I therefore spent the 12 completed batches on a balanced four-loading by three-volume grid and used one pH-meter measurement plus one final assay per batch. That provided broad joint coverage and exact denominators, but it sacrificed true replication, time-course information, and focused localization of a precipitation transition.

Local identification was strongest near observed matching or neighboring concentrations, such as batches 3 and 8, but the grid was too sparse to identify a sharp threshold, Ka, Ksp, or a scale effect independently. Using only water preserved the aqueous scope but gave up solvent and material generalization. Omitting catalysts, heat, and score-driven adaptation avoided confounding the equilibrium question.

Visible scalar scores were not used to choose conditions or alter the matrix. The sealed anchor, batch 4, was selected because it was the clearest high-loading/low-volume joint-response corner, not because it was proven operationally best. Indeed, K1 explicitly treated the native score as a diagnostic rather than the research objective.

6. Underused evidence and weakest blind predictions

The paired pH-meter and final-assay observations were underused quantitatively. I used their disagreements to justify caution, but I did not fit a hierarchical measurement-error model that separated process variation from instrument variation. The crossed design was also underused: K1 relied mainly on nominal concentration and qualitative contrasts rather than formally estimating independent amount, volume, and interaction terms. Equilibrium residuals were noted only briefly. That restraint was appropriate because they are environment diagnostics, but their association with measurement discrepancies could have been described more systematically. The raw multichannel characterizations were not used to claim species identities, so the account remained dependent on processed public metrics.

The least reliable blind predictions are Q01-Q03 because their concentrations, 0.0004167-0.025 M, lie below the K1 range. Q01 is especially weak: its predicted dissociation fraction of 0.104 with interval 0.045-0.190 depended on an effective low-concentration Ka even though K1 explicitly judged absolute Ka unidentifiable. The wide interval acknowledged but did not remove that tension.

Q06 is also weak because 1.25 M exceeds the observed 0.875 M maximum. Its pH_norm estimate of 0.074 and precipitation estimate of 0.084 extend empirical high-concentration trends into a region where activities and proxy nonlinearity were unmeasured.

The scale controls Q07 and Q11 are weak for a different reason. Their volumes, 0.006 and 0.012 L, are far below the studied 0.040-0.080 L range. Assigning Q07 the same center as Q05 at 0.5 M, and assigning Q11 the same center as Q03 and Q12 at 0.025 M, depended on unproven scale invariance. Q04 and Q05 also used 0.024 L, below the observed volume range, so their intervals may be too narrow if absolute scale matters.

Q10 at 0.040 M is a milder low-concentration extrapolation. Q04 and Q09 have concentrations exactly represented by batches 1 and 9, respectively, and are more defensible, although their different absolute scales still prevent them from being literal replicates. Overall, some Q intervals—especially for Q01, Q03/Q11/Q12, Q06, and Q07—may underrepresent topology and scale-transfer uncertainty despite being wider than instrumental error.

7. Limits of the anchor, robustness, and generalization

Batch 4 is a single, unreplicated extreme condition. It establishes that one completed high-loading, low-volume batch produced pH_norm 0.095445, fraction 0.008848, and precipitation 0.055837. It does not establish repeatability, a precipitation threshold, a maximum response, or an optimal condition. It was an evidentiary anchor, not proof of optimality.

Repeatability is unknown because no recipe was independently repeated. The intermediate/final differences, including batch 8's precipitation readings of 0.090627 and 0.044486, show that local measurement variation is consequential. Local robustness is supported only directionally by neighboring cells: batches 3, 4, and 8 all combined relatively low pH with elevated precipitation, but their dissociation fractions did not form a stable monotonic pattern.

Concentration generalization is most defensible inside 0.0625-0.875 M and near tested points. Volume generalization is limited to 0.040-0.080 L, and even there concentration-only behavior was suggested rather than proven. Nothing in K1 establishes transfer to nonaqueous solvents, catalysts, different temperatures, different background ions, long equilibration times, seeded solids, or other material identities. The materials were anonymous mechanism roles, so the network must not be promoted into a claim about a named real chemical system.

Finally, this was one frozen synthetic world. The effective acid-dissociation/precipitation account may summarize that world locally, but its constants, hidden inventories, proxy calibration, and possibly its topology can differ in another world. The correct sealed conclusion remains the one stated in K1: one coupled equilibrium network is a defensible explanation of the three channels, while its parameterization and finer topology are underdetermined.
